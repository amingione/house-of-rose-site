## 1. The 3-sentence answer

## 2. How it works

**Plain English.** A microneedling pen (or stamp) moves many very fine needles rapidly in and out of the skin, making thousands of tiny controlled punctures. Your body reads those micro-injuries as something to repair, and repairs skin by building **new collagen** — the structural protein that firms and smooths. Over the weeks after a session, that new collagen fills in and softens the appearance of scars, crepey texture, and fine lines. Because the pen's depth is adjustable, the provider can go shallow on delicate areas and deeper where the concern (an acne scar, a stretch mark) sits lower in the skin.

**One level deeper.** Microneedling is also called **percutaneous collagen induction therapy**. Each needle pass creates a controlled micro-wound that triggers the body's natural three-phase wound-healing cascade — inflammation, proliferation, and remodeling. The micro-injuries cause minimal superficial pinpoint bleeding and release a wave of growth factors (platelet-derived growth factor, transforming growth factor alpha/beta, fibroblast growth factor, connective-tissue growth factor). Fibroblasts migrate in, lay down a fibronectin matrix around day 5, and drive **neocollagenesis and neovascularization**. New collagen and elastin are deposited over the following months; histology studies have shown large increases in collagen and elastin after a course of sessions spaced roughly a month apart, with remodeling continuing for about 3–6 months after treatment. Depth matters because the target sits at different levels: superficial needling refreshes texture and helps product penetrate, while deeper needling reaches the dermal scar tissue and stretch-mark bands.

**The adjustable-depth idea is the whole point of "corrective."** Unlike a fixed-setting protocol, the provider selects depth by region and by indication — thin skin and delicate zones get a shallow setting; thick body skin, scars, and stretch marks get a deeper, slower pass (see §8/§10 and the clinical reference for House-standard ranges).

## 3. Who it's for / not for

**Good candidates.** Adults who want to improve the **appearance** of: deeper or rolling atrophic acne scars, defined/textural facial scarring, mature stretch marks and scar texture on the body, crepey texture, fine lines, and overall skin quality where a fixed facial protocol isn't the right tool. Good for people comfortable with a short recovery and a planned series rather than a single-visit fix.

**Not for / postpone / needs provider review** (this is a screening guide, not a candidacy decision — the licensed provider decides):
- **Active acne** (especially inflammatory lesions) or any active breakout in the treatment area.
- **Active infection** at the site — bacterial, viral, or fungal — including an **active cold-sore/herpes** outbreak, or warts.
- **History of keloid or hypertrophic scarring** / keloidal tendency.
- **Recent isotretinoin** (Accutane) use — historically screened for within the prior ~6 months; the provider confirms current spacing.
- **Immunosuppression**, chemotherapy/radiotherapy, systemic steroids, or known malignancy.
- **Bleeding/clotting disorders or anticoagulant (blood-thinner) use** — especially relevant for the PRF blood-draw upgrade.
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding.**
- **Moderate-to-severe chronic skin disease** (active eczema, psoriasis) in the area.
- **Sunburned, inflamed, irritated, or broken skin** — never needle it.
- **Caution near recent botulinum-toxin injection sites.**

**Honest limits.** This is corrective, not curative. It does not treat active acne, and it does not "remove," "erase," or "cure" scars, stretch marks, or wrinkles — it softens their **appearance** over time. Pore size is not permanently changed. Response varies with the age, color, depth, and location of the concern and with your skin biology. Deep ice-pick scars, tethered scars, and hypertrophic/keloid-prone scars may need a clinician's assessment or a different approach. Not every client is a candidate, and individual outcomes vary.

## 4. The visit

**Protocol, step-by-step (representative — provider tailors it):**
1. **Consultation & skin analysis.** Amber reviews the concern, skin history, medications, and contraindications; confirms candidacy; selects the tool, depth, and pattern; and confirms whether to add topical PRF.
2. **Prep.** Cleanse and, where appropriate, dermaplane prep to remove peach fuzz. A single-use sterile cartridge is opened for each client and each session.
3. **(If PRF upgrade)** A small blood sample is drawn on-site and spun in a centrifuge to prepare the platelet-rich fibrin, which is applied **topically** during the needling.
4. **Needling.** The provider works zone by zone at the depth dialed to each area — shallow on delicate zones, deeper and slower on scars/body. Expect pinpoint bleeding in deeper areas.
5. **Soothe & finish.** Calming serum/mask; optional **LightStim LED** to calm the skin, or a **Glo2Facial finish** for a signature glow. Written aftercare provided.

**Duration.** Corrective — Face: 60–75 min. Body: 60–90 min. Add ~90 min total when PRF is included (draw + prep time).

**Sensation.** Most describe a vibrating, scratchy, or sandpaper-like feeling; deeper settings and bony areas feel more intense. Topical numbing may be used per provider policy. The PRF upgrade adds a quick blood draw (mild pinch, occasional lightheadedness or bruising at the draw site).

**Downtime & what to expect.** Expect redness/erythema, mild swelling, warmth or a sunburn-like sensation, tightness, dryness, itching, and light flaking/exfoliative scaling. Authoritative sources describe erythema, mild edema, and exfoliative scaling typically lasting **2–3 days**, with many able to resume normal activity the next day (with sun protection). Deeper corrective and body work can run longer; House aftercare frames normal recovery as resolving within **3–10 days**. Pinpoint bleeding and minor bruising can occur. The provider gives written aftercare rather than promising a fixed timeline.

**Aftercare (from the House pre/post-care):**
- Drink 64+ oz water day-of and daily for a week.
- Don't touch/press/rub the area for 8 hours. Ice 10-on/10-off for swelling if needed.
- **No sunscreen or makeup for 12 hours;** first cleanse no sooner than 6 hours (gentle cleanser like Cetaphil, lukewarm water).
- 12–72 hours: minimal makeup ok; gentle cleanser + **hyaluronic acid serum** + **physical/mineral SPF 25+**. Occlusive balm (e.g., Aquaphor) if the provider advises after more aggressive work.
- **No actives** (AHAs/BHAs/retinol/low-pH vitamin C) for at least 2 hours post; return to regular routine after 48–72 hours (retinol strongly recommended once resumed for long-term results).
- Avoid direct sun/tanning/heat for 3 days; no swimming for 24 hours; no exercise/sweat for 3 days; avoid alcohol-based toners 10–14 days.
- **Before:** stop retinoids/vitamin A 3 days prior; avoid alcohol/caffeine/nicotine 3 days before & after; avoid NSAIDs (aspirin, ibuprofen, Aleve) and blood-thinning supplements (ginkgo, garlic, fish/flax oil, vitamin E) 3 days–1 week before & after — the treatment is an intentional controlled inflammatory response and these can blunt it.

**Results timeline.** Early changes often 2–6 weeks; collagen remodeling develops over weeks to months (neocollagenesis continues ~3–6 months). Results are individual.

**Cadence.** Most corrective goals are a **series of 3–6 sessions, ~4–6 weeks apart**, then individualized maintenance. Body skin is thicker, so a **longer series (Series of 6) is realistic**, ~4 weeks apart. (Clinical literature notes sessions can be repeated after a minimum of ~3 weeks; the House consent doc notes acne scarring often runs longer courses and stretch-mark/surgical scars are spaced further apart to allow fuller recovery — the provider sets the actual plan.)

## 5. Comparisons (honest)

**Corrective microneedling vs. Procell Microchanneling.** Two different tools, not gentle-vs-strong tiers of one thing.
- **Procell microchanneling wins** when the goal is **overall skin quality** — texture, fine lines, enlarged-looking pores, dullness, glow, maintenance — and when the client is sensitive skin / a first-timer wanting predictable low downtime. It's a **branded, fixed-protocol stamping system** delivered with Procell's professional growth-factor serums (Pro for under-40/preventative; MD for 40+, 2× the growth factors of Pro). Predictable-recovery, comfortable, face + approved body areas.
- **Corrective microneedling wins** when a **specific correction** is the job and the provider needs to **dial depth and pattern** to it: deeper/rolling acne-scar appearance, defined scar texture, or stretch-mark texture on the body. It's the adjustable-depth pen/stamp category, often planned as a series, ± topical PRF.
- **In one line:** channeling for *quality and glow*; corrective needling for *depth-targeted correction*. The provider chooses at consultation.

**Corrective microneedling vs. the PRF-microneedling upgrade.** Same needling procedure — the upgrade adds **topical PRF** (platelet-rich fibrin prepared from your own blood, applied on the surface during the protocol). It suits clients who value an **autologous ("your own blood") boost** and are appropriate for a blood draw and screening; it adds a draw, prep time, cost, and clinical screening. It is **topical only — never injected**; the pen does not inject or "deliver PRF into" the skin (the FDA states microneedling devices are not authorized to deliver blood products into skin). Small studies of fluid PRF alongside needling for atrophic acne scars report encouraging results, but protocols vary — so we don't promise PRF outperforms needling alone for every person. Injectable PRF/EZ-Gel is a **separate medical procedure and Diana's (RN) lane**, not this.

**Corrective microneedling vs. BioRePeel.** BioRePeel is a **TCA-based resurfacing peel** (biphasic, minimal-downtime) that works chemically at the surface to refresh tone and texture — no needles, no controlled micro-injury cascade in the same sense. **BioRePeel wins** for radiance, tone, and superficial texture with very little social downtime, and it layers well as a booster. **Corrective microneedling wins** for reaching deeper dermal scar tissue and stretch-mark bands that a peel doesn't remodel. They're complementary: BioRePeel can be a prep or an add-on, and for advanced acne scarring the studio also offers a dedicated **BioRePeel Advanced — Acne Scarring** protocol.

**Corrective microneedling (Body) vs. other options for stretch marks.** For **stretch-mark texture on the body**, adjustable-depth **Microneedling — Body** is the depth-targeted regenerative choice (thicker skin → deeper, slower settings → realistic Series of 6). **BioRePeel Body** (TCA peel for stretch marks/scarring) is the chemical-resurfacing alternative, and **PRF Body** adds the autologous topical boost. None "removes" stretch marks — they soften the **appearance** of mature marks; response varies with age, color, and location, and newer marks (≤6 months) may be spaced differently. The provider picks the modality per zone.

## 9. FAQ bank (answer-first, compliance-clean)

**What is corrective microneedling?** It's adjustable-depth collagen-induction therapy: a pen or stamp makes controlled micro-channels in the skin, and the provider dials the depth to a specific concern — the appearance of deeper or rolling acne scars, defined texture, or stretch marks on the body.

**How is it different from Procell microchanneling?** They're two tools for two jobs. Procell microchanneling is a branded, fixed-protocol stamping system for overall skin quality and glow; corrective microneedling is the adjustable-depth category the provider dials to a specific correction. Neither is simply "stronger" than the other.

**Does it hurt?** Most people feel a vibrating, scratchy, sandpaper-like sensation; deeper settings and bony areas feel more intense. Topical numbing may be used per the provider's policy.

**How much downtime is there?** Commonly redness, mild swelling, tightness, and light flaking for a couple of days — authoritative sources describe erythema and exfoliative scaling lasting about 2–3 days. Deeper corrective and body work can run longer; the House aftercare frames normal recovery as resolving within 3–10 days. Your provider gives written aftercare rather than a fixed promise.

**How many sessions will I need?** Most corrective goals are a series — often three to six sessions about four to six weeks apart — then individualized maintenance. Body areas are usually a longer series. Individual results vary.

**Can it remove my acne scars or stretch marks?** No treatment should promise removal. Corrective microneedling may help improve the *appearance* of selected scars and mature stretch marks over time; some concerns need a different approach, and outcomes vary.

**Does it treat active acne?** No. Microneedling is not for active acne, and active breakouts in the area mean we postpone. For active acne, our Face Reality acne program is the right path.

**What is the topical PRF upgrade?** PRF (platelet-rich fibrin) is prepared from a small sample of your own blood, drawn on-site, and applied **on the surface** during the needling protocol as an autologous boost. It adds a draw, prep time, and screening.

**Is the PRF injected through the micro-channels?** No. We describe it accurately as a topical adjunct applied during the protocol. The FDA states microneedling devices are not authorized to deliver blood products into the skin. Injectable PRF is a separate procedure performed by our RN.

**Is this a "vampire facial"?** It's in the same family of blood-derived skin treatments, but ours is **topical PRF only** — applied on the surface during needling, never injected.

**Can you treat the body?** Yes — Microneedling — Body is priced per zone (e.g., stretch marks, scar areas, neck/décolleté, hands, back). Body skin is thicker, so a longer series is realistic.

**Am I a candidate if I have keloids or take Accutane?** Those need provider review. A history of keloid/hypertrophic scarring and recent isotretinoin use are screened carefully, along with active infection, pregnancy/breastfeeding, blood thinners, and immune-suppressing conditions. Not every client is a candidate.

**When will I see results?** Early changes often show in 2–6 weeks; new collagen keeps remodeling for weeks to months. Results are individual.

**How is this different from a BioRePeel?** BioRePeel is a TCA-based resurfacing peel that works chemically at the surface with minimal downtime; corrective microneedling reaches deeper to target dermal scar tissue and stretch-mark bands. They can be used together in a plan.

**Who performs it?** Amber, our advanced esthetician, performs microneedling and topical PRF at House of Rose. Injectables and injectable PRF are handled separately by our RN.
