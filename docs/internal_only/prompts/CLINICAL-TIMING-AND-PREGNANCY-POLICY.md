# §1 — Post-Injectable Timing Windows

**The problem this solves.** Brandy's Sculpt & Lift is a massage-forward service. Aundrea's Royal
Rose has 90 minutes of it. Amber needles. Diana injects. **Nobody had a number.** A client who gets
tox on Tuesday and a lymphatic massage on Thursday can walk out with a drooping eyelid for three
months — and it happened in *our* room, on *our* table, because two of our own services collided.

## 1.1 — The table (draft defaults)

**Read down the left: what Diana did. Read across: how long before you may do yours.**

| After the client had… | No touch / no pressure | Facial massage · lymphatic · sculpting | Dermaplaning over the area | Microneedling / microchanneling over the area | RF · ultrasound · heat · Glo2 over the area | Waxing over the area |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Neurotoxin** (Botox / Daxxify) | **24 h** | **7 days** | **7 days** | **14 days** | **14 days** | **7 days** |
| **Dermal filler** (HA) | **24 h** | **14 days** | **14 days** | **14 days** | **28 days** | **14 days** |
| **PRF — injected** (under-eye / facial) | **24 h** | **14 days** | **14 days** | **14 days** | **14 days** | **14 days** |

**Rationale.** Neurotoxin stays mobile in the tissue for the first days — pressure and increased
circulation migrate it, and migration into the levator is a ptosis. Filler needs roughly **two weeks
to settle and integrate**; pressure, needling, or heat before that moves it or degrades it. Energy
devices (RF/heat) get the longest leash after filler because heat can accelerate HA breakdown.

## 1.2 — The reverse direction (aesthetic → injectable)

Diana also needs a number before she injects into skin we just worked on.

| After the client had… | Wait before neurotoxin / filler / PRF injection |
|---|---|
| Microneedling · microchanneling · PRF fibrin veil | **14 days** |
| BioRePeel / TCA / medium peel | **14 days** |
| Light peel · enzyme · hydrodermabrasion | **7 days** |
| Glo2Facial · basic facial · dermaplaning | **48 h** |
| Waxing over the injection site | **48 h** |

**Why:** injecting through recently needled or peeled skin raises infection risk and muddies the
inflammatory picture — if something goes wrong, nobody can tell which service caused it.

## 1.3 — The four overrides that beat the calendar

These outrank the table. Every time.

1. **Visible bruising, swelling, or tenderness → do not treat, regardless of how many days have passed.** The calendar is a floor, not a permission slip.
2. **Never same-day.** No injectable + needling, peel, or energy device on the same day. Not as a package, not as a favor, not because they drove from Naples.
3. **When in doubt, ask Diana.** She has the chart, the product, the volume, and the placement. You have a guess.
4. **The client's own injector's instructions win** if they were injected elsewhere. Get them in writing. If you can't, apply the longest window in the table.

## 1.4 — Booking rules (front desk / GlossGenius)

- **Ask at booking, not at check-in:** *"Have you had Botox, Daxxify, or filler recently — and when?"*
- **Flag the client file** with the injectable date. Diana's appointments should auto-flag.
- If the requested service falls inside a window → **book them past it.** Do not "check when they get here" — they'll be in the chair, disappointed, and someone will bend.
- **Book the facial BEFORE the tox**, not after, whenever the client wants both. A facial the week before is free of all of this.

# §2 — Pregnancy & Breastfeeding Policy

**The problem this solves.** Mommy & Me is on the menu. Pregnant clients will book it. Right now the
product tray is chosen from memory, and memory is where a retinoid ends up on a pregnant woman's face.

## 2.1 — Ask. Do not infer.

> **"Are you pregnant, breastfeeding, or trying to conceive?"**

Ask it directly, at booking **and** at intake. Never guess from appearance. Never assume from age.
Document the answer.

## 2.2 — HARD STOP — do not perform on a pregnant or breastfeeding client

| Service | Provider | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **All microneedling / microchanneling** | Amber | Infection risk, anesthetic absorption, no safety data |
| **PRF — topical or injected** | Amber / Diana | Blood draw + no safety data in pregnancy |
| **Neurotoxin** (Botox / Daxxify) | Diana | Contraindicated |
| **Dermal filler** | Diana | Contraindicated |
| **IV therapy** | Diana | Fluid/additive load; provider + OB clearance |
| **GLP-1** (Semaglutide / Tirzepatide) | Diana | Contraindicated — includes *trying to conceive* |
| **BioRePeel / TCA / medium-depth peels** | Amber | Systemic absorption; not established as safe |
| **RF, heat, and energy devices** | Amber / Brandy | Not established as safe |
| **Electrical modalities** (galvanic, high-frequency, microcurrent) | Brandy | Not established as safe |

## 2.3 — PERMITTED — the safe lane

- Gentle cleansing facials · hydration · **enzyme exfoliation** (papain, bromelain)
- **Low-strength lactic / glycolic / mandelic** — professional judgment, conservative concentration
- Hydrodermabrasion (gentle setting)
- **Dermaplaning** — permitted; skin is more sensitive, go lighter
- Facial massage · lymphatic drainage — **with positioning (see 2.5)**
- **Waxing** — permitted; skin is more sensitive and more vascular, expect more redness and bruising
- **Makeup** — permitted (Jane Iredale mineral)
- **Permanent jewelry** — ⚠️ **defer.** Err conservative; it can wait.

## 2.4 — The product tray

### 🚫 NEVER on a pregnant or breastfeeding client

| Ingredient | Why |
|---|---|
| **Retinoids** — retinol, retinal, tretinoin, Retin-A, adapalene, tazarotene, any Vitamin A derivative | High-dose Vitamin A in pregnancy is linked to birth defects. **The single most important line on this card.** |
| **Hydroquinone** | **30–40% systemic absorption** after topical use — by far the highest of anything on our shelf |
| **High-percentage salicylic acid · BHA peels** | Low-strength (<2%) leave-on is generally considered acceptable, but **our house rule is to avoid it entirely** — it removes the judgment call from a busy room |
| **TCA · BioRePeel · medium/deep peels** | Absorption + depth |
| **Benzoyl peroxide** (high %) | House rule: conservative — skip it |
| **Aromatherapy / essential-oil-heavy blends** | Several are contraindicated in pregnancy; not worth parsing which |
| **Formaldehyde releasers · chemical depilatories** | Avoid |

### ✅ SAFE — reach for these

Hyaluronic acid · glycerin · **niacinamide** · **azelaic acid** · **Vitamin C (L-ascorbic)** ·
**bakuchiol** (the pregnancy-safe retinol alternative — *say this to her, she'll want to know*) ·
PHAs · low-strength lactic / glycolic / mandelic · enzyme exfoliants (papain, bromelain) ·
ceramides · peptides · **mineral SPF (zinc oxide / titanium dioxide)**.

> **Melasma note.** Pregnancy hormones drive melasma hard — "the mask of pregnancy." She will ask
> about it. **Mineral SPF is the answer, and it is the honest one.** Do not treat it aggressively
> now: no hydroquinone, no aggressive peels, no heat. Tell her we'll build the plan **after**
> she's done breastfeeding, and that protecting from sun in the meantime is the single highest-value
> thing she can do. That conversation *keeps* the client — it doesn't lose her.

## 2.5 — Positioning & comfort

- **After ~20 weeks: do not lay her flat on her back.** The uterus compresses the vena cava and can
  drop her blood pressure — she gets dizzy, nauseated, and can faint on your table.
- **Incline the bed, or use a wedge to tilt her to the left.** Offer a bolster under the knees.
- Check in verbally. Dizziness, nausea, shortness of breath → **sit her up immediately.**
- Nausea in the first trimester makes strong scents intolerable. **Keep the room neutral.**
- She may need the bathroom mid-service. Build the buffer in and mention it up front so she doesn't
  feel awkward asking.

## 2.6 — Breastfeeding

The needling / injectable / IV / GLP-1 hard stops **still apply while breastfeeding.**
Retinoids and hydroquinone **still apply.** The safe-lane products above are fine.
When in doubt: her OB or pediatrician, not us.

## §3 — What we never say

- 🚫 Never say a service is *"safe for pregnancy"* as a marketing claim. Say: **"we adjust the
  treatment and the products, and your OB has the final say."**
- 🚫 Never tell a client to stop a prescription. That is her prescriber's call.
- 🚫 Never treat a pregnant client "just gently" through a hard-stop service. **The hard stops are hard.**
