ProCell Microchanneling is a structured, provider-selected stamping treatment that creates thousands of tiny, controlled micro-channels in the skin and pairs them with the ProCell growth-factor + cytokine topical serum — the go-to at House of Rose for overall **skin quality**: texture, fine lines, enlarged-looking pores, dullness, and glow. ## 2. How it works

**Plain English.** A microchanneling device stamps very fine needles straight down into the skin, opening thousands of microscopic channels. Those channels do two things: they prompt the skin's natural repair response (which supports collagen and elastin remodeling over the following weeks), and they give the ProCell topical serum a fresh surface to sit on and absorb into during and right after treatment. The result many clients notice is smoother-looking texture and a more refined, luminous appearance as the skin remodels.

**One level deeper.** Microchanneling is a form of **collagen-induction therapy** (neocollagenesis). The controlled micro-injury triggers the ordinary wound-healing cascade — fibroblasts migrate to the micro-wounds and lay down new collagen (type III converting toward type I) and elastin, with growth factors released on injury supporting collagen production, epithelialization, and angiogenesis; remodeling continues over weeks to months. ProCell's own framing describes its system as "microchanneling" that stimulates "the outermost layers of the skin" to start this process — notably, the manufacturer states it works "**without penetrating into living tissue**" and that microchanneling "creates the pathways" while the topical serum supplies the signaling proteins. (This manufacturer framing usefully *reinforces* our compliance line: nothing is delivered *into* the skin — see §10.) The pre/post-care sheet is candid about the mechanism: the retinol/NSAID/fish-oil restrictions before treatment exist because the protocol is "intentionally creating a controlled inflammatory response" — the point is a measured, healthy repair signal, not damage.

**What's delivered is a separate layer from the tool.** Microchanneling is the *modality* (the stamping). What's applied into/onto the channels is the *serum layer* — the ProCell professional serum (**Pro** or **MD** tier; see `procell.md`) or, as an alternative autologous add-on, **topical PRF** made from the client's own blood. ProCell describes its Microchannel Delivery Serum, in its own words, as containing **growth factors, cytokines, and DNA repair enzymes in a hyaluronic-acid suspension**, made from **bio-engineered sources**, with **no human, animal, or plant cells, DNA, blood, or other foreign substances**. Neither serum is "injected" or "delivered into the skin" (see §10 — this is a load-bearing compliance line), and we do **not** repeat ProCell's own "stem cell"–adjacent marketing language (§10).

## 3. Who it's for / not for

**A good starting point when the goal is:** overall skin quality — visible texture, fine lines, dull-looking skin, enlarged-looking pores, mild unevenness, glow, and maintenance; a structured, controlled facial protocol rather than an aggressive one-off; a sensitive-skin or first-timer client who wants predictable, lower downtime; approved body areas (neck, décolleté, hands, crepey skin — see §5).

**Screen carefully / postpone / provider review required** (FDA screening guidance for microneedling — a screening guide, not a full list; the licensed provider makes the actual call): active skin infection or rash, a current cold-sore outbreak, uncontrolled diabetes, immune suppression, bleeding/clotting disorders or anticoagulant use, recent isotretinoin use, a history of keloid scarring, certain autoimmune conditions, pregnancy or breastfeeding, planned sun exposure, and known allergies to materials or topicals. Never needle sunburned, actively inflamed, infected, or open skin.

**Honest limits.** Results are individual and develop gradually — no treatment here promises a specific degree of improvement or a permanent result. Pore size is not permanently changed. Deep ice-pick, tethered, hypertrophic, or keloid-prone scars, and defined scar/stretch-mark texture, are usually better matched to **corrective microneedling** (adjustable depth) — not the fixed microchanneling protocol (see §5). Not every client is a candidate.

## 4. The visit

**Protocol, step by step (experience guidance, not a fixed medical protocol):**
1. **Skin analysis & candidacy review** — the provider confirms candidacy, contraindications, treatment area, device setting, and serum tier (Pro vs MD; topical PRF is discussed as an optional add-on).
2. **Prep & cleanse** — the skin is cleansed; a topical numbing product may be used per the house protocol. A **new single-use cartridge** is opened for every client, every session.
3. **Microchanneling pass(es)** — the device stamps the fixed, controlled settings across the treatment zones. (If **topical PRF** is added, a small blood draw is taken on-site and processed in a centrifuge beforehand, then applied topically during the protocol.)
4. **Serum layer** — the ProCell professional serum (or topical PRF) is applied into/onto the fresh channels.
5. **Finish & aftercare** — optional add-ons (LightStim LED to calm the skin, a Glo2Facial finish); written aftercare is given.

**Sensation.** Most clients describe a light stamping/vibration and warmth; the fixed microchanneling settings are the comfortable, predictable-recovery end of the needling spectrum.

**Downtime / what to expect.** Redness and a warm, "sunburn-like" sensation are common (the "sunburn-like" descriptor comes from our own consent form; per the FDA the general effects are bleeding, bruising, redness, tightness, itching, and peeling that resolve on their own over days to weeks); tightness, dryness, mild flaking, or pinpoint bleeding can occur. ProCell positions microchanneling as **minimal downtime** — skin stays pink/light-red and may be sensitive for several days — versus deeper microneedling, which it puts at ~5–7 days. Recovery varies with the area, setting, and skin, so the house gives written aftercare rather than promising a fixed 24- or 72-hour timeline. The care sheet's general "what to expect" window for mild redness/flaking is roughly **3–10 days** (individual).

**Aftercare highlights (from the pre/post-care sheet):** no sunscreen or makeup for 12 hours; don't touch/rub the area for 8 hours; wash no sooner than 6 hours with a gentle cleanser and lukewarm water; hyaluronic acid serum + a physical (mineral) SPF 25+ from 12–72 hours; nothing "active" (AHA/BHA/retinol/low-pH vitamin C) for at least 2 hours post (and typically resume retinol 48–72 hrs); no exercise/sweat, swimming for 24 hrs, or heat/sun/tanning for ~3 days; hydrate (64 oz+/day for a week); avoid alcohol/caffeine/nicotine and NSAIDs/blood-thinning supplements ~3 days before & after. Retinol is encouraged once the routine resumes, for long-term results.

**Results timeline.** Early changes often 2–6 weeks; remodeling continues over weeks to months. Individual.

**Cadence.** Most goals are a **series of 3–6 visits, ~4–6 weeks apart**, then individualized maintenance. Body skin is thicker, so a longer series (Series of 6) is realistic there. Sold at House of Rose as **Single · Series of 3 ("Renewal") · Series of 6 ("Full Correction")**.

## 5. Comparisons (honest)

**Microchanneling vs. corrective microneedling — the flagship "two tools, different jobs" distinction.** These are *different tools for different jobs*, not gentle-vs-strong tiers of one thing.
- **ProCell Microchanneling** — a *branded, fixed-protocol stamping* treatment with controlled settings and the ProCell serum. Wins for **overall skin quality** (texture, fine lines, pores, dullness, glow, maintenance) and for sensitive skin / first-timers who want predictable, low downtime. Face + approved body. *(ProCell's own positioning: stamping creates "uniformly distributed channels without causing skin tearing or dragging," whereas pen/roller microneedling creates "irregular and angled channels" — attribute this "cleaner vertical channels" claim to ProCell; it is not independently FDA-adjudicated.)*
- **Microneedling — Corrective** (`/services/microneedling-corrective/`) — the *broader, adjustable-depth* category (pen/stamp). The provider dials depth and pattern to a **specific correction**: the appearance of deeper or rolling acne scars, defined scar texture, and stretch-mark texture on the body. Wins when the job is targeted correction rather than global quality.
- *The provider chooses based on concern, area, skin history, and downtime tolerance — not on "stronger vs. gentler."*

**Microchanneling vs. Glo2Facial** (`/services/glo2facial/`, from $185). Glo2Facial is the **no-downtime gateway** — oxygenation, gentle exfoliation, lymphatic massage; an immediate glow with nothing broken. Microchanneling is the **regenerative** step: it works below the surface with recovery attached. Glo2Facial wins for event-ready glow with zero downtime and as the entry point / base for corrective work; microchanneling wins for lasting texture and collagen-support goals. They pair well — a **Glo2Facial finish (+$95)** can be added to a needling treatment.

**Microchanneling vs. BioRePeel** (`/services/biorepeel/`, from $295). BioRePeel is a TCA-based resurfacing **peel** — chemical resurfacing for radiance/texture with minimal downtime; microchanneling is **mechanical** collagen induction. Different mechanisms, overlapping goals (texture, glow). BioRePeel wins for a peel-based radiance boost or as a **+$65 finish** layered onto channeling; microchanneling wins for collagen-support/texture over a series. The provider decides whether to layer or alternate.

**Microchanneling with vs. without topical PRF.** Baseline microchanneling delivers the ProCell serum. Adding **topical PRF (+$175 face / +$200 body)** swaps in an **autologous** boost made from the client's own blood, applied topically during the protocol. With-PRF suits clients who value an "own-blood" approach and are appropriate for the blood draw and screening; without-PRF is simpler and streamlined. PRF adds a blood draw, cost, and clinical screening — and it is **topical only, never injected** (see §10). Evidence for topical PRF + needling is encouraging but variable, so we don't claim PRF is better for every client or concern.

**ProCell body microchanneling** (`/services/procell-microchanneling-body/`, from $349/zone). Same structured growth-factor channeling, on the body — crepey skin, hands, décolleté, and overall body-skin quality, priced **per zone**. Body skin is thicker, so a longer series (Series of 6) is realistic. For the *appearance of stretch marks and defined scar texture* on the body, **Microneedling — Body** (adjustable depth) is often the better tool; body microchanneling is the skin-quality choice.

## 9. FAQ bank

**Is ProCell microchanneling the same as microneedling?**
It's a branded needling protocol *within* the broader microneedling category. Both use tiny needles to create controlled punctures, but microchanneling is a fixed, structured stamping protocol with the ProCell serum, while "microneedling" is the broader, adjustable-depth category the provider dials to a specific correction. The device, settings, serum, and candidacy review matter more than the label.

**What is microchanneling best for?**
Overall skin quality — the appearance of texture, fine lines, enlarged-looking pores, dullness, and glow — and as a comfortable, predictable-recovery maintenance treatment. Many clients notice smoother-looking, more luminous skin as it remodels over weeks.

**How is microchanneling (stamping) different from a dermaroller?**
The device stamps needles straight down to create clean, vertical channels at a controlled depth, rather than dragging at an angle the way a roller does. That precision is part of why microchanneling is positioned as the controlled, predictable-recovery option.

**Does it hurt? What's the downtime?**
Most clients feel a light stamping and warmth. Redness and a "sunburn-like" feel are common afterward; mild flaking or tightness can follow. Recovery varies with the setting and your skin, so we give written aftercare rather than a fixed timeline — mild effects generally settle within a few days to about a week.

**How many sessions will I need?**
Most goals are a series — often three to six visits about four to six weeks apart — then individualized maintenance. Your provider recommends the plan after reviewing your concern, skin history, and how you recover. Individual results vary.

**Can microchanneling remove wrinkles, scars, or pores?**
No treatment should promise removal. Microchanneling may help improve the *appearance* of texture, fine lines, and selected shallow scars over time; pore size isn't permanently changed, and some concerns need a different approach.

**What is the ProCell serum? Is it a "stem cell" serum?**
It's a professional serum that ProCell describes as containing growth factors, cytokines, and DNA repair enzymes in a hyaluronic-acid base, made from bio-engineered sources — with no human, animal, or plant cells, DNA, or blood. It is not a "stem-cell" treatment, and we don't say anything is delivered deep into the skin. Which serum tier (Pro or MD) fits your skin is confirmed at consultation.

**Can I add topical PRF to my microchanneling?**
Yes — topical PRF is an optional, autologous add-on made from your own blood and applied *topically* during the protocol. It adds a small blood draw, cost, and clinical screening. It is topical only; it is never injected.

**Does the topical PRF or serum get injected through the channels?**
No. We describe PRF and the serum accurately as applied topically during the protocol. The FDA states microneedling devices are not authorized to deliver blood products or cosmetics into the skin.

**Can microchanneling be done on the body?**
Yes — ProCell Microchanneling — Body is offered per zone for crepey skin, hands, décolleté, and overall body-skin quality. Body skin is thicker, so a longer series is realistic. For the appearance of stretch marks or defined scar texture, corrective microneedling is often the better tool.

**Who performs it, and is it safe?**
It's performed by Amber, a licensed esthetician, with a new single-use cartridge every session and a candidacy/contraindication review first. We never needle sunburned, inflamed, infected, or open skin.

**Am I a candidate?**
Many people are, but not everyone. Active infection, cold sores, certain medications (like recent isotretinoin or blood thinners), a keloid history, pregnancy, and some health conditions mean we postpone or adjust. The licensed provider makes the final call at consultation.

**How is this different from a Glo2Facial?**
Glo2Facial is a no-downtime glow treatment that works at the surface; microchanneling works below the surface to support collagen remodeling and has some recovery. Many clients start with Glo2Facial and step up to microchanneling for lasting texture — or add a Glo2Facial finish to their channeling.

**When will I see results?**
Some clients notice early changes within a few weeks; collagen remodeling continues over weeks to months, which is why a series is common. Outcomes are individual.

**Do I need to prep my skin beforehand?**
Yes — typically pause retinols/vitamin A and NSAIDs/blood-thinning supplements for a few days before, avoid alcohol and excess caffeine, and arrive with clean skin. Your provider gives you the full pre-care list.

## 10. Provider + compliance notes

**Lane.** Microchanneling is **Amber's** lane (licensed esthetician; advanced facials). Topical PRF that accompanies it is **topical only — never injected**. Injectable PRF, EZ-Gel, PRF injections, and any future Micro-Tox procedure are **Diana's (RN)** clinical lane. Do not combine a neurotoxin with microchanneling in public or protocol copy unless a medical-director-approved workflow explicitly supports it.

**Phrases to avoid for this topic:**
- Never "injects," "delivers into the skin," "delivered deep," or "FDA-approved PRF microneedling." The FDA states microneedling devices are not approved to deliver cosmetics, topical medications, vitamin solutions, drugs, or blood products (e.g. PRP/PRF) **into the skin**, and has **not cleared any microneedling device for use with another product**. (ProCell's own language — "without penetrating into living tissue" — aligns with this; lean on it.)
- **Do not lift ProCell's own "stem cell"–adjacent marketing.** ProCell's science/SEO material references mesenchymal/bone-marrow-derived stem-cell literature and "conditioned medium." For a Florida med-spa: never say the treatment or serum *contains, uses, or delivers* stem cells, or "regenerates/repairs DNA." Use only ProCell's literal ingredient statement (growth factors, cytokines, DNA repair enzymes, HA; bio-engineered; no cells/DNA/blood) and make no efficacy claim.
- **FDA clearance is narrow.** Microneedling devices are FDA-cleared to *improve the appearance of* facial acne scars, facial wrinkles, and abdominal scars in patients 22+. Broader ProCell indications (rosacea, hyperpigmentation, stretch marks) are **manufacturer claims, not FDA clearances** — never present them as "FDA-approved/cleared."
- Never "erases / removes / eliminates" wrinkles, scars, pores, or stretch marks; never "reverses aging / anti-aging / age-defying"; never "heals" or "treats/cures acne (or any condition)."
- Never "guaranteed," "permanent," a fixed downtime, or "clinically proven / FDA-approved" without a specific verifiable citation.
- No "day spa." No discount language.
- Do **not** market ProCell **MD** as simply "stronger/deeper/medical/superior" without the confirmed manufacturer positioning — the confirmed, usable framing is: **Pro** = professional growth-factor serum (maintenance/prevention, under-40); **MD** = ~2× the growth factors, the go-to for 40+ (see `procell.md`).

**Say instead:** "designed to," "supports," "helps reveal," "for the appearance of," "the visible signs of," "many clients notice," "supports skin longevity." Pricing = "investment," "from," "starting at."

**Required guardrails on every surface:** provider authority (final candidacy/settings/serum/combinations confirmed by the licensed provider at consultation) · "not every client is a candidate" · "individual outcomes vary" · single-use cartridge every session · never needle sunburned/inflamed/infected/open skin · packages/discounts carry the Fla. Stat. 456.062 disclaimer · identify the licensee performing each service.

- U.S. FDA — *Microneedling Devices: Getting to the Point* consumer update (fda.gov) — plain-language recovery/effects ("bleeding, bruising, redness, tightness, itching and peeling... after a few days or weeks"). Authoritative regulator.
- ProCell Therapies (https://procelltherapies.com/faq/ and /science/) — manufacturer: the "microchanneling = stamping, no tearing/dragging" positioning, "without penetrating into living tissue," the verbatim serum ingredient statement, minimal-downtime framing, and the manufacturer contraindication list. Primary source, but marketing — its stem-cell-adjacent science language is flagged as **do-not-repeat** (§10).
- *Microneedling narrative review* (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11993440/) — peer-reviewed; wound-healing/neocollagenesis mechanism (type III→I collagen, VEGF/TGF-β/PDGF) and the "encouraging but limited/variable, multi-session" evidence framing.
- Diab et al., *Fluid Platelet-Rich Fibrin vs PRP with Needling in Atrophic Acne Scars* (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10205840/) — peer-reviewed; supports "encouraging but variable" topical-PRF framing, not universal outcome claims.
