## 2. How it works

**Plain English.** Hyaluronic acid is a sugar molecule your own skin already makes; it binds water and gives tissue its plumpness and cushion. As the face ages it loses HA, fat, and bone support, so cheeks flatten, folds deepen, and lips thin. A dermal filler is a manufactured HA gel placed under the skin to physically replace that lost volume the moment it's injected — you see the result immediately, not weeks later. Because it's the same base molecule your body recognizes, it integrates with your tissue and is gradually, naturally broken down over months.

**One level deeper (for the curious).** Raw HA would dissolve in a day, so manufacturers **cross-link** the chains into a durable gel; how they cross-link determines how the product behaves:

- **G-prime (G′) / elastic modulus = "lift."** A high-G′ gel is firm and resists deformation, so it holds tissue up — ideal for structural work like cheeks. A lower-G′ gel is softer and spreads smoothly — better for delicate, mobile areas like lips and fine lines. In peer-reviewed rheology data, **Juvéderm Voluma** measures a high G′ (~600 Pa) — a lifting/structural gel — while **Juvéderm Ultra** measures much lower (~100–175 Pa) — a soft, integrating gel. This is *why* different products go in different places; it's not marketing.
- **Cohesivity = how the gel holds together** under the compression of a smiling, chewing, talking face, which resists migration.
- **The RHA difference — movement.** The RHA Collection (Teoxane, distributed by Revance) uses a gentler, heatless manufacturing process ("Preserved Network Technology") with fewer chemical modifications, so the gel more closely resembles your skin's own HA and is designed to **stretch and recoil with dynamic expression** — the areas that crease when you move. RHA 2, RHA 3, and RHA 4 are the only HA fillers FDA-approved specifically for *dynamic* wrinkles and folds.
- **Juvéderm's VYCROSS technology** blends low- and high-molecular-weight HA for a smooth, cohesive, longer-lasting gel (Voluma XC).
- **Reversibility.** Because it's HA, an enzyme called **hyaluronidase** can dissolve it — for fine-tuning, or urgently if there's ever a vascular problem. This reversibility is a core safety feature of HA fillers and a reason they're the most-used category.

## 3. Who it's for / not for

**Good candidates** typically want to:
- Restore midface/cheek volume that has flattened with age ("a subtle mid-face reset").
- Add definition or fullness to lips, or soften the vertical lines around the mouth.
- Soften static folds — nasolabial folds (nose-to-mouth) and marionette lines.
- Keep a natural, movement-preserving result rather than an overfilled look.

**Not a candidate / defer (Diana confirms at consultation):**
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — fillers are not offered.
- **Active infection, inflammation, or an active breakout at the injection site.**
- **Known hypersensitivity** to HA, to lidocaine (both Juvéderm and RHA products here contain lidocaine, the "XC" / built-in anesthetic), or to gram-positive bacterial proteins.
- **History of severe or anaphylactic allergy**, or **bleeding disorders / anticoagulant use** that raise bruising or bleeding risk.
- **A tendency to keloid or hypertrophic scarring.**
- **Recent or upcoming dental work, or an unmanaged autoimmune condition** — timing and suitability are a provider call.
- **Anyone wanting a "permanent" result** — HA fillers are temporary by design, and we don't offer or imply permanence.

**Honest limits.** Filler adds volume; it does not relax muscles (that's neurotoxin's job — see §5) and it does not resurface skin texture or tone (that's PRF/microchanneling/peels). Under the eyes specifically, HA filler is often *not* the first choice — see the under-eye note in §5 and §6. Results are temporary and individual; not every client is a candidate for every product or area.

## 4. The visit

**Consultation first, always.** Diana assesses your anatomy, goals, and history, explains realistic outcomes, and selects the product(s) and amount. Final combinations and candidacy are confirmed by her — not promised in advance.

**Step-by-step (typical):**
1. Facial assessment, photos, mapping of injection points.
2. Cleanse/prep; a topical numbing step if used (all products here also contain lidocaine for comfort).
3. Injection via fine needle or **blunt-tip cannula** (a cannula can reduce bruising and vessel risk in some areas; Voluma XC is FDA-approved for cannula delivery in the cheeks).
4. Gentle molding to shape the result; a look-and-adjust check.

**Sensation.** Small pinches and pressure; the built-in lidocaine and (if used) topical numbing keep most visits very tolerable. Lips are the most sensitive area.

**Downtime.** This is a "some social downtime" treatment, not a no-downtime one. Expect possible swelling, tenderness, redness, and bruising for a few days (lips can swell noticeably for 24–72 hours). Most people return to work quickly but may schedule around a visible event.

**Aftercare (general — Diana gives you the specifics):**
- Keep the area clean; avoid heavy makeup on day one.
- Avoid strenuous exercise, alcohol, and excess heat (sauna, sun, hot yoga) for ~24–48 hours to limit swelling/bruising.
- Avoid pressing or massaging unless instructed; sleep slightly elevated the first night for lips/cheeks.
- Arnica or cool compresses can help bruising if approved.
- **Call immediately** for blanching (white/pale skin), unusual or increasing pain, dusky discoloration, or vision changes — these are the vascular-safety warning signs Diana reviews with you.

**Results timeline.** Volume is visible immediately; the true result settles over ~2 weeks as swelling resolves and the gel integrates. A touch-up/refinement visit at ~2–4 weeks is common and normal.

**Longevity & cadence (by product — general clinical ranges, not a guarantee; your result varies):**
- **Juvéderm Voluma XC (cheeks):** up to ~24 months with optimal treatment.
- **Juvéderm Ultra XC (lips/lower face):** up to ~1 year (lips often less due to movement).
- **RHA Collection (RHA 2/3, dynamic areas):** up to ~15 months.
- **RHA 1 (finest tier, fine lines/delicate areas):** shorter in high-movement zones.
Maintenance is periodic top-ups as the gel breaks down; Diana sets your interval.

## 5. Comparisons (honest — only against treatments House of Rose offers)

**HA filler vs. neurotoxins (Botox / Daxxify — also Diana's lane).**
Different jobs. **Filler adds volume and structure** (restores what's lost — cheeks, lips, folds). **Neurotoxin relaxes the muscles** that crease dynamic lines (forehead, frown, crow's feet). Filler fills; tox softens motion. They're frequently *paired* — e.g., tox to soften the upper face while filler restores midface volume — but one is never a substitute for the other. Wins: choose **filler** for hollowing, flat cheeks, thin lips, and deep static folds; choose **neurotoxin** for expression lines caused by muscle movement. See the neurotoxin brief and `/services/injectables/`.

**HA filler vs. PRF "EZ Gel" bio-filler (also Diana's lane).**
Both add volume, but the material is fundamentally different. **HA filler is a manufactured gel** — off-the-shelf, precise, immediate, longer-lasting, and reversible with hyaluronidase. **EZ Gel is an autologous bio-filler** made from *your own blood* (heated platelet-rich fibrin), so it's "made from you," fully natural, and works partly by stimulating your own collagen — but it's more modest in projection and doesn't last as long as HA. Wins: choose **HA filler** when you want defined structure, a strong lip or cheek result, predictable longevity, and the reversibility safety net; choose **EZ Gel** when the priority is an all-natural, no-synthetic material, gradual skin-quality improvement, or you prefer your own biology. The two can be complementary. See `/services/ez-gel-bio-filler/`.

**RHA family vs. Juvéderm family (both HA, different engineering).**
Not better/worse — different design goals. **Juvéderm** (VYCROSS) leans toward **cohesive structure and longevity** — Voluma XC is a firm, high-lift gel for cheek scaffolding; Ultra XC is a smooth, softer gel for lips. **RHA** is engineered for **flexibility and dynamic movement** — it stretches with expression, so it's favored where the face moves a lot (perioral lines, lips-in-motion, animated folds). Wins: choose **Voluma XC** for structural midface lift; **Ultra XC** for classic lip/lower-face volume; **RHA** when preserving natural movement in an expressive area is the priority. Diana matches the gel's rheology (lift vs. spread) to the job.

**Under-eye special case (important):** for the **tear trough / dark circles**, House of Rose often prefers **PRF (autologous) over HA filler**, because HA under thin under-eye skin can trap water and puff, and PRF regenerates the area more naturally. This is a Diana call — see §6 and the PRF brief. Do not position HA filler as the default under-eye answer.

## 9. FAQ bank (answer-first, compliance-clean)

1. **What are dermal fillers made of?**
   Hyaluronic acid — a water-binding sugar your skin already produces — formulated into a smooth gel. House of Rose uses the Juvéderm and RHA hyaluronic-acid families.

2. **How long do fillers last?**
   It depends on the product and the area. General ranges: cheeks with Voluma XC up to about 24 months; lips/lower face with Ultra XC up to about a year; the RHA Collection up to about 15 months. Movement-heavy areas like lips tend toward the shorter end. Your result is individual.

3. **Does it hurt?**
   Most clients tolerate it well. Every filler we use contains lidocaine for comfort, and a topical numbing step is available. Lips are the most sensitive area; expect small pinches and pressure.

4. **Will I look "done" or overfilled?**
   The goal here is natural — restore, don't distort. Diana doses conservatively and uses movement-preserving products (the RHA Collection is designed to flex with expression). You can always add; that's why we start measured.

5. **Is filler reversible?**
   Yes — a key advantage of hyaluronic acid. An enzyme called hyaluronidase can dissolve HA filler, whether to refine a result or to resolve a problem quickly. This is one reason HA is the most-used filler category.

6. **What's the difference between RHA and Juvéderm?**
   Both are hyaluronic acid, engineered differently. Juvéderm leans toward cohesive structure and longevity (Voluma for cheek lift, Ultra for lips). RHA is built to stretch and move with your expressions, so it's favored in animated areas. Diana matches the product to the job.

7. **Filler or Botox — which do I need?**
   Different tools. Filler *adds volume* (cheeks, lips, folds). Botox/Daxxify *relaxes muscles* that crease dynamic lines. Many clients do both. Diana determines the right mix at consultation.

8. **Can filler fix my under-eye dark circles?**
   Sometimes — but under the eyes we often prefer PRF, a natural option made from your own blood, because HA under thin under-eye skin can hold water and puff. Diana will tell you honestly which is right for you.

9. **How many syringes will I need?**
   It varies by goal and anatomy. Lips may take one; restoring midface volume often takes more than one over time. Diana estimates this at consultation — there's no one-size answer.

10. **Are these products FDA-approved?**
    Yes, for their labeled uses: Juvéderm Voluma XC is FDA-approved for cheek/midface volume; Juvéderm Ultra XC is FDA-approved for lip and perioral augmentation; RHA 2, RHA 3, and RHA 4 are FDA-approved for dynamic facial wrinkles and folds. Your specific plan and product are chosen by Diana.

11. **What's the downtime?**
    Plan for a few days of possible swelling, tenderness, and occasional bruising; lips can swell for 24–72 hours. Many people return to work quickly but book around a visible event.

12. **Why does it matter that a nurse injects, not just anyone?**
    Because facial anatomy includes blood vessels, and rare complications like vascular occlusion require immediate, trained management (including hyaluronidase). Diana is an RN working under medical-director oversight, trained to place filler safely and to respond if anything is off.

13. **Can I combine filler with a facial or microneedling?**
    Often yes, as part of a plan — but usually on separate visits and in the right order. Injectables (Diana) and skin-quality treatments (Amber) are sequenced so each does its job. We'll map it for you.

14. **When will I see the final result?**
    Volume shows immediately; the result settles over about two weeks as swelling resolves. A refinement visit around 2–4 weeks is common.

15. **Do I have to keep getting it?**
    No obligation. Filler is temporary and gradually breaks down; if you like the look, periodic maintenance keeps it. If you don't, it simply fades — or can be dissolved.
