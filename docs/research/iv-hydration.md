## 1. The 3-sentence answer

IV hydration and vitamin therapy at House of Rose is an RN-administered infusion of fluids, electrolytes, and vitamins delivered directly into a vein by Diana, RN, under medical-director oversight, at our private Punta Gorda studio. Because the infusion bypasses digestion, it helps replenish fluid and nutrients quickly and completely — the menu runs from a simple **Hydration IV** (a fast fluid-and-electrolyte reset) up through the classic **Myers' Cocktail**, with targeted blends for immune support, post-activity recovery, a from-within "beauty glow," and morning-after rebound. It is designed to support hydration and overall wellness for SW Florida's heat-, water-, and activity-heavy lifestyle — it is not a treatment for any medical condition, and Diana screens every client for candidacy first.

## 2. How it works

**Plain English.** When you take a vitamin by mouth or drink water, it has to survive your stomach and be absorbed through your gut — a process that's variable and incomplete, and that your liver partly filters before the rest reaches your bloodstream. An IV skips all of that: a small catheter placed in a vein by an RN drips a sterile fluid-and-nutrient solution straight into your circulation, so the fluid and the vitamins are available to your body right away and at close to 100% of the delivered dose. That's why an IV can restore hydration and replenish nutrients faster and more completely than the oral route — the trade-off is that it requires a nurse, a needle, and a 30–45-minute sit.

**One level deeper.**
- **Fluid + electrolytes.** The base of every drip is a crystalloid fluid (a saline- or balanced-electrolyte solution — small dissolved molecules that move easily from bloodstream into tissue), which restores circulating fluid volume and replaces electrolytes (sodium, and where blended in, magnesium and others) more quickly than drinking. This is the core mechanism of the **Hydration IV** and the fluid backbone of every other blend.
- **Water-soluble vitamins (B-complex, B12, vitamin C).** Given orally, absorption of these is capped and excess is lost; delivered IV, higher circulating levels are reached transiently. B vitamins are cofactors in energy metabolism; B12 (often as hydroxocobalamin) supports red-blood-cell and nerve function; vitamin C is an antioxidant and a cofactor in collagen synthesis.
- **Magnesium & calcium.** Minerals in the Myers'-style blends; magnesium supports muscle and nervous-system function and is the classic Myers' component.
- **Glutathione.** The body's "master antioxidant" tripeptide — it helps neutralize reactive oxygen species (the byproducts of normal metabolism) and supports the cellular redox balance the skin depends on for clarity. This is the science behind the "beauty / detox" positioning of the Glutathione add-on and Beauty Glow IV.
- **NAD+.** Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide is a coenzyme every cell needs for mitochondrial energy (ATP) production and for enzymes that handle DNA repair and cellular signaling. It's positioned around cellular energy and recovery. Honesty note: the human clinical evidence for infused NAD+ is early — tolerability data exist, but metabolic outcomes are still being studied. We frame it as "supports cellular energy," never as an anti-aging or disease claim.

**The honest ceiling (keep this in the copy, not buried).** The high-quality clinical evidence that IV vitamin drips outperform a good diet and adequate oral hydration *in already-healthy people* is limited — reputable sources (Cleveland Clinic, peer-reviewed reviews) are candid that this is a wellness/self-care tool, not a proven medical therapy for the average person. That honesty is a feature: it's exactly why the value here is the **RN + medical-director standard of care**, not a miracle-cure promise.

## 3. Who it's for / not for

**A good fit (supports wellness goals, screened by Diana):**
- Adults who are dehydrated or depleted after heat exposure, a day on the water, travel, or intense activity/workouts.
- Clients who want a convenient, complete way to replenish fluids and vitamins and who "many clients notice" feel refreshed afterward.
- Skin-health clients pursuing a from-within complement to their facial plan (Beauty Glow IV / glutathione).
- People who tolerate oral supplements poorly or want the bypass-the-gut route on occasion.

**Not a candidate / provider screens first — Diana will decline or defer for:**
- **Kidney (renal) disease**, **heart disease / congestive heart failure**, or **uncontrolled high blood pressure** — fluid and electrolyte (especially magnesium/potassium) loads can be unsafe; these are hard screening flags.
- **Pregnancy** — infusions are avoided.
- Known allergy/sensitivity to a component, certain medication interactions, or a G6PD deficiency (relevant to high-dose vitamin C / glutathione) — provider-screened.
- Anyone seeking IV therapy to *treat* a diagnosed illness — that's outside this wellness service; we refer to their physician.

**Honest limits.** IV therapy is not a substitute for prescribed medication, a healthy diet, sleep, or a dermatologist/physician's care. Effects of a single drip are generally short-lived; hydration status and nutrition are ongoing. Not every client is a candidate, and individual outcomes vary — final candidacy is confirmed by Diana, RN, under medical-director oversight, at consultation.

## 4. The visit

**Protocol, step by step:**
1. **Intake & screening.** Diana, RN reviews your health history, medications, allergies, and goals, and confirms you're a candidate (blood pressure/vitals as indicated). This is the medical-director-overseen gate — it's what separates a clinical infusion from a pop-up drip bar.
2. **Blend selection.** You and Diana choose the base IV for your goal and any add-ons (see menu below).
3. **Placement.** Diana places a small IV catheter, typically in the arm/hand. A brief pinch on insertion; then no needle stays "sticking out."
4. **Infusion.** The drip runs while you relax in the studio — roughly **30 minutes** for a Hydration IV and **~45 minutes** for the vitamin blends (add-ons add a few minutes). Diana monitors you throughout for comfort and for the normal cautions of any infusion (fluid balance, how you're feeling).
5. **Finish.** The catheter is removed, a small bandage applied, and you're on your way. Most clients return to normal activity immediately.

**Sensation.** A quick pinch at insertion; some blends (magnesium, high-dose vitamin C, glutathione, NAD+) can produce a warm, flushed, or "heavy" feeling, and **NAD+ in particular can feel intense if run too fast**, so Diana paces it. Nothing here is a treatment for a condition — just sensations to expect.

**Downtime.** None to speak of — no recovery window. Mild soreness or a small bruise at the IV site is possible.

**Results timeline & cadence.** Many clients notice feeling refreshed and rehydrated within the session or later the same day. Effects are supportive and not permanent; there is **no fixed medical cadence** — clients use IVs situationally (before/after an event, travel, heavy activity, seasonal stretches) or as a **routine wellness habit** (see §13). Cadence is a wellness choice made with Diana, not a prescription.

**Aftercare.** Keep the bandage on briefly, hydrate normally, resume activity. No special restrictions.

## 5. Comparisons (honest)

**IV vs. oral hydration & supplements — and when oral is genuinely enough.**
- **Bioavailability & speed:** IV bypasses the gut and liver first-pass, so it delivers near-complete absorption immediately; oral absorption is variable (gut health, age, interactions, GI issues cap it, and water-soluble vitamin excess is simply excreted). For rapid, complete replenishment, IV wins on mechanism.
- **When oral is enough (say this plainly):** for a healthy, well-nourished person with normal digestion, **drinking water/electrolytes and eating well covers day-to-day needs** — an IV is a convenience and an occasional boost, not a daily necessity. Oral is cheaper, needle-free, and appropriate for routine maintenance. IV earns its place when you want speed, completeness, the bypass route, or the RN-monitored experience — not as a replacement for basic hydration and diet.
- Bottom line for the page: *"If a glass of water and a good meal would do it, that's the right call. IV therapy is for when you want a faster, more complete reset — under an RN's care."*

## 9. FAQ bank

1. **What is IV hydration therapy?** It's an RN-administered infusion of fluids, electrolytes, and vitamins delivered directly into a vein by Diana, RN, under medical-director oversight, to help replenish hydration and support overall wellness. It is not a treatment for any medical condition.
