## 2. How it works

**Plain English.** GLP-1 medications are weekly injectable medications that work with your body's
own appetite-regulating system. They mimic a natural gut hormone (GLP-1) your body already makes
after eating. The result is that many people feel full sooner, stay full longer, and think about
food less — so it becomes easier to eat less without the constant hunger and "food noise" that
makes weight loss hard to sustain. Because appetite is regulated rather than white-knuckled, the
medication supports a slower, steadier, more maintainable pattern of eating.

**One level deeper.** GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) is an incretin hormone. GLP-1 receptor
agonists like semaglutide act through two coordinated pathways:

- **Central (brain).** They stimulate GLP-1 receptors in the hypothalamus and brainstem, activating
  appetite-suppressing (anorexigenic) neurons and quieting appetite-stimulating (orexigenic)
  neurons — reducing hunger and increasing satiety.
- **Peripheral (gut).** They **slow gastric emptying**, so the stomach stays fuller longer and
  digestion is prolonged, promoting earlier satiety. They also enhance glucose-dependent insulin
  secretion and suppress glucagon, which is why the same molecules are used in type-2-diabetes care.

**Semaglutide vs tirzepatide — the mechanistic difference.** Semaglutide is a **single-agonist**
(GLP-1 only). **Tirzepatide is a dual agonist** — it activates **both the GLP-1 receptor and the
GIP receptor** (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide). The added GIP activity contributes
to insulin sensitivity, adipose-tissue function, and a strong combined appetite-reducing signal in
the brain — and, in preclinical work, GIP agonism appears to have an anti-nausea (antiemetic)
effect that may soften some GI side effects. This dual mechanism is why tirzepatide is positioned as
a "more comprehensive metabolic approach" in our menu — **it is a mechanism difference, not a
promise of superior results for any individual.** Which medication is right for a given client is a
clinical decision made by the provider.

## 3. Who it's for / not for

**Who it may be for (candidacy is set by the provider, not the website).** Adults seeking a
medically supervised path to weight management who meet the provider's clinical criteria. In the
FDA-approved weight-management context, the medications' labeling describes adults with **obesity
(BMI ≥ 30)** or **overweight (BMI ≥ 27) with at least one weight-related condition** (such as high
blood pressure, high cholesterol, or type 2 diabetes). **These are label reference points, not a
House of Rose eligibility guarantee** — the provider makes the final candidacy determination through
screening, health history, and (as clinically indicated) labs.

**Who it is NOT for — real contraindications (defer specifics to the provider).**

- **Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)**, or **Multiple Endocrine
  Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2)** — a boxed/contraindicated warning on these medications.
- **Known hypersensitivity** to the medication or its components.
- **Pregnancy, planning pregnancy, or breastfeeding** — these medications are not used in pregnancy.
- **A history of pancreatitis** (acute or chronic) warrants caution and provider review.
- Other conditions the provider screens for: history of gallbladder disease, severe GI disease /
  gastroparesis, diabetic retinopathy, certain medication interactions.

**Honest limits.**

- This is **not** an overnight or guaranteed result. It is an **ongoing program** — the medication
  supports appetite regulation while you build sustainable habits; it is not a standalone cure.
- Results are **individual** and depend on the person, the plan, adherence, and lifestyle. We make
  no pounds-lost promises.
- **The provider may decline a candidate** if it is not clinically appropriate. "Not every client is
  a candidate" is a real statement here, not boilerplate.
- Weight regain is possible if the medication is stopped; the program includes provider guidance on
  what continuation, maintenance, or tapering looks like.

## 4. The visit (how the supervised program works)

The value of doing this with House of Rose is the **structure and oversight** — screening, guided
dose titration, and regular check-ins — versus an unsupervised online source.

1. **Consultation & screening.** Health history, goals, medication review, and candidacy assessment
   with Diana under physician oversight. Contraindications are screened here; labs are ordered when
   clinically indicated (this is a clinical decision, not a fixed menu item).
2. **Program start & titration.** GLP-1 medications are started at a **low dose and increased
   gradually** ("start low, go slow"). Slow titration is the single most important lever for keeping
   side effects tolerable — it lets the body adjust and lets GI symptoms settle before stepping up.
   In clinical practice a client may stay at a given dose for several weeks as needed.
3. **Monthly dosing & check-ins.** The menu lists a **~15-minute monthly visit**. Each check-in
   reviews tolerance, side effects, progress, and whether to hold or advance the dose. This cadence
   is where supervision earns its keep.
4. **Lifestyle support alongside the medication.** GLP-1 therapy works best paired with adequate
   **protein intake, hydration, and resistance/strength activity to protect lean muscle**, plus
   smaller, more frequent, lower-fat meals early on to reduce nausea. (Program-level nutrition
   guidance; not medical nutrition therapy claims.)

**Sensation / administration.** A small weekly subcutaneous self-injection (fine, short needle) —
most people describe it as minor. The provider trains you on technique and rotation.

**Side effects (set expectations honestly).** The most common are **gastrointestinal — nausea,
vomiting, diarrhea, constipation** — affecting a large share of people (roughly half) during the
early/dose-escalation phase and generally easing over time. Nausea often shows up in the morning or
after long gaps without food. Mitigation: slower titration, smaller/more-frequent meals, avoiding
very fatty or high-fiber foods in the first days, hydration. Clients are told to contact the
provider early if side effects develop. Serious but uncommon risks (e.g. pancreatitis, gallbladder
issues) are part of the provider's counseling and monitoring — defer specifics to the provider.

**Results timeline.** Appetite and eating changes are often noticed within the first weeks; weight
change is gradual over months as the dose is titrated up. **We do not quote pounds or timelines as
promises** — the provider frames realistic expectations for each individual.

**Cadence.** Ongoing program with monthly visits; the provider sets the plan for maintenance,
continuation, or tapering over time.

| | **Semaglutide** (menu: from $225/mo) | **Tirzepatide** (menu: from $325/mo) |

| **Mechanism** | Single agonist — **GLP-1 only** | **Dual agonist — GLP-1 + GIP** |
| **Positioning** | Established, single-pathway appetite regulation | "More comprehensive metabolic approach" — added GIP activity |
| **Active-ingredient FDA context** | Same active ingredient as Wegovy (weight mgmt) / Ozempic (T2D) | Same active ingredient as Zepbound (weight mgmt) / Mounjaro (T2D) |
| **GI tolerability note** | GLP-1 GI side effects are common early | GIP activity may have an anti-nausea effect (mechanistic/preclinical note) |

**When each "wins" — honestly.** There is no universal winner. Tirzepatide's dual mechanism is why
it's positioned for clients seeking a more comprehensive metabolic approach, and head-to-head
research has shown differences at the population level — **but individual response, tolerability,
health history, and cost all factor in, and the provider makes the choice.** We describe mechanism
and positioning; **we never guarantee that one produces more weight loss for a specific person.**

### Medically supervised program vs unsupervised online/telehealth sources

This is the honest, compliance-clean case for doing it with House of Rose — framed as **safety and
oversight**, not as attacking anyone:

- **Real candidacy screening.** A provider screens for the contraindications above (thyroid/MEN 2
  history, pancreatitis, pregnancy, interactions) before you ever start — the step most likely to be
  skipped when medication is ordered sight-unseen online.
- **Guided titration.** Rushed dose escalation is a known driver of severe GI side effects (and, in
  case reports, gastroparesis). Provider-managed "start low, go slow" titration is safer.
- **Monthly in-person check-ins.** Someone is watching tolerance and progress and can hold, adjust,
  or pause — not a fire-and-forget subscription.
- **Local accountability.** Diana, RN, in Punta Gorda, under physician oversight — a real person you
  can reach if something feels off.

## 9. FAQ bank (answer-first, compliance-clean)

1. **What is House of Rose's GLP-1 program?**
   A medically supervised, physician-guided weight-management program led by Diana, RN, using
   semaglutide or tirzepatide, with monthly dosing and progress check-ins. It is a wellness program
   with real candidacy screening — not an over-the-counter product.

2. **How do GLP-1 medications work?**
   They mimic a natural gut hormone that regulates appetite — helping you feel full sooner and
   longer and reducing food cravings — while also slowing how quickly the stomach empties. The goal
   is to make sustainable, lower-calorie eating easier without constant hunger.

3. **What's the difference between semaglutide and tirzepatide?**
   Semaglutide acts on one appetite pathway (GLP-1). Tirzepatide acts on two (GLP-1 and GIP), which
   is why it's positioned as a more comprehensive metabolic approach. Which one is right for you is a
   clinical decision your provider makes — it isn't a matter of one always being "better."

4. **Are these the same as Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, or Zepbound?**
   They share the same active ingredients. Semaglutide is the active ingredient in Wegovy (weight
   management) and Ozempic (type 2 diabetes); tirzepatide is the active ingredient in Zepbound
   (weight management) and Mounjaro (type 2 diabetes). Your provider will explain exactly what your
   program uses.

5. **How much weight will I lose?**
   We don't promise a number. Results are individual and depend on you, your plan, your adherence,
   and your lifestyle. Your provider will set realistic expectations for your situation.

6. **Am I a candidate?**
   Candidacy is determined by the provider through screening and health history. In the approved
   weight-management setting, the medications are described for adults with a BMI of 30 or higher, or
   27 or higher with a weight-related condition — but the provider makes the final decision, and not
   every client is a candidate.

7. **Who should NOT take these medications?**
   People with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2, anyone who is
   pregnant or planning pregnancy or breastfeeding, and those with certain histories such as
   pancreatitis. Your provider screens for all of this before you start.

8. **What are the side effects?**
   The most common are digestive — nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, or constipation — especially early on
   as the dose is increased, and they usually ease over time. We start low and increase the dose
   gradually specifically to keep side effects manageable, and you're told how to reach us if they
   develop.

9. **How is the medication given, and how often do I come in?**
   It's a small weekly self-injection you're trained to do at home, with a roughly 15-minute
   in-person check-in each month to review tolerance, progress, and dosing.

10. **Why do this here instead of ordering online?**
    Oversight. You get real candidacy screening, provider-guided dose titration (which is the safest
    way to manage side effects), and monthly in-person check-ins with a nurse you can actually reach
    — in Punta Gorda, under physician oversight.

11. **Will this affect my face or skin?**
    Significant weight loss from any cause can reduce facial volume and affect skin firmness — some
    people notice this. If it's a concern for you, it's worth discussing volume-supporting options
    with your provider as part of your plan.

12. **What's included in the monthly program fee?**
    The supervised-program structure — your dosing and monthly check-ins. Any labs (when clinically
    indicated) or additional wellness services are separate. Your provider reviews everything at
    consultation.

13. **What happens if I stop?**
    Appetite effects fade after stopping, and weight regain is possible — which is why the program
    includes provider guidance on maintenance, continuation, or tapering. This is a conversation to
    have with your provider, not a decision to make alone.

14. **Do I still need to change how I eat and exercise?**
    Yes. The medication makes appetite easier to manage, but the best and most durable results come
    when it's paired with adequate protein, hydration, and strength activity to protect muscle. It
    supports your effort — it doesn't replace it.

15. **Is this a membership?**
    No. It's a normal wellness service billed as a monthly program. House of Rose does not offer
    memberships of any kind.
