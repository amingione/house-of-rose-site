# Membership Economics — Structure & Pricing-Model Research

**Date:** 2026-07-13
**Author:** Research agent (Phase-2 pricing-model input)
**Scope note — STRUCTURE ONLY, NO HoR PRICES.** This brief gathers *models, ratios, mechanics, and framing* to inform a House of Rose membership **pricing model**. It deliberately proposes **no dollar figures for House of Rose**. Every market number below is labeled **"industry context, NOT HoR pricing."** HoR's actual monthly fee, credit amounts, and tier prices are **derived separately** from HoR's own service menu (a separate agent builds that catalog). Feed those real numbers into the decision rules in the final section.

**Compliance constraint (binding — see `docs/COMPLIANCE-COPY-RULES.md`).** HoR copy bans "discount / % off / deal / sale / coupon / pressure" framing. This brief therefore **favors the banked-credit / prepaid-investment / founding-member model** and translates every industry mechanic into HoR-safe language. Where the industry uses "10–20% off," we reframe as **bonus credit / member credit**, never a discount.

**Default model already chosen by HoR:** a flat **monthly fee that banks as rollover studio credit** the member spends on any service or retail, plus perks. This brief pressure-tests and details *that* model primarily, and contrasts the alternatives.

## 1. Membership model types

Five structures recur across the aesthetics practice-management literature. For each: how it works, and its fit for a small premium studio.

| Model | How it works | Pros | Cons | Fit for a small premium studio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Banked / rollover credit** ("membership bank," "beauty bank," "treatment credit," "skin banking") | Flat monthly fee posts to the member's account as **spendable credit**, redeemable against *any* service or retail; unused credit rolls forward. | Maximal flexibility (member self-selects); reads as **prepaid investment, not a discount**; drives **retail attach + upsell** (members "spend 140% of their banked credits" per visit — *industry context*); premium-clean framing. | Requires a stored-value ledger; creates a **balance-sheet liability** (unredeemed credit); FL expiry constraints (§3). | **Best fit + HoR default.** Works across four provider lanes without pre-committing a client to one service. Most compliance-clean. |
| **Monthly-service-included** | Fee entitles a **specific service each period** (e.g. one facial/month), often +perks. | Easiest to *sell* — the client knows exactly what they get; predictable provider scheduling. | Rigid; wrong for a multi-lane studio (which service?); "use it or lose it" reads punitive; underuses HoR's menu breadth. | Weak fit as the *core* model for a 4-lane studio; can appear as a **tier variant** ("banked credit sized to one signature service"). |
| **Percentage-discount club** ("member rate," "10–20% off") | Fee unlocks a standing **% off** menu prices. | Simple; common. | **Directly collides with HoR compliance** ("% off / deal / discount" banned); erodes premium positioning ("a destination, never a deal"); margin bleeds on every visit. | **Avoid.** This is the framing HoR's rules exist to prevent. Reframe any "member value" as *bonus credit*, not a rate cut. |
| **Points / rewards** | Spend earns points redeemable later. | Gamifies repeat spend. | Adds a second currency to explain; "Rose Rewards" concept was explicitly killed at HoR; breakage-driven, can feel like a "trick." | **Avoid as core.** Redundant with banked credit (credit already rewards spend, in dollars, transparently). |
| **Prepaid packages** (series of 3 / 6) | One-time upfront purchase of a fixed treatment series. | **Cash-flow spike at purchase**; great for a defined protocol (series-of-3/6). | One-and-done, not recurring; no MRR; not a membership. | **Complementary, not a substitute.** Industry guidance: *packages for acquisition, memberships for retention* — offer both. HoR already has series-of-3/6 packages; membership sits alongside them. |

**Which maximizes recurring revenue + retention while staying premium:** the **banked-credit model.** It produces true MRR, self-serves across lanes, drives add-on/retail spend, and — critically for HoR — is expressible entirely as *investment / member credit* with **zero discount language**. This confirms HoR's default choice.

> **Reframing table (industry term → HoR-safe term):** "% off / member rate" → **"member credit / bonus credit"**; "discount" → **"the value that banks each month"**; "savings" → **"credit you can spend on anything"**; "deal / promo" → **"founding-member access."**

## 2. How the monthly price relates to service value

The unit-economics logic (synthesized from MyTime, DaySpark, Pabau, Nextech, Prospyr — all *industry context*):

**A. Start from margin, not the competitor menu.** "Membership pricing should begin with unit economics, not competitor menus." The monthly fee must clear the **cost to deliver** the value it banks (provider time, product/consumables, room/device capacity, payment-processing fees, expected redemption rate, no-show risk) **and leave margin**. A membership that "feels easy to sell can still drain profit if included perks are too rich."

**B. Anchor the fee to one signature service per period.** The most legible anchor in the literature: size the monthly fee so the banked credit ≈ **one signature/"best-seller" treatment per month** (or a natural cadence for that service). This gives the member an obvious mental model ("my membership = my monthly facial, bankable") and gives HoR a clean derivation rule once the real menu price is known.

**C. Credit = fee, vs credit < fee + breakage — the core lever.** Two consumer-facing constructions:
- **Credit = fee (1:1).** Every dollar paid becomes a dollar of credit. Cleanest, most trusted, most premium; some clinics run exactly this ("monthly fee converts directly to service credits, 1:1" — *industry context*). Margin comes from the **spread between menu price and cost-to-deliver**, plus retail attach and breakage.
- **Credit < fee (breakage-driven).** Bank less than paid. Higher margin but reads as taking value; **collides with HoR's premium/consumer-friendly stance.** Not recommended for HoR.
- **Credit > fee (bonus credit — the join incentive).** Bank **more** than paid (e.g. pay X, receive X + a modest bonus). This is the recommended **"why join"** lever *instead of a % discount*: it shifts psychology from "sale" to "reward," and is fully HoR-compliant ("bonus member credit," never "10% off"). DaySpark's illustrative construction: "$199/month → $220 in account credit (~10% bonus)" — *industry context, NOT HoR pricing;* the **mechanic** (small bonus credit as the incentive) is what transfers, not the numbers.

**D. Minimum perceived value.** Industry rule of thumb: members who actively use benefits should see **at least ~10% more value** than pay-as-you-go — *delivered at HoR as bonus credit + perks, never as a discounted rate.*

**Decision framework (not HoR numbers):**
1. Pick the **signature service** each tier anchors to (from HoR's real menu).
2. Compute **cost-to-deliver** that value (provider time + product + room + processing).
3. Set the **monthly fee ≥ cost-to-deliver + target margin**, landing near one signature service's menu price.
4. Choose the **join incentive = modest bonus credit** (credit slightly > fee), sized so blended margin (after bonus, expected redemption, retail attach, and breakage) still clears target.
5. Never express any of it as "% off."

## 3. Rollover / breakage / expiry mechanics — and the Florida constraint

**Accrual & rollover (consumer-friendly is the premium play).** Best-practice for a premium, trust-first brand is **rollover, not "use it or lose it."** Aggressive expiry "can hurt motivation and engagement… and limit success long-term." A **rolling-inactivity** approach (credit stays alive as long as the membership is active) preserves trust and still nudges maintenance visits. Industry pattern where any limit exists: credit rolls **1–2 months** *or* per stated policy — but a hard short expiry is exactly the "trick" feeling to avoid.

**Breakage (real, but don't engineer it).** Breakage = value paid but never redeemed. Industry context: **~28% of collected membership fees go unredeemed**, and members **spend ~140% of banked credits** per visit (i.e. redemption drives *additional* spend). Breakage is a genuine margin contributor, but **CFO-style "engineered breakage" (short expiry / high thresholds) is off-brand for HoR** and — for paid-for credit in Florida — likely **unlawful** (below). Treat breakage as an incidental tailwind, not a design goal.

**Florida stored-value / gift-card law — MATERIAL, confirm with counsel.**
> **⚠️ Flag for counsel — do not treat as a legal ruling.** Under **Fla. Stat. § 501.95**, a "gift certificate" is defined broadly as *"a certificate, gift card, **stored value card**, or similar instrument **purchased for monetary consideration** … redeemable for merchandise, food, or services,"* and such an instrument **"may not have an expiration date, expiration period, or any type of postsale charge or fee."** Membership credit that a member **pays cash for each month** looks a lot like "stored value purchased for monetary consideration" — which would mean **it may not lawfully expire** and **no post-sale fees** may be charged on it.
> The statute's expiry **exceptions** are narrow and mostly *don't* apply to paid credit: they cover credit given as a **loyalty/promotional benefit where the recipient pays no separate identifiable charge**, charitable gifts (≥3 yr), employee-incentive (≥1 yr), and event-linked value. **Bonus credit** HoR *grants* (member pays nothing separate for it) may fall under the loyalty/promotional exception and *could* carry an expiry; **credit the member paid for almost certainly cannot.** This distinction — *paid credit = no expiry; granted bonus credit = possibly expirable* — is the key design seam, **to be confirmed with Florida counsel** before any expiry rule ships. Practically, a **no-expiry-on-paid-credit, rollover** design is both the compliant *and* the on-brand choice.

**Liability hygiene.** Non-expiring credit is a standing balance-sheet **liability**. Track **unused-value / credit-utilization** as a core KPI; the offset is that active members redeem *and* over-spend their balances (the 140% stat), converting liability into visits and retail.

## 4. Perks that drive joins without discounting

Perks do the "why join" work that HoR forbids a discount from doing. The literature is consistent: **"perks that feel special — priority booking or member events — instead of cutting prices"** carry **disproportionate perceived value at near-zero operational cost.**

| Perk | Relative COST to studio | Relative perceived VALUE | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Priority booking** (member-first slots, top-provider access) | ~zero | High | Highest value-to-cost ratio; pure scheduling policy. Ideal HoR headline perk. |
| **Member events / sneak-peeks / VIP nights** | Low–moderate | High (experiential) | Reinforces "destination, not a deal"; community + retention. |
| **Guest pass** | Low | Medium–high | Built-in referral engine; brings prospects in the door. |
| **Birthday gift** (small credit or complimentary add-on) | Modest | High (loyalty) | Strong loyalty impact per dollar; frame as a *gift*, not a coupon. |
| **Complimentary add-on / service enhancement** | Low–moderate | Medium–high | Uses spare provider time / low-cost enhancement to lift the visit. |
| **Bonus member credit** (the join incentive from §2) | = the bonus amount | High | The compliant substitute for "% off." Direct, dollar-denominated, transparent. |
| **Retail benefit** | Margin-dependent | Medium | Frame as *bonus credit applies to retail*, **not** "X% off products." |
| **Member-only access** (new services first, limited protocols) | ~zero | Medium–high | Exclusivity without price. |

**Design rule:** lead the perk stack with **near-zero-cost, high-perception** benefits (priority booking, events, guest pass, birthday), and let **bonus credit** be the one economic incentive — because it's the only one HoR can express without touching discount language.

## 5. Tier design

**How many tiers.** The literature's default is **three tiers** (entry / core-regular / premium-exclusive), with the **mid tier deliberately anchored** as "the balanced choice" most members pick. A **single tier** is recommended for *smaller or niche* studios / launch simplicity ("maybe you'll start with a single plan… eventually clients want more"). Some practices add a 4th premium level, but three is the standard ceiling. *(Illustrative industry tier ladders — e.g. $99 / $179–199 / $299 — are **industry context, NOT HoR pricing**; only the **shape** transfers.)*

**Whole-studio vs per-provider-lane.** Two axes for HoR's four lanes (Advanced Facials · Waxing · IV · Injectables):
- **Whole-studio banked credit (recommended default).** One credit currency spendable across *any* lane. Maximizes flexibility, matches the banked-credit model, avoids stranding a member in one lane, and is simplest to explain. The industry mainstream treats memberships as **whole-clinic** offerings, not per-service-line.
- **Per-lane memberships** (a facial membership, a wax membership, etc.). Works when lanes have **distinct client bases and cadences** (HoR's old "Rose Circle" was lane-organized: IV/Diana, facials/Amber, wax/Aundrea). Cost: fragments the base, multiplies admin, and a whole-studio credit already lets a facial-focused member spend where they like. **Use lane tiers only if a lane's cadence genuinely differs** (e.g. wax is high-frequency/low-ticket vs injectables low-frequency/high-ticket).

**Recommended tier shape for HoR (structure, not price):**
- **Launch simple:** start with **one clean banked-credit tier** (or two), then expand — matches "start single, grow to multi-tier" guidance and HoR's coming-soon status.
- If multi-tier: **2–3 whole-studio credit tiers** that differ by (a) the **credit amount banked** (anchored to different signature-service cadences) and (b) the **perk stack** (priority booking / events / guest passes escalating). Keep the **mid tier as the anchor.**
- **Tier differentiation via credit size + perks, never via a bigger "% off"** — the discount axis is unavailable to HoR by rule.

## 6. Retention / LTV / churn economics — why memberships beat one-off booking

All figures **industry context, NOT HoR pricing/targets** — they justify *doing* memberships and set KPI expectations.

- **Predictable revenue.** Memberships can reliably generate **20–30% of a med spa's total revenue**, converting volatile walk-in income into forecastable MRR (enables staffing + capital planning).
- **Higher LTV.** Membership clients average **~$5,166 LTV vs ~$1,495** for transaction clients (**~3.5×**). ~$1,100 additional annual spend per member is cited elsewhere.
- **Higher visit frequency + spend.** Members visit **~2.9× more often** and spend **~35% more** than non-members.
- **Stickiness.** Members are **~78% less likely to switch providers**; a **5% retention lift can raise LTV 25–30%.**
- **Redemption behavior.** **~72% service-utilization** on service-based memberships; members **spend ~140% of banked credit** per visit (upsell/retail); **~28% breakage** on collected fees.
- **Market adoption.** ~**85% of US med spas** now offer some membership/subscription; **~73% of med-spa patients are repeat clients** (up from 65% in 2022).

**Commitment terms & churn norms.**
- **Healthy monthly churn ≈ 3–5%;** **>7%** signals a value/engagement problem. Target **retention 70–90%.**
- **Month-to-month is the mainstream default** (low friction, automated card-on-file billing). A common on-ramp pattern: a short **initial term (e.g. 6-month)** then convert to month-to-month, sometimes with a small one-time enrollment fee — *industry context.*
- **Annual pre-pay** is offered as an *option* for cash-flow, sweetened with a **small perk (one or two free months), never a deep discount** — which fits HoR (perk/bonus-credit framing, not "% off").
- Publish clear **pause / cancel / upgrade / downgrade** policies; automated billing + failed-charge handling is table stakes.

## 7. Recurring-billing mechanics

- **Recurring dues need a subscription rail — HoR's retail checkout can't do it.** HoR retail runs on **one-time Stripe Elements** (`docs/CHECKOUT.md`) — single PaymentIntents, not recurring. Monthly membership dues require **recurring billing**: either **Stripe Billing / Subscriptions** (card-on-file, scheduled charges, dunning/failed-charge retries, proration on up/downgrade) **or** the **booking-system's native membership feature.**
- **GlossGenius** (HoR's booking + in-person POS) **has native memberships/packages**: sell "prepaid packages and recurring memberships in person or online," with **automated redemptions and renewals**, **customizable weekly/monthly billing**, card-on-file, and automated billing "on autopilot." Notably, **GlossGenius itself is built on Stripe Billing + Stripe Terminal** under the hood. Plans start ~**$24/mo**; processing is a flat **~2.6%** — *industry context.*
  - **Decision seam:** if GlossGenius memberships handle the credit-ledger + auto-renew adequately, HoR can run memberships **inside GlossGenius** (keeps booking, POS, redemption, and dues in one system) — distinct from HoR's **Stripe Elements retail** rail. If HoR wants membership dues on its **own** rail (deeper control of the banked-credit ledger, web "join" button, unified customer record), stand up **Stripe Subscriptions** separately. Either way, **memberships are NOT the one-time retail checkout** — that's the load-bearing architectural line.
- **Ledger requirement.** The banked-credit model needs a **stored-value ledger** (accrue monthly, decrement on redemption, roll forward, honor no-expiry-on-paid-credit). Whichever rail holds dues, decide **where the credit balance lives** (GlossGenius account credit vs a HoR-owned ledger) — this is the single biggest implementation choice under the model.

## 8. Compliance-clean language (sells recurring value without discount/pressure)

Per `docs/COMPLIANCE-COPY-RULES.md`, membership copy must use **prepaid-investment / banked-credit** framing and **never** "deal / % off / discount / sale / coupon" or pressure ("lock in before it's gone," "you can't say no").

**Say:** "member credit," "banked studio credit," "your monthly investment, spendable on anything," "bonus member credit," "founding-member access," "priority access / priority booking," "member events," "rolls over — yours to keep," "curated," "an investment in your skin, not a punch card."

**Never:** "10% off," "member discount / member rate," "save $X," "deal," "sale," "promo price," "limited time — act now," "cancel anytime or lose your spot."

**Also carry the standing guardrails** (unchanged): provider-authority ("tiers/candidacy confirmed by your licensed provider"), candidacy ("not every client needs a membership"), individual outcomes ("outcomes are individual"), and a clear plain-language **cancel/pause** policy stated as *transparency*, not pressure.

## Recommended model & pricing FRAMEWORK for House of Rose (numbers derived separately)

**Model: keep the default — a single-currency, whole-studio, banked-rollover-credit membership + perks.** It's the only model that produces real MRR *and* survives HoR's discount ban intact, and it fits four provider lanes without stranding a member in one.

**Decision rules the synthesis step plugs HoR's real service prices into:**

1. **Anchor each tier to one signature service/period.** From HoR's real menu, pick the signature treatment a tier is built around; the banked monthly credit ≈ that service's menu price (or its natural cadence).
2. **Fee floor = cost-to-deliver + target margin.** Compute provider time + product + room/device + ~2.6% processing for the anchored value; set the monthly fee at/above that, landing near one signature service's price. Never below cost-to-deliver.
3. **Credit is 1:1 with the fee** (every dollar paid banks as a dollar of credit) — cleanest and most premium; margin comes from the menu-vs-cost spread + retail attach + incidental breakage.
4. **Join incentive = modest BONUS credit, not a discount.** Grant a small bonus (credit slightly > fee) as the "why join," sized so blended margin still clears target after expected redemption and retail attach. This is HoR's compliant substitute for "% off." (Confirm bonus-credit expiry treatment with counsel per rule 6.)
5. **Rollover, no expiry on paid credit.** Design credit to **roll forward and not expire** — both the premium/consumer-friendly choice and the likely FL-law requirement (Fla. Stat. § 501.95) for credit paid for with money. **Confirm with Florida counsel** before shipping any expiry; if any expiry is used, restrict it to *granted bonus* credit, not paid credit.
6. **Tiers: launch with 1 (max 2) whole-studio credit tiers; expand to 2–3 later.** Differentiate tiers by **credit amount (anchored to different signature cadences) + escalating perk stack**, with the **mid tier anchored** as the intended default. Add **per-lane** tiers only if a lane's cadence is genuinely distinct (e.g. high-frequency waxing).
7. **Perk stack leads with near-zero-cost/high-perception perks:** priority booking → member events → guest pass → birthday gift → complimentary add-on → member-only access. Bonus credit is the sole economic lever.
8. **Terms: month-to-month default, card-on-file auto-billing;** offer an optional annual pre-pay sweetened with a **small perk** (not a deep discount); publish transparent pause/cancel/upgrade policies.
9. **Billing rail:** run dues on a **subscription rail** — either **GlossGenius native memberships** (if its credit-ledger + auto-renew suffice; keeps booking/POS/redemption unified) or **Stripe Subscriptions** on HoR's own rail — **never** the one-time Stripe Elements retail checkout. Decide **where the credit balance lives** (GlossGenius account credit vs HoR-owned ledger).
10. **KPIs to set targets on** (not to be invented as promises in copy): monthly churn (aim <5%), retention (aim 70–90%), credit-utilization/unused-value, member visit-frequency, member AOV, and retail attach rate.
11. **Copy is investment/credit-only.** All member value is expressed as banked/bonus **credit** and **perks** — never a discount, rate, or "% off." Carry provider-authority + candidacy + individual-outcomes + transparent cancel language.
