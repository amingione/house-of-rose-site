### What neurotoxin does — and how we keep it natural

A neurotoxin (a wrinkle relaxer) softens the expression lines that come from repeated movement — the frown "11s," forehead lines, crow's feet. It works by gently quieting the muscle's signal to contract, so the skin above it creases less. The difference between natural and frozen is entirely in the dosing, and that's where Diana's approach lives: enough to soften, not so much that your face stops being yours. Movement stays. The tiredness reads less.

House of Rose carries two, and Diana will lay out the trade-offs honestly:
- **Botox** — the long-established standard, predictable and well understood.
- **Daxxify** — a newer, peptide-stabilized formula designed to last longer for many people.

Both are **$14 per unit**, priced by the unit rather than by a flat "per area" number — because how much you need depends on your muscles, not a menu. That's a feature: you pay for what your face actually calls for, and Diana estimates it with you at consultation.

### What filler does — and why "movement-preserving" matters

Filler is a smooth hyaluronic-acid gel that restores volume the face loses over time — cheeks, midface, lips — and softens the folds that set in at rest. Hyaluronic acid is something your skin already makes, and the result is visible right away, then gradually and naturally broken down over months.

The natural-results discipline shows up in the *product choice*. Diana reaches for the **RHA Collection** — resilient HA engineered to stretch and flex with your expressions — precisely so a cheek or a smile line doesn't look stiff when you talk and laugh. For structural lift she uses **Juvéderm Voluma XC**; for lips and lower-face softening, **Juvéderm Ultra XC**. And she builds gradually, because — as she puts it — you can always add. Starting measured is how you avoid the overfilled look nobody actually asks for.

### Knowing when an injectable is the wrong tool

Neurotoxin relaxes muscles. Filler restores volume. Neither resurfaces skin or tightens real laxity. If your concern is texture, tone, or crepiness, Diana will send you toward PRF, microneedling, or a peel instead of selling you a syringe that won't do the job. The classic honest plan is "relax and restore" — a little neurotoxin for upper-face movement lines, a little filler for volume — but only the parts you need. The goal is the right result, not the extra sale.

### Who's a candidate — and who isn't

**A good fit if you:**
- are bothered by expression lines or lost volume and want a subtle, natural result
- want a registered-nurse injector who'll dose conservatively and explain the plan
- are comfortable building over a couple of visits rather than chasing one dramatic change

**Not a fit / deferred:**
- **Pregnant or breastfeeding** — we don't perform injectables.
- **Neuromuscular conditions** (such as myasthenia gravis, ALS) — evaluated carefully and often ruled out for neurotoxin.
- Active infection or breakout at the site, known allergy to a product or to lidocaine, bleeding disorders or blood thinners, or a history of keloid scarring — all reviewed first.

Diana confirms candidacy, product, and dose at consultation. Not every client is a candidate for every option, and results are individual.

### Downtime, onset, and how long it lasts

**Neurotoxin:** little to no downtime — most people carry on with their day. Small bumps settle within an hour; occasional light bruising near the eyes. Give it about two weeks to fully set in. Botox typically lasts around three to four months; Daxxify often longer.

**Filler:** some social downtime — possible swelling, tenderness, or bruising for a few days, and lips can swell noticeably for one to three. Volume shows immediately, then settles over about two weeks. HA fillers last months to a couple of years depending on the product and area. A quiet safety point worth knowing: HA filler is reversible — it can be dissolved if you ever want to fine-tune or, rarely, if something needs correcting. That reversibility is one reason a trained RN injector matters.

### Questions people ask

**Will I look frozen?**
Not the way Diana does it — the whole approach is softened movement, not a mask. You keep your expressions; the lines just read quieter.

**How do I choose Botox or Daxxify?**
Mostly it comes down to how long you want it to last and your history with either. Diana walks through it — see our Botox vs. Daxxify comparison for the short version.

**Why isn't filler priced "per area"?**
Because faces aren't uniform. Neurotoxin is per unit and filler is per syringe so you pay for what you actually need — no padded flat rate.

**Is under-eye filler an option here?**
Sometimes — but under the thin under-eye skin, Diana often prefers PRF, which improves the skin without the puffiness heavier filler can cause there. She'll tell you which fits.
