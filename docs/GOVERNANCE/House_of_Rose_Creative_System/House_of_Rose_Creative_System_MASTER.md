# 2. House of Rose Creative Bible / Brand Foundation

> **THE STANDARD:** House of Rose should look considered, sound clinically grounded, and explain how care is delivered. The brand does not rely on spa sentiment, beauty clichés, or exaggerated outcomes to create desire.

## 1. Brand in one sentence

**House of Rose Aesthetics is a medical aesthetics practice in Punta Gorda, Florida, providing individualized facial, body, skin, injectable, wellness, and maintenance care through consultation, assessment, clinical judgment, and long-term treatment planning.**

This is the master internal definition. Shorter public versions may change the service list, but they must preserve the same identity.

## 2. Positioning

### Category

**Medical Aesthetics Practice**

Use this phrase on the website, in biographies, in advertisements, in staff language, and in AI instructions. “Medical spa” may remain as a Google Business Profile category when it is the closest available platform label. It is not the preferred public-facing brand description.

### Positioning statement

House of Rose is for people who want aesthetic and wellness services approached as medical care: evaluated individually, selected intentionally, explained clearly, and supported with preparation and aftercare. The practice combines advanced modalities and maintenance care without treating every concern as a quick cosmetic transaction.

### Signature line

**Medical Aesthetics. Thoughtfully Practiced.**

Use the signature line selectively on brand introductions, campaign covers, and high-level pages. It should not appear under every logo or at the end of every post.

### Brand promise

**Recommendations are based on the individual, the treatment indication, and the long-term plan - not on a trend, a template, or pressure to purchase.**

## 3. Why House of Rose exists

House of Rose exists to provide medical aesthetic care that is both technically considered and personally accountable. Some procedures require preparation, temporary downtime, strict aftercare, and continued compliance. The practice earns trust by setting those expectations before treatment, not by minimizing them in marketing.

The work is not positioned as pampering. A customized facial may be part of maintenance care, while a more intensive procedure may require a clinical protocol and recovery plan. The brand must be capable of communicating both without collapsing them into generic spa language.

## 4. Mission

To help patients make informed aesthetic and wellness decisions through individualized assessment, clinically appropriate treatment planning, clear education, and responsible follow-through.

## 5. Vision

To become Southwest Florida’s most trusted independent medical aesthetics practice for people who value specificity, natural-looking outcomes, and a long-term relationship with their care team.

## 6. Values in practice

### Clinical judgment

Treatments are selected because they fit the person, indication, timing, health information, and goals. Devices, products, and trends do not dictate the plan.

### Individualized planning

The practice evaluates the patient before defining the protocol. “Customized” is not decorative language; it should describe a real decision-making process.

### Informed participation

Preparation, consent, downtime, aftercare, maintenance, and realistic limitations are part of the service. Patient compliance is treated as part of the outcome, not as fine print.

### Natural-looking results

The goal is not to make every face or body conform to a template. The work should preserve identity, proportion, expression, and individual character.

### Long-term thinking

House of Rose plans across prevention, correction, restoration, and maintenance. One appointment can be useful, but the practice is built for continuity.

### Honest communication

The brand states what a treatment is designed to do, what it cannot promise, and what the patient must do before and after. It avoids guarantees and manufactured urgency.

### Documented standards

Before-and-after photography, treatment records, consent, product selection, clinical review, and marketing approval should follow repeatable standards.

## 7. The patient experience

The physical environment may feel residential, collected, and quietly refined. The service experience should feel organized, attentive, and unhurried. Public copy, however, should not lead with “warm,” “welcoming,” “luxurious,” or “relaxing.” Those qualities should be demonstrated by the space and the behavior of the team.

### What the brand should make people think

- They will evaluate me before recommending a procedure.
- They will explain what I need to do before and after.
- They are prepared to say when a treatment is not appropriate.
- They care about the plan, not only today’s appointment.
- They want results that still look like me.
- This is a real practice with consistent standards.

### What the brand should not make people think

- This is a day spa with medical equipment.
- Every service is easy, relaxing, or free of downtime.
- The main goal is to sell youth, perfection, or status.
- The practice follows every trend or promotion.
- The same protocol is sold to everyone.

## 8. Primary audiences

### The informed planner

Researches procedures, wants to understand preparation and recovery, and values a practitioner who can build a sequence rather than sell a single service.

### The natural-results patient

Wants improvement without looking standardized, overtreated, or unlike themselves. Responds to conservative language and honest documentation.

### The corrective skin patient

May be managing texture, tone, scarring, laxity, or accumulated damage. Needs clear expectations about treatment series, downtime, home care, and maintenance.

### The maintenance patient

Values consistent facials, skincare, injectables, or supportive treatments as part of a planned routine. Should not be spoken to as less “medical” simply because the service has limited downtime.

### The wellness and hydration patient

Is interested in IV hydration or related services and needs accurate, non-exaggerated education about the service, screening, formulation, and limitations.

## 9. Brand personality

The House of Rose personality is:

- Precise, not severe
- Refined, not decorative
- Calm, not vague
- Direct, not cold
- Educated, not academic
- Personal, not familiar
- Confident, not promotional
- Contemporary, not trendy
- Accountable, not aspirational

## 10. Brand pillars

### Assessment before recommendation

Show consultation, imaging, evaluation, and discussion. Do not imply that a device or injectable is automatically appropriate because someone clicked an ad.

### Protocol over promotion

Explain treatment selection, preparation, series, recovery, aftercare, and maintenance. Avoid advertising that reduces a medical procedure to a price tile.

### Natural-looking outcomes

Use factual, restrained language. Show honest results without retouching, theatrical lighting changes, or expressions that manufacture improvement.

### A practice built for continuity

Connect corrective procedures to maintenance, skincare, and reassessment. The content system should show a relationship over time.

### The real House of Rose

Use the actual space, staff, equipment, products, and process whenever possible. The practice already has a distinctive environment; generic stock and invented AI scenes weaken it.

# 3. Brand Voice & Copy Standards / Writing Rules, Claims Discipline & Copy Application

> **VOICE RULE:** Describe what House of Rose evaluates, decides, explains, documents, and follows. Do not substitute atmosphere or beauty language for proof of practice.

## 1. Voice profile

House of Rose writes like a capable medical aesthetics practice with time to explain its reasoning. The tone is clear, restrained, specific, and human. It is not academic, intimidating, cute, breathless, or overly polished.

### The voice is

- **Specific:** names the process, service, decision, preparation, or limitation.
- **Clinically grounded:** distinguishes marketing language from medical information.
- **Individualized:** avoids implying that everyone receives the same protocol.
- **Direct:** uses short sentences and plain language.
- **Measured:** avoids guarantees, superlatives, and exaggerated urgency.
- **Accountable:** includes what the practice does and what the patient must do.
- **Respectful:** never assumes a reader is ashamed of their appearance.

### The voice is not

- Spa-like or pampering
- Influencer-led
- Vague “wellness” language
- Beauty-industry aspiration
- Overly clinical or impersonal
- Aggressive, fear-based, or shaming
- Search-engine copy that repeats the same phrase unnaturally

## 2. Writing principles

### Lead with the decision process

Prefer:

“Medical procedures begin with a consultation and assessment appropriate to the treatment.”

Avoid:

“Begin your journey to a more radiant you.”

### Explain the protocol, not only the device

Prefer:

“The treatment plan may include a series, home care, recovery instructions, and reassessment.”

Avoid:

“Our advanced technology transforms your skin.”

### State preparation and aftercare when they matter

Some House of Rose procedures require pre-procedure compliance, temporary downtime, and structured aftercare. Include these facts early enough to inform the decision.

Prefer:

“Your consultation includes a review of preparation, expected downtime, aftercare, and the timing of follow-up.”

Avoid:

“Get back to your day with no interruption.”

### Make the outcome proportional to the evidence

Prefer:

“Designed to improve the appearance of texture and support collagen remodeling over time.”

Avoid:

“Erases texture and rebuilds collagen.”

### Use medical terms only when they improve understanding

Define technical terms in plain language. Do not use jargon as a status signal.

### Write to one reader without diagnosing them

It is acceptable to use “you” for instructions and choices. Do not imply that the platform or practice knows the reader has a condition, insecurity, symptom, or protected personal attribute.

Prefer:

“For people considering treatment for uneven texture, a consultation can clarify the available options.”

Avoid:

“Tired of your damaged, aging skin?”

## 3. Brand sentence architecture

Most House of Rose copy should move through four parts:

1. **What it is:** Identify the service, concern, or decision.
2. **How it is approached:** Consultation, assessment, treatment selection, or protocol.
3. **What participation involves:** Preparation, series, downtime, aftercare, or maintenance.
4. **What to do next:** Learn, schedule, call, visit, or shop.

### Example

“Radiofrequency microneedling is used to address selected texture and firmness concerns. At House of Rose, candidacy and settings are determined after consultation and assessment. The plan may involve preparation, a treatment series, temporary downtime, and prescribed aftercare. Schedule a consultation to review whether the procedure fits your goals and timing.”

## 5. Terms that require precision

### Patient, client, guest, and customer

- Use **patient** for medical consultation, procedures, injectables, IV hydration, clinical documentation, consent, aftercare, and follow-up.
- Use **customer** for retail purchase and pickup.
- Use **client** only for clearly non-medical beauty services if that is how the practice documents them.
- Do not use **guest** as the default. It shifts the practice toward hospitality language.
- Use **you** when it improves readability and does not imply a diagnosis.

### Medical aesthetics practice and medical spa

- Use **Medical Aesthetics Practice** in controlled copy.
- Use **Medical spa** only when naming Google’s platform category, discussing common search language, or explaining the market.

### Skin rejuvenation

Use as a category or search phrase, not as filler. One mention per short bio or main page section is usually enough.

### Evidence-based

Use only when the treatment approach or claim can be supported. “Evidence-informed clinical planning” may be more accurate when the evidence is evolving or applied alongside practitioner judgment.

### Regenerative

Name the exact therapy and approved purpose when possible. Do not imply that an internal branded protocol is an FDA-recognized product or that it regenerates tissue beyond the supported evidence.

## 6. Claim discipline

### Level 1 - Descriptive claims

Usually acceptable after a basic fact check; no fresh clinical approval is needed when approved language is reused unchanged:

- What the practice offers
- Where it is located
- How consultation and planning work
- Current hours, access, pickup, payment, and availability when directly relevant
- What device or product is used, when factually accurate
- Approved preparation or aftercare language reproduced without material changes

### Level 2 - Intended-use and appearance claims

Require clinical review and exact wording when newly introduced or materially changed. This level also includes new or changed candidacy, contraindication, preparation, recovery, downtime, aftercare, and treatment-combination guidance:

- Designed to improve the appearance of texture
- Used to address selected pigmentation concerns
- Supports collagen remodeling
- May improve the appearance of fine lines
- Intended to restore hydration
- Results develop over time

### Level 3 - Performance, safety, or comparative claims

Require documented substantiation, clinical approval, and often legal review:

- Clinically proven
- Safer, better, longer-lasting, or more effective
- No downtime
- Pain-free
- Immediate results
- Treats a named medical condition
- Prevents disease, dehydration, aging, or damage
- Works for everyone

### Prohibited house claims

Do not publish:

- Guaranteed results
- Permanent wrinkle removal
- Reverse aging
- Cure, heal, or eliminate a condition without appropriate authorization and evidence
- Risk-free
- “FDA approved” when the claim applies only to a component, device clearance, or indication that does not match the advertised use
- Testimonials or patient stories that were invented, compensated without disclosure, or edited to change meaning

> **CLINICAL REVIEW TRIGGER:** If a sentence answers “what will this do to the body?” or “how safe/effective is this?” it requires a clinical fact check before publication.

## 7. House style

### Sentence length

Average 12 to 20 words. Use a short sentence after a technical one.

### Paragraph length

One to three sentences for social and landing pages. Two to four sentences for patient education.

### Punctuation

- Use periods more than exclamation points.
- Use no more than one exclamation point in a full campaign, and preferably none.
- Use em dashes sparingly. A comma, colon, or new sentence is usually cleaner.
- Avoid repeated ellipses, all caps, and decorative symbols.

### Capitalization

- Medical Aesthetics Practice may be capitalized when used as a formal category line.
- Service names use title case only in headings or menus.
- Do not capitalize common terms such as consultation, skin analysis, facial, treatment plan, or aftercare in body copy.

### Numbers

- Use numerals for treatment intervals, days, weeks, units, percentages, and prices.
- Do not state a typical interval as universal.
- Add qualifiers such as “when appropriate,” “may,” “varies,” or “as directed” only where they are genuinely needed.

### Emoji

Avoid in website, Google Business Profile, clinical education, and Google Ads. Use sparingly on Instagram or Facebook, never as substitutes for clinical meaning. No sparkles.

## 11. Writing about downtime and compliance

Downtime is not a negative word. Avoid euphemisms that leave patients unprepared.

Include:

- What the patient may see or feel
- The approximate range, when clinically supportable
- Activities or products to avoid
- When the practice should be contacted

# 5. Visual Standards / Photography, Video & Design Direction

House of Rose already has a distinctive physical environment. The visual system should document and extend that world instead of replacing it with generic med-spa imagery.

The practice should appear:

- Architectural rather than decorative
- Collected rather than themed
- Residential in materiality, not casual in operation

# 7. Marketing System / Meta, Google, SEO, Landing Pages, and Measurement

> **MARKETING RULE:** Match the campaign to the service, the search intent, and the next measurable action. Do not use one generic ad or landing page to sell the entire practice.

## 1. Marketing strategy

House of Rose should market the practice in three layers:

1. **Identity:** What House of Rose is and how it operates
2. **Service intent:** What the person is actively researching
3. **Clinical expectation:** What assessment, preparation, downtime, aftercare, or maintenance may involve

The environment creates attention. The process creates trust. The service creates relevance. The next step creates measurable action.

Use:

- Real providers and verified credentials
- Real practice and device photography
- Standardized, consented results
- Specific process
- Clear policies
- Approved reviews with no altered meaning

Do not use:

- Fake review tiles
- AI patients
- stock “doctor” imagery
- inflated procedure counts
- unverified awards
- countdown timers without a real deadline
- hidden qualifications

### Forms

Collect the minimum information needed for the next step. Do not ask for diagnoses, medications, detailed medical history, or treatment candidacy in a general advertising lead form unless the workflow, privacy controls, and clinical need have been approved.

- Retail revenue by source

## 9. First 30 days

### Week 1 - Foundation

- Confirm profile facts, categories, hours, pickup, and service links.
- Test booking, call, retail, and form tracking.
- Select one high-intent service campaign plus brand search.
- Finish the matching landing page.
- Build the initial negative list.

### Week 2 - Launch

- Launch brand and one service campaign.
- Launch one Meta practice-introduction creative and one service creative.
- Review ad approval, destination behavior, and search terms.
- Correct tracking before increasing spend.

### Week 3 - Quality

- Listen to or classify qualified calls appropriately.
- Compare bookings with reported platform conversions.
- Add negatives based on irrelevant searches.
- Revise copy that attracts the wrong expectation.
- Add one useful preparation or downtime resource.

### Week 4 - Expand carefully

- Keep the best intent/landing-page match.
- Add a second service only if tracking and follow-up are working.
- Build retargeting or follow-up only after checking sensitive-health and privacy restrictions.
- Report booked and attended value, not vanity metrics.
