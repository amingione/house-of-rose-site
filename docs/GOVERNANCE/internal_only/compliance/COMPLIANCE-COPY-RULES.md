> **This is binding infrastructure.** Every agent or person who writes or edits customer-facing
> copy for House of Rose — website pages, Sanity content, social blurbs, email, ads — MUST follow
> these rules. It is referenced from `AGENTS.md` and sits alongside `docs/SEO-AEO-PLAYBOOK.md`
> (strategy) and `docs/CONTENT-MODEL-MAP.md` (wiring), so it is inherited automatically by any
> content task. This file defines enforceable claim boundaries; it is not a source of hooks,

## 1. Why this exists

House of Rose sells **individual services and treatment packages**. Copy must describe only services,
prices, provider roles, and operational details that are current and verified. _(Memberships are **NOT
OFFERED** — none exist, none are planned, and **no "member rate," "founding-member," or tier framing is
permitted anywhere**; see `CLAUDE.md`. Do not write "deferred" — that wording invites reintroduction. The
retired brand names "Rose Pass" / "The Rose Circle" / "Rose Rewards" / "Rose Method" stay dead.)_
Productizing aesthetics raises the compliance stakes: the more we describe outcomes, sequences, and
"transformation," the easier it is to drift into language Florida regulators, the FTC, and the
brand itself prohibit.

House of Rose leads publicly as a **Medical Aesthetics Practice in Punta Gorda, FL**. Walk-ins may be
available; appointments reserve a time (see `CLAUDE.md`'s binding Walk-In Policy).
_(Two prior fixes to this sentence, do not reintroduce either: it once read "appointment-only," which §2
below bans outright — fixed 2026-07-26; and it once led with "private advanced aesthetics & wellness
studio," superseded by the current category rule. "Advanced aesthetics & wellness" survives as
supporting/SEO phrasing, not as the lead. "Private" is dead — it contradicts the walk-in policy.)_
**"Med spa" / "medical spa" positioning is ON — "allow, don't lead":** the
GBP primary category is `Medical spa`, and the term is permitted in meta, SEO, and supporting copy
where it aids discovery; **we never deny being a med spa.** Just don't make it the lead brand
descriptor, and still avoid "day spa" (undersells).
Regardless of label, copy must **never imply House of Rose diagnoses, treats, or cures any medical
condition.** Advanced/clinical treatments are delivered under the appropriate licensed provider and
medical-director oversight — that authority lives with the *provider*, never with the marketing
copy. When in doubt, defer to the provider and say less. _(This reverses the earlier "never say med
spa" rule; the medical-claims guardrails below are unchanged.)_

These rules are conservative by design. If a phrase is borderline, **don't use it.**

## 2. The claim boundary — what to avoid and how to stay factual

Each row is a hard avoid. The final column describes a safer factual boundary, not approved brand
copy and not a replacement template. Omit the sentence when no specific, supported version exists.

| ❌ Never say | Why it's a problem | Safer factual boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "day spa" (describing House of Rose) | Undersells; implies non-medical relaxation spa | **"Medical Aesthetics Practice"** (the lead descriptor); "advanced aesthetics & wellness" as supporting phrasing |
| "by appointment only," "no walk-ins," "not a walk-in mill/clinic" (any framing that denies walk-ins) | Factually wrong — House of Rose takes walk-ins (waxing/facials always; other services, including injectables, whenever the schedule allows) | In a direct visit-policy answer: walk-ins may be available; appointments reserve a time. Do not make access policy recurring marketing copy. |
| _"med spa" / "medical spa"_ — **allowed, don't lead** | Permitted in meta/GBP/SEO and supporting copy; the GBP category is `Medical spa`. Just don't make it the lead brand descriptor, and never deny being one. | Lead with **"Medical Aesthetics Practice"** under the current reset rules; use "med spa" where it aids discovery. |
| "luxury," "luxe," "premium," "boutique" (describing House of Rose) | Retired positioning — Amber rejected this category language | Use a specific, verifiable fact or omit the descriptor |
| "glow," "radiance," "flawless," "ageless," "timeless beauty," "pamper," "indulge," "treat yourself," "best version of yourself" | Retired brand language — generic aesthetics vocabulary Amber rejected | Name the visible concern, service, or factual result under discussion |
| "pain-free," "no downtime" (as a blanket statement) | Only usable when factually true for that exact procedure **and** clinically reviewed | State only verified, procedure-specific recovery information. |
| "reverse aging," "anti-aging," "turn back the clock," "age-defying" | Implies a result skin biology can't deliver; unprovable claim | Name the visible concern, such as texture or pigment, and state only the supported appearance change. |
| "guaranteed," "guaranteed results," "permanent," "will eliminate / erase / cure" | Outcome guarantee; individual results vary and are never assured | State the supported, treatment-specific outcome and include concise variation language when making a results claim. |
| "stem cell" / "stem-cell therapy" (as a treatment we provide or a claim) | Unapproved/overstated regenerative claim; regulatory minefield | Name the actual service (e.g. "PRF," "Procell microchanneling") and describe it factually |
| "exosome" / "peptide" therapeutic claims that aren't substantiated | Unsupported efficacy claims on emerging actives | Describe the product/ingredient neutrally; make no healing/efficacy promise |
| "treats," "cures," "heals," "fixes" + a condition (acne, rosacea, eczema, scarring, hair loss, disease) | Medical-treatment / disease claim; we do not diagnose or claim disease treatment in marketing copy | Describe only a supported cosmetic appearance concern; omit the disease claim. |
| "Groupon," "deal," "discount," "% off," "sale," "coupon," "lowest price," "cheap" | House of Rose does not use discount framing | State the verified price plainly; **never** use "member rate" / "founding-member" / "member access" (memberships are not offered) |
| "FDA-approved" / "clinically proven" (unless citing a specific, verifiable clearance/study) | Unverifiable or misattributed authority claim | State what the treatment *is* and *does* in plain terms; cite only what's real |
| Uniform before/after promises ("you will look like…") | Implies a guaranteed, universal outcome | "individual results vary," show consented, honest case studies only |

> The avoid-list mirrors LWB-11's brief: reverse-aging, guaranteed results, stem-cell claims,
> cure/treat-disease language, Groupon-style discounting, and unsupported exosome/peptide claims.
> _(Note: "med spa" is no longer on the avoid-list — it's allowed, just not the lead descriptor.)_

## 3. Context-triggered safeguards

Apply each safeguard only when the corresponding public statement appears:

1. **Provider authority.** Marketing must not determine treatment combinations, dosing, intervals, or
   suitability. Name the licensed provider and license type where relevant; use the exact medical-
   director attribution on medically directed services.
2. **Candidacy is real.** State that not every client is a candidate when that fact materially informs
   an advanced treatment or package. Do not repeat it as brand boilerplate on unrelated surfaces.
3. **Individual outcomes.** Every results claim needs a concise individual-variation statement. Case
   studies render only with `consentGiven == true`.
4. **Pricing.** State only a current GlossGenius-backed price or range. Never use financing pressure,
   member pricing, or discount framing.

## 4. Claim-safe constructions (not a voice palette)

> These constructions describe a compliance ceiling, not preferred brand language. Use them only
> when they state something specific and true. Do not mechanically replace one cliché with another.

There is no approved synonym list or sentence formula. Use the narrowest accurate construction needed
for the specific claim. Words such as "designed to," "may help," and "for the appearance of" can limit
an otherwise supportable claim, but they cannot rescue an unsupported one. If a sentence is vague,
promotional, or unsupported, omit it instead of sanitizing it.

## 5. Pre-publish checklist (compliance)

Before publishing or editing any customer-facing copy, confirm:

- [ ] Factual, clinical, visual, and platform preflight has been run; superseded voice criteria are excluded.
- [ ] "Med spa" is used as support, not the lead descriptor (lead = **"Medical Aesthetics Practice"**); no "day spa"; never deny being a med spa.
- [ ] No retired language (luxury/luxe/premium/boutique, glow, radiance, flawless, ageless, pamper, indulge, transformation) under the current reset rules.
- [ ] Controlled-use words (skin rejuvenation, evidence-based, advanced, personalized, customized) appear only where they add meaning, and are not repeated.
- [ ] No guarantees, no "permanent," no "reverse aging" / "anti-aging."
- [ ] No "treats / cures / heals" + a medical condition.
- [ ] No stem-cell / unsupported exosome / unsupported peptide efficacy claims.
- [ ] No Groupon / discount / "% off" language; verified pricing is stated plainly.
- [ ] Provider authority, candidacy, and individual-outcomes language appears where legally or clinically relevant, without becoming repeated brand boilerplate.
- [ ] Before/after only with written consent (`caseStudy.consentGiven == true`).
- [ ] Claims of authority ("FDA-approved," "clinically proven") are removed unless specifically verifiable.
- [ ] Any free, discounted, or credited healthcare-service offer has current approved disclosure copy in every required placement and documented counsel review; no statutory text was improvised.
- [ ] **Provider named with license type** (MD / DO / APRN / RN — never a job title like "Aesthetic Injector") wherever a practitioner is named. § 456.072(1)(t).
- [ ] **No "dermatologist," "skin doctor," or "dermatology specialists" in copy** — implies a specialty not held. R. 64B8-11.001(2)(k).
- [ ] No membership, tier, or "member rate" framing anywhere.

When a phrase is borderline, choose the more conservative wording or omit it. Compliance outranks
copy polish, every time.
