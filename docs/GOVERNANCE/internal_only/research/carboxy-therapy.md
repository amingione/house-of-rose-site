## 1. The 3-sentence answer

A carboxy facial is a needle-free treatment that uses a two-part carbon-dioxide (CO₂) gel or mask, activated on the skin, to support local microcirculation and the skin's oxygen delivery for a fresh, awake, radiant appearance. At House of Rose it's an **advanced facial in Amber's lane** — topical only, no injections — and it's especially popular for the look of tired, dull skin and shadowed under-eyes, with essentially no downtime. It's often used on its own as a "brightening / glow" facial or added onto other treatments to soothe and finish the skin.

---

## 2. How it works

**Plain English.** A two-component gel is mixed for about half a minute and applied to the skin, where it sets and slowly releases carbon dioxide against the surface for roughly 45 minutes, then peels off. Your body reads that pocket of CO₂ as a signal to send more blood and oxygen to the area — so vessels gently widen, microcirculation picks up, and skin looks more perfused, hydrated, and awake afterward.

**One level deeper (the Bohr effect).** The mechanism is the **Bohr effect**, first described by physiologist Christian Bohr in 1904. Hemoglobin binds oxygen less tightly when local CO₂ is high, so raising tissue CO₂ shifts the oxygen–hemoglobin dissociation curve rightward and **releases more O₂ into the surrounding tissue**. Transcutaneous (through-the-skin) CO₂ also drives **vasodilation and increased microcirculation**, measurable by laser Doppler. In published work on the topical gel form, single applications were associated with increased microcirculation and skin hydration, and repeated use with improvements in the appearance of elasticity and periorbital darkness. The topical gel is reported to produce microcirculation and capillary changes *similar in direction* to those seen after CO₂ injection — but achieved **without needles**, because the CO₂ is generated at the skin surface and diffuses transdermally.

**Topical vs. injectable — the real difference (important).** Both share the Bohr-effect rationale, but they are different procedures:
- **Topical carboxy facial (this brief / Amber's lane):** CO₂ is released from a gel/mask **onto** the skin and diffuses through the surface. No needles, no injection, minimal-to-no downtime, esthetician-appropriate.
- **Injectable carboxytherapy (NOT this brief):** CO₂ gas is **injected** subcutaneously or intradermally with a device (e.g., an "MCT injector"). That is a medical injection procedure — different depth of delivery, more direct effect, and a different provider lane (RN). Note that some manufacturer pages label their **injector** device "facial carboxytherapy," so the word "carboxytherapy" alone is ambiguous — always specify **topical** for our offering.

## 3. Who it's for / not for

**Great candidate if you want:**
- The look of **brighter, more awake skin** — dullness, "tired" complexion, sallowness.
- Softer-looking **under-eye shadowing** (especially bluish/vascular-type darkness, which varies day to day).
- A **gentle, no-needle, no-downtime** glow treatment — event prep, sensitive skin, first-timers.
- A **soothing finish** after a more active treatment, or a comfortable option when needling isn't wanted.

**Not the right first choice for:**
- **Deep structural correction** — significant volume loss/hollowing under the eye (that's a filler question, RN lane), deep set acne scarring or stretch-mark texture (microneedling/microchanneling), or advanced laxity. Carboxy supports skin *quality and circulation*, not structure.
- Anyone expecting a single dramatic, permanent change — benefits are cumulative and individual.

**Contraindications / defer to provider (from professional aesthetic literature — final candidacy is confirmed by the provider at consultation):** pregnancy and breastfeeding; active skin infection at the site; and, as a general caution, significant uncontrolled systemic illness (e.g., uncontrolled hypertension, congestive heart failure, severe respiratory/renal/hepatic disease, severe anemia). Any active/inflamed/broken skin at the treatment site is assessed first. **Not every client is a candidate; individual outcomes vary.**

## 4. The visit

**Protocol (topical carboxy facial, typical):**
1. Cleanse and prep the skin; a consult/skin-analysis on the first visit confirms candidacy and goals.
2. Mix the two-part CO₂ gel (~30 seconds) and apply as a mask over the face (and can extend to under-eye area, neck as appropriate).
3. The mask sets and releases CO₂ against the skin for roughly **45 minutes** — this is the relaxing part; many clients feel a mild tingling, warmth, or light effervescent/fizzing sensation as circulation increases.
4. The mask is peeled off; skin is finished with serums/SPF suited to the client.

**Sensation:** comfortable — gentle tingle/warmth, sometimes a light "champagne" fizz feeling. It is a needle-free, low-sensation treatment; the gel is reported as non-irritating and safe around the eyes and lips.

**Downtime:** essentially none. You can return to normal activities right away. Common aftercare guidance in professional literature: keep skin hydrated, use broad-spectrum SPF and avoid direct sun, skip harsh exfoliants/actives for a day or so, and avoid rubbing the area — Amber confirms your specific aftercare.

**Results timeline:** an **immediate** fresh/plumped, glowing look from the circulation boost (why it's an event favorite). Firmer, more lasting changes in the **appearance** of hydration, elasticity, and under-eye tone build with **repeated sessions over several weeks** — published topical protocols have used frequent short courses (e.g., multiple 45-minute applications per week over 2+ weeks) with elasticity gains noted at ~10 weeks. Results are individual.

## 5. Comparisons (honest — only vs. what House of Rose offers)

**Carboxy facial vs. Glo2Facial (the key one — both are "oxygenation / glow" stories, but the mechanism differs).**
- **Glo2Facial (Geneo)** creates a surface CO₂ reaction using an OxyPod and gel, then includes topical infusion and facial massage. It is a separate current $225, 60-minute appointment with Amber Mingione, Licensed Esthetician.
- **Carboxy facial** uses a **CO₂-releasing gel/mask** whose whole job is **transdermal CO₂ → Bohr-effect oxygen delivery and microcirculation** over ~45 minutes; it's a quieter, single-purpose "circulation & glow" treatment, strongest as a **soothing, no-downtime brightener** and for **under-eye/periorbital** tone.
- **Real difference:** Glo2Facial is a *fuller facial experience with exfoliation + serum infusion + massage*; carboxy is a *focused CO₂-oxygenation mask*. **Pick Glo2Facial** as the do-everything glow/entry facial and corrective base. **Pick carboxy** when the target is dull/tired skin and vascular under-eye shadowing, when you want the gentlest no-exfoliation option, or as a calming finish. Carboxy already has its own current $100, 30-minute staff-added option; do not substitute an unrelated historical finish price.

**Carboxy facial vs. a standard hydrating facial.**
- A hydrating facial layers humectants/masks for surface hydration and comfort. Carboxy works **from circulation inward** (oxygen delivery + microcirculation) plus its own hydration boost. **Pick carboxy** when "tired/dull/flat" is the complaint rather than simple dryness; **pick a hydrating facial** for pure comfort/moisture on reactive skin. They can also be complementary.

**Carboxy facial vs. LED therapy.**
- LED uses specific light wavelengths to calm and support skin post-treatment; carboxy uses CO₂ chemistry for oxygenation/microcirculation. They have different mechanisms. The current ledger does not list a separate LED appointment or add-on, so this comparison must not imply that clients can book or purchase one.

**When carboxy is the pick:** dull/tired complexion, day-to-day (vascular) under-eye shadowing, congestion-appearance and want a needle-free option, sensitive skin, event glow, or a soothing finish where you don't want exfoliation or needling.

## 9. FAQ bank (substantive, compliance-clean)

1. **What is a carboxy facial?** A needle-free treatment that uses a two-part carbon-dioxide gel/mask to support your skin's local circulation and oxygen delivery for a fresh, radiant look. At House of Rose it's a topical facial in Amber's advanced-facials lane — no injections.
2. **Does it involve needles or injections?** No. Our carboxy facial is **topical only** — the CO₂ is released from a gel on the skin's surface. (Injectable carboxytherapy is a separate, injection-based medical procedure we frame separately.)
3. **How does CO₂ help my skin?** Through the Bohr effect: a pocket of CO₂ at the skin prompts your body to release more oxygen locally and gently widens small vessels, boosting microcirculation — skin looks more perfused and awake.
4. **What does it feel like?** Relaxing, with a mild tingle, warmth, or light "fizzy" sensation as circulation increases. Most clients find it very comfortable.

7. **Will it get rid of my dark circles?** It's designed to support the *appearance* of under-eye tone, and it works best on day-to-day, circulation-related (bluish) shadowing. It doesn't fill hollows or remove pigment; if that's the cause, your provider will discuss other options at consultation.
8. **How is it different from the Glo2Facial?** Glo2Facial uses an OxyPod reaction, topical infusion, and facial massage. CO2 Lift Carboxy uses a topical CO₂ gel/mask and is a separate current appointment.

10. **When will I see results?** Literature findings do not establish a promised House of Rose outcome. Keep public wording to supported appearance changes and state that individual outcomes vary.
11. **Is it safe around my eyes?** The topical CO₂ gel is reported as non-irritating and is commonly used around the eye area — your provider confirms suitability for you.
12. **Can I add it to another treatment?** Often yes — a carboxy step can be a soothing finish after treatments like microchanneling or a peel. Your provider confirms combinations at your visit.
13. **Who shouldn't get it?** It's generally deferred during pregnancy or breastfeeding, over active skin infection at the site, or with certain significant uncontrolled health conditions. Not every client is a candidate — candidacy is confirmed at consultation.

- No "FDA-approved / clinically proven" unless citing a specific, verifiable clearance (none needed here — omit).
- Avoid "day spa." Use **Medical Aesthetics Practice** when a category descriptor is needed. "Medical
  spa" may support accurate platform or search context but does not lead.
