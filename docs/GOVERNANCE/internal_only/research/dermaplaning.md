## 1. The 3-sentence answer

Dermaplaning is a manual exfoliation treatment in which a licensed esthetician uses a sterile, single-use surgical blade to gently sweep away the outermost layer of dead surface skin cells and the fine vellus hair (peach fuzz) on the face — revealing skin that looks instantly smoother, brighter, and camera-ready. Because it clears that dulling layer, it's a favorite "week-of" treatment before weddings, photos, and events, and it lets makeup sit flawlessly and skincare absorb more effectively. At House of Rose in Punta Gorda, dermaplaning is part of Amber's advanced-facial lane and is offered both as a standalone treatment and as a prep step layered before facials, peels, and other resurfacing work.

## 2. How it works

**Plain English.** Your skin constantly sheds dead cells, but they don't always slough evenly — they build up on the surface as a rough, dull layer, and fine facial hair traps a bit of shadow, oil, and product on top of them. Dermaplaning uses a fine blade held at roughly a 45-degree angle to "shave" that layer off in light, feathery strokes. The provider removes two things in one pass: (1) the dead corneocytes of the outer skin (physical exfoliation), and (2) the vellus hair. The result is an immediately smoother surface, softer texture, a brighter look, and skin that takes makeup and skincare better.

**One level deeper (for the curious).** The layer being removed is the stratum corneum's loose outer corneocytes — dead, flattened skin cells. Removing them is a form of superficial physical exfoliation (mechanical, not chemical). Two downstream effects follow: because the occlusive dead-cell/hair layer is gone, topically applied serums and actives penetrate more readily, and light reflects more evenly off the smoother surface (the "glass-skin" glow). Estheticians also describe a modest stimulation of surface cell turnover. This is an **esthetician-grade, superficial** procedure — it works at the very top of the epidermis. It is distinct from a medical **dermatome** resurfacing procedure (a powered oscillating blade that removes deeper layers, done in a clinical/dermatology setting for scars). Some general medical references (e.g., Cleveland Clinic) describe the deeper dermatome version; House of Rose performs the light, superficial esthetician blade version — the framing throughout this brief reflects that.

**The "grows back thicker/darker" myth — debunked.** Vellus (peach-fuzz) hair grows back the same size, shape, and color it was before. Cutting a hair at the surface does not change the follicle, the hair's diameter, its growth rate, or its pigment — it only removes the tapered tip, which can make regrowth *feel* momentarily blunt, never actually thicker or darker. This is the same well-established shaving myth. Dermaplaning removes fine vellus hair; it is not a treatment for coarse terminal hair and is not a substitute for waxing.

## 3. Who it's for / not for

**Good candidates.** Clients who want an instant, low-downtime smoothing and brightening; anyone prepping for an event, photos, or a wedding; people bothered by visible peach fuzz that makes foundation look fuzzy or patchy; those with dry, flaky, dull, or rough-textured skin; and clients who want their skincare or a follow-on treatment (facial, peel, Glo2Facial) to work harder. It suits most skin tones and is a comfortable option for those who dislike the tingle of chemical exfoliation.

**Not for / use caution (provider confirms at consultation).** Dermaplaning is generally **not** appropriate over:
- Active acne breakouts, pustules, or cystic lesions (the blade can spread bacteria and irritate active lesions).
- An active **rosacea flare** or inflamed, reactive, or broken skin.
- Active cold sores / herpes simplex outbreaks, open lesions, or sunburn.
- Eczema, psoriasis, or dermatitis in flare; raised moles, skin tags, or vascular lesions (worked around, not over).
- Very sensitive or thin/reactive skin where physical exfoliation is poorly tolerated.
- Recent isotretinoin (Accutane) use, and recent chemical peels/laser or active prescription retinoids/acids without an appropriate wait — the provider will advise a pause window.

**Honest limits.** Dermaplaning is exfoliation and hair removal — it is **not** a collagen-building or corrective treatment. It does not lift the skin, fill lines, remove scars, or clear acne. The glow and smoothness are real but **temporary** (typically a few weeks) — it is a maintenance/prep treatment, not a one-and-done fix. Not every client is a candidate; individual outcomes vary, and final candidacy is confirmed by the licensed provider at consultation and skin analysis.

## 4. The visit

**Protocol (typical, ~50 minutes for the standalone).**
1. **Consult & skin analysis** — the provider confirms candidacy, checks for contraindications (active acne, rosacea flare, cold sores, recent actives), and reviews goals.
2. **Cleanse & dry** — skin is thoroughly cleansed and fully dried; dermaplaning is performed on dry skin so the blade glides.
3. **Blade work** — using a sterile, single-use blade at ~45 degrees, the provider works in small sections with short, light, feathery strokes, holding the skin taut, removing dead surface cells and vellus hair.
4. **Finish** — soothing serum/hydration, and typically SPF. Optional add-ons (see §7) such as an enzyme step, hydrating mask, or LED can follow.

**Sensation.** Comfortable and painless for most — described as a light scraping or "feathering" feeling, no numbing needed.

**Downtime.** Minimal to none — this is a classic "lunchtime" treatment. Skin may look slightly pink or feel a touch of "windburn" tightness briefly; makeup can usually go on the next day (or per provider guidance).

**Aftercare.**
- **Sun protection is the priority** — freshly exfoliated skin is more sun-sensitive; wear broad-spectrum SPF 30+ daily and minimize direct sun for several days (especially important in SW Florida).
- **Hold actives ~72 hours** — pause retinoids/retinol, AHAs/BHAs (glycolic, salicylic), benzoyl peroxide, and other exfoliants/astringents for about three days.
- Skip heavy sweat, saunas, steam rooms, and chlorinated pools for a couple of days.
- Keep skin hydrated; don't pick, scrub, or aggressively rub.
- If pairing with facial **waxing**, allow a gap (commonly ~a week) — don't wax freshly dermaplaned skin.

**Results timeline.** Immediate — smoothness, brightness, and makeup-readiness are visible the same day. The glow generally lasts **around 3–4 weeks** as the skin's natural turnover cycle (~28 days) restores the surface layer and vellus hair.

**Cadence.** Because skin turns over roughly every 28–40 days, most clients maintain results with a session about **once a month** — this spacing prevents over-exfoliation and lets skin recover between visits. This is why House of Rose offers a **Package of 4** for consistent, camera-ready maintenance. Cadence is individualized by the provider.

## 5. Comparisons (honest — vs treatments we offer)

**Dermaplaning vs. facial waxing (Brandy's lane).** Different jobs. Dermaplaning removes fine **vellus** (peach-fuzz) hair *and* exfoliates dead skin — its main purpose is surface smoothing, with hair removal as a bonus. **Facial waxing** (hard wax) is purpose-built **hair removal** that pulls coarser hair from the **root**, so it lasts longer (weeks) but does not exfoliate the way dermaplaning does and is shaped for brows, lip, chin, and face-framing. Choose dermaplaning for surface smoothing and peach fuzz; choose waxing when the goal is durable removal of more defined facial hair. Facial waxing is Brandy's lane, not Amber's.

**Dermaplaning vs. enzyme exfoliation (Brandy's basic-facial lane).** Both exfoliate the surface. Dermaplaning is **physical/mechanical** (a blade) and also removes vellus hair; **enzyme exfoliation** is **chemical/biological** (fruit/plant enzymes dissolve the "glue" between dead cells) and is often gentler for reactive skin but removes no hair. Dermaplaning gives a more immediately "shaved-smooth," makeup-ready finish; enzymes suit clients who prefer no blade or who have active/sensitive concerns. They can also be layered (enzyme within a facial, dermaplane as prep).

**Dermaplaning vs. hydrodermabrasion (Brandy's lane).** Hydrodermabrasion combines gentle abrasion with a hydrating/serum infusion and light suction — it exfoliates *and* deep-cleanses/hydrates in one, and can help with congestion. Dermaplaning is a dry-skin, blade-based exfoliation + peach-fuzz removal with a smoother makeup canvas as the signature outcome. Pick hydrodermabrasion for a hydrating "clean-and-plump"; pick dermaplaning for the glass-skin, fuzz-free finish. (Both are surface treatments, not corrective/collagen work.)

**Dermaplaning vs. BioRePeel (Amber's lane).** BioRePeel is a **TCA-based resurfacing peel** — a chemical treatment that works deeper for tone, texture, radiance, and firmness, with a bit more of a resurfacing effect over a series. Dermaplaning is a **superficial mechanical** exfoliation with essentially no downtime. Dermaplaning is the lighter, faster, event-week choice and a great **prep step before** a peel (it clears the dead-cell/hair layer so the peel penetrates more evenly); BioRePeel is the choice when the goal is genuine resurfacing/texture work. They are complementary, not interchangeable.

**Dermaplaning as prep before Glo2Facial or a peel.** This is one of dermaplaning's most valuable roles. Removing the dead-cell/vellus-hair layer first lets the **Glo2Facial** oxygenation and the OxyPod actives — and any **peel** — penetrate more evenly and work harder. House of Rose formalizes this as **"Dermaplane Prep" (+$45)**, an add-on to a needling or facial treatment (see §8). When each wins: dermaplaning *alone* for a quick pre-event glow; dermaplaning *as prep* when the client is already booked for a Glo2Facial/peel and wants to maximize it.

## 9. FAQ bank (substantive, compliance-clean)

**Will the hair grow back thicker or darker?** No. Dermaplaning removes fine vellus (peach-fuzz) hair at the surface; it grows back the same size, shape, and color it was before. Cutting a hair doesn't change the follicle, its thickness, or its pigment — this is the same well-worn shaving myth.

**Does dermaplaning hurt?** No — most clients find it painless and relaxing, describing a light feathering or scraping sensation. No numbing is needed.

**Is there any downtime?** Essentially none. Skin may look slightly pink or feel a touch tight briefly. It's a classic quick-turnaround treatment.

**How long do results last?** The smooth, bright finish typically lasts about 3–4 weeks, as your skin's natural turnover cycle restores the surface layer and the vellus hair. Results are individual.

**How often should I get it?** Because skin turns over roughly every month, most clients maintain the look with a session about once every 4 weeks — which is why we offer a Package of 4. Your provider will individualize the spacing.

**Can I get it before an event or my wedding?** Yes — that's one of its most popular uses. Many clients book it the week of a wedding, photoshoot, or event for a flawless, makeup-ready finish. We'll help you time it (often paired with event makeup).

**Will it help my makeup go on better?** Yes. Removing dead surface cells and peach fuzz gives foundation a smoother, more even canvas so makeup sits better and looks less "fuzzy."

**Does it help my skincare work better?** It can. Clearing the dulling dead-cell layer helps serums and actives absorb more readily afterward — one reason we use it as a prep step before facials and peels.

**Can I do it if I have active acne?** Generally not during active breakouts — the blade can irritate active lesions and spread bacteria. Your provider will assess your skin and may recommend a different approach or a different treatment first.

**I have rosacea — can I dermaplane?** It's approached with caution and not during a flare. If your skin is calm and clear, your provider will confirm candidacy at consultation; if it's inflamed or reactive, we'll steer you toward gentler options.

**Is dermaplaning the same as shaving my face?** It's related but not the same — dermaplaning uses a sterile, single-use professional blade and a trained technique to exfoliate the dead-skin layer *and* remove peach fuzz, which is more precise and more exfoliating than a home razor.

**Can I combine it with a facial or peel?** Yes — dermaplaning is often done as prep before a Glo2Facial or a peel so the treatment penetrates more evenly. We offer it as a "Dermaplane Prep" add-on as well as a standalone.

**How should I care for my skin afterward?** Wear broad-spectrum SPF 30+ daily and limit direct sun for a few days (especially here in SW Florida), pause retinoids and exfoliating acids for about 72 hours, skip saunas/heavy sweat briefly, and keep skin hydrated — don't pick or scrub.

**Who performs dermaplaning at House of Rose?** Amber, our esthetician, performs dermaplaning as part of her advanced-facial lane in Punta Gorda.

**Is it right for everyone?** Not every client is a candidate — active acne, an active rosacea/eczema flare, cold sores, sunburn, or very reactive skin can make it unsuitable. Your provider confirms candidacy at consultation, and individual outcomes vary.

## 10. Provider + compliance notes

**Lane.** Dermaplaning is **Amber's** lane (advanced facials/esthetics). It is **not** injectables (Diana) or facial waxing (Brandy). If a client's real need is coarse-hair removal, refer to waxing; do not position dermaplaning as a waxing substitute.

**Phrases to avoid for this topic:**
- Don't say it "removes," "erases," or "gets rid of" wrinkles, scars, or acne — use "helps reveal," "improves the appearance of," "smooths the look of."
- Don't call it "anti-aging" / "age-defying" / "turn back the clock." Don't say "permanent," "guaranteed," "cure," "treats acne," or "eliminates."
- Don't imply it's a hair-removal *solution* for coarse hair or that hair "won't come back" — it removes vellus hair temporarily and it grows back normally.
- Don't overstate depth — it's a **superficial** exfoliation; don't conflate it with deep dermatome resurfacing or claim it "renews collagen."
- Pricing: state the verified standalone or add-on amount directly; never use deal, percentage-off,
  sale, or cheap framing.

**Required guardrails on the surface:** provider authority ("final candidacy, combinations, and timing confirmed by the licensed provider at consultation & skin analysis"); "not every client is a candidate"; "individual outcomes vary."
