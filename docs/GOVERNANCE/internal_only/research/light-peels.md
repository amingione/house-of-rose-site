## 1. The 3-sentence answer

A light (superficial) chemical peel is a gentle, professional resurfacing treatment in which a low-strength cosmetic acid — such as lactic, glycolic, mandelic, or salicylic acid, sometimes fruit-enzyme-boosted — is applied to loosen the outermost layer of dull, built-up skin cells so fresher, smoother, brighter-looking skin can surface. At House of Rose it sits in **Brandy's basic / maintenance-facial lane** as the gentlest rung on our resurfacing ladder: a low-to-no-downtime "glow" treatment for the appearance of dullness, rough texture, mild unevenness, and everyday sun-exposed skin, designed to be repeated as a series and worked into a routine. It is deliberately lighter than Amber's advanced BioRePeel (a TCA-based resurfacing peel) and separate from our Face Reality acne program — it's the comfortable place many clients start.

## 2. How it works

**Plain English.** Healthy skin naturally sheds and renews itself roughly every 4–6 weeks, but sun, age, and buildup slow that turnover, leaving skin looking dull, rough, or uneven. A light peel uses a mild acid to gently dissolve the "glue" holding tired surface cells together, so they release and reveal the fresher skin underneath. Depending on the formula and strength, you may see light flaking a few days later — or you may see nothing visible at all and simply notice smoother, brighter skin. **Visible peeling is not required for the treatment to be doing its job.**

**One level deeper (for the curious).**
- **Depth class.** Superficial peels act on the **epidermis** only, without penetrating the basement membrane. That's what makes them the low-downtime, maintenance-tier option. Medium-depth peels reach the papillary/upper reticular dermis (that's the corrective territory a TCA-based peel like BioRePeel lives closer to), and deep peels go further still — neither of which is Brandy's light-peel service.
- **AHAs vs. BHA.** *Alpha-hydroxy acids* (AHAs — glycolic, lactic, mandelic) are **water-loving (hydrophilic)** and work across the skin surface, reducing corneocyte (dead-cell) adhesion so the stratum corneum sheds more evenly; glycolic (smallest molecule) penetrates fastest and is the classic brightener, lactic is gentler and more hydrating, mandelic (larger molecule) penetrates slowest and is the go-to for sensitive or more melanin-rich skin. *Beta-hydroxy acid* (BHA — salicylic) is **oil-loving (lipophilic)**, so it can slip into pores and loosen oil/comedonal buildup — useful where congestion and shine accompany dullness.
- **Enzyme-boosted "peels."** Fruit-enzyme exfoliants (papaya/papain, pumpkin, cranberry, pineapple/bromelain) digest surface keratin proteins rather than acidifying the skin. They're the gentlest form of professional exfoliation and are often used to warm the skin up before, or in place of, an acid peel for very sensitive clients. Skin Script's enzyme line and Cranberry Turnover Peel are exactly this category.
- **Beyond exfoliation.** Even superficial peels produce a mild reactive stimulation in the upper skin that, over a series, is associated with a fresher, smoother, more even-looking surface — which is why a *series* outperforms a one-off.

*(Mechanism/depth-class sourcing is general dermatology literature, cited in §Sources — not a House of Rose medical claim.)*

## 3. Who it's for / not for

**A good fit for clients who want:**
- A brighter, "lit-from-within" glow and smoother texture without real downtime.
- To address the **appearance** of dullness, rough or bumpy texture, mild unevenness in tone, and everyday sun-exposed skin — on a maintenance cadence.
- A gentle, comfortable entry point into resurfacing (first-timers, sensitive skin, or those not ready for advanced treatments).
- To maintain results between advanced treatments, or simply keep skin looking fresh.

**Not for / requires provider clearance (honest limits — candidacy is real):**
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — commonly treated as a contraindication for professional peels (salicylic/BHA especially); defer to the provider.
- **Recent oral isotretinoin (Accutane)** — a standard hold period (commonly cited as ~6 months off) before resurfacing; the provider confirms timing.
- **Active skin infection or open/broken skin in the area** — cold sores/active herpes simplex, bacterial infection, active inflamed breakouts, eczema/dermatitis/psoriasis flares, sunburn, or any open lesion. Never peel compromised skin.
- **History of keloids / hypertrophic scarring or known poor wound healing** — provider judgment.
- **Recent sun exposure, sunburn, waxing, or other resurfacing in the area** — spacing matters.
- **Very sensitive, reactive, or rosacea-prone skin** — may be steered to an enzyme or the gentlest acid, or a lower strength, at the provider's discretion.
- **Melanin-rich skin** — safe and common, but formula/strength must be chosen carefully to avoid the appearance of post-inflammatory pigment change; this is exactly why provider selection matters.

**Honest limit as a feature:** a light peel is a *maintenance-tier* treatment. It gives incremental, cumulative improvement and is meant to be repeated — it is **not** a corrective or "one-and-done" fix for deeper texture, established acne scarring, or significant sun damage. When the concern is bigger, the honest answer is to step up the ladder (see §5) — and final candidacy, formula, strength, and whether a peel is even the right tool are the licensed provider's decision at consultation and skin analysis. Not every client is a candidate, and individual outcomes vary.

## 4. The visit

**Protocol (typical superficial-peel flow — final protocol is the provider's):**
1. **Consultation & skin analysis** — history, contraindication screen, and goals; the provider selects formula (enzyme vs. AHA vs. BHA), acid, and strength for your skin. (House of Rose leads with advanced skin imaging as the smart first step.)
2. **Double cleanse & prep/degrease** — removes oil and residue so the peel acts evenly.
3. **Peel application** — the solution is applied in timed layers and closely monitored; you may feel warmth, mild tingling, or slight stinging that fades. Enzyme masks feel more like a warm, comfortable mask.
4. **Neutralize / remove** — AHA peels are neutralized or timed off; enzymes are rinsed.
5. **Soothe & finish** — calming, hydrating products, and **SPF applied before you leave.** Often paired with a relaxing facial massage/mask in Brandy's maintenance-facial style.

**Sensation:** mild — warmth, tingling, or light stinging during application; enzymes are the gentlest.

**Downtime:** minimal to none. Some clients have a day or two of mild redness and light flaking around days 3–5; **many see no visible peeling at all** and just notice smoother, brighter skin. Makeup is generally fine the next day per provider guidance.

**Aftercare (provider will personalize):**
- Strict sun protection is the non-negotiable — broad-spectrum SPF 30+ daily, reapplied, plus hats/shade. The first ~72 hours are the most sensitive.
- Keep the skin gently moisturized; **do not pick or peel** flaking skin (raises infection/pigment risk).
- Pause retinoids, scrubs, other acids, and aggressive actives for a few days per provider instruction.
- Avoid excess heat, heavy sweating/saunas, and prolonged sun immediately after.

**Results timeline:** a smoother *feel* first, then a brighter, more even-looking tone as the skin cycles over the following days. Because superficial peels are epidermis-only, effects are gentle and build with repetition.

**Cadence:** best as a **series**, commonly ~3–6 sessions spaced roughly 3–5 weeks apart, then ongoing maintenance every few weeks. (General dermatology norm — the provider sets your actual plan.)

## 9. FAQ bank (answer-first, compliance-clean)

1. **What is a light chemical peel?** A gentle professional treatment that uses a mild cosmetic acid (or fruit enzyme) to loosen dull surface skin cells, helping reveal smoother, brighter-looking skin — with little to no downtime.
2. **Will I actually peel / flake?** Not necessarily. Some clients see light flaking around days 3–5; many see nothing visible and simply notice smoother, brighter skin. **Visible peeling isn't required for the treatment to work.**
3. **How is this different from BioRePeel?** BioRePeel is a stronger, TCA-based resurfacing peel in Amber's advanced lane for the appearance of texture, tone, and scarring. A light peel is Brandy's gentle, maintenance-tier "glow" version — the comfortable starting rung.
4. **Is this the acne peel?** No. Congestion/acne-prone skin is handled through our Face Reality program with Amber. A light peel is a brightening/smoothing maintenance treatment.
5. **Does it hurt?** Most clients feel only mild warmth, tingling, or light stinging during application that fades quickly. Enzyme versions are the gentlest.
6. **How much downtime?** Typically little to none — occasionally a day or two of mild redness or light flaking. You can usually resume normal life right away and makeup the next day, per your provider.
7. **How many treatments will I need?** Light peels work best as a series — commonly around 3–6 sessions a few weeks apart — then ongoing maintenance. Your provider sets your plan.
8. **How soon will I see results?** Many clients notice smoother-feeling skin first, then a brighter, more even-looking tone over the following days, with more improvement as the series continues. Individual outcomes vary.
9. **Can I get one before an event?** Yes — a light peel or enzyme (often with dermaplaning and a Glo2Facial finish) is a popular pre-event glow. We'll time it so any flaking is well behind you; ask us at booking.
10. **Is it safe for sensitive skin or darker skin tones?** Often yes, with the right formula and strength — your provider may choose an enzyme, lactic, or mandelic acid, which are gentler options. Careful provider selection is exactly why this is a professional treatment.
11. **Can I do it if I'm pregnant or breastfeeding, or recently on Accutane?** These generally require waiting or a modified approach; please tell us at consultation so your provider can advise. Not every client is a candidate.
12. **What do I do afterward?** Wear broad-spectrum SPF 30+ every day (essential in our Florida sun), keep skin gently moisturized, don't pick or peel flaking skin, and pause strong actives for a few days per your provider's instructions.
13. **Do I need to protect my skin from the sun even if I'm indoors mostly?** Yes — daily sunscreen protects and preserves your results, especially in Southwest Florida's year-round sun. Skip it and you undo the work.
14. **How is this different from a facial?** A relaxing facial cleans, hydrates, and pampers; a light peel adds a gentle chemical-exfoliation step focused on brightening and smoothing. We often combine the two.
15. **Should I get a light peel or a stronger treatment?** That depends on your skin and goals — a light peel is our maintenance/glow option; deeper texture, tone, or scarring may be better served by advanced treatments. Your skin analysis and provider will guide the honest choice.
