## 1. The 3-sentence answer

Injectable PRF (platelet-rich fibrin) at House of Rose uses a small sample of **your own blood**, spun in a centrifuge with no additives, then injected by Diana, RN, to put your body's own growth factors to work beneath the skin — softening under-eye hollows and dark circles, and rebuilding collagen for firmer, more even-toned facial skin over the months that follow. **PRF EZ-Gel (also called Alb-PRF or "bio-filler")** takes that same autologous blood a step further: the plasma is gently heated and cooled into a soft gel that adds **subtle, temporary volume** while the regenerative growth factors keep working — a natural-derived alternative to synthetic filler, made entirely from you. Because both are drawn from your own blood, they carry a very low risk of allergic reaction or rejection, and results develop gradually and naturally rather than all at once.

## 2. How it works

**Plain English.** A phlebotomy-style blood draw (just like routine lab work) is spun in a centrifuge. Because **no anticoagulant** is added, the blood forms a natural **fibrin matrix** — a soft scaffold that traps platelets, white blood cells, and growth factors. When this PRF is injected, that scaffold sits in the tissue and **releases growth factors gradually** over roughly a week or two, signalling your own fibroblasts to build new collagen and elastin. It works *with* your biology rather than adding a foreign substance.

**One level deeper (for the curious).**
- **PRF vs PRP — the difference is timing of release.** First-generation PRP uses an anticoagulant and a higher-speed spin, stays liquid, and releases growth factors in a fast burst (much of it within the first hour). Second-generation **PRF uses no anticoagulant and a single low-speed spin**, so the fibrin scaffold releases growth factors **slowly over ~7–14 days** — a steady renewal signal instead of a spike (Kobayashi/Miron slow-release data; Davies & Miron 2024 SR).
- **Key growth factors:** PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF, EGF, IGF — these drive collagen production, angiogenesis (new micro-vessels), cell migration, and tissue repair. In cell-culture work, fibroblasts migrated substantially more with fluid-PRF than with PRP or control (Wang et al. 2019, *J Cosmet Dermatol*) — laboratory rationale, not a clinical guarantee.
- **A-PRF vs i-PRF.** "Advanced" PRF (**A-PRF**) is the solid/clot form (membranes, plugs). "Injectable" PRF (**i-PRF**) is a liquid form from a shorter, lower spin, ideal for injecting into the dermis and delicate areas like the tear trough. Diana selects the form and spin protocol to the goal.
- **EZ-Gel / Alb-PRF "bio-filler."** Liquid PRF is great for skin quality but thin and quickly resorbed, so it can't replace lost *volume*. To make a bio-filler, the plasma fraction is processed through a **heat step (denaturing human serum albumin, ~75 °C for ~10 minutes) then a cool step**, and mixed back with the growth-factor fraction — commercially the EZGEL protocol names these **EZHEAT** and **EZCOOL** (a roughly 2:1 mix) — producing an **"albumin gel"** that, once injected, gives structure lasting **roughly 4–6 months while simultaneously building collagen over time** (Davies & Miron 2024 SR; process detail corroborated by EZGEL device/clinic sources). It is fully autologous: your own blood, heated and cooled, with **no synthetic material.**

## 3. Who it's for / not for

**Good candidates.** Generally healthy adults who want **natural-derived, gradual rejuvenation** and prefer to avoid or minimize synthetic filler: tired, hollow, or darkened under-eyes; early skin laxity, fine lines, and dull texture on the face, neck, and décolleté; and those who like the idea of a treatment made from their own biology with a very low allergy/rejection profile.

**Not for everyone (honest limits).**
- **Not a heavy-volume filler.** PRF and even EZ-Gel deliver *subtle* volume plus skin-quality improvement. Deep tear-trough hollows or significant mid-face volume loss may still be better served by hyaluronic-acid (HA) filler — or a combination. Diana will say so honestly.
- **Results are gradual, not instant.** This is a build-over-months treatment; someone who needs a one-visit dramatic change may be disappointed.
- **Typical exclusions for autologous platelet procedures** (confirmed at consultation): active infection or breakout at the injection site, certain blood/platelet or bleeding disorders, current anticoagulation, and pregnancy or breastfeeding are generally deferred. Final candidacy, contraindications, and combinations are determined by Diana, RN, under medical-director oversight — **not every client is a candidate, and individual outcomes vary.**

## 4. The visit

**Protocol, step by step (Diana, RN).**
1. **Consultation & candidacy.** Skin and under-eye assessment, honest expectation-setting, review of contraindications and medications.
2. **Blood draw.** A small sample, drawn like routine lab work.
3. **Centrifugation.** Spun with no additives to form PRF (or processed through the heat/cool cycle for EZ-Gel bio-filler).
4. **Comfort.** Topical numbing as appropriate; fine needles or a cannula for delicate areas.
5. **Injection.** Diana places the PRF/EZ-Gel precisely — beneath the under-eye, or into the target facial area.

**Sensation.** A brief pinch at the draw and small pinches or pressure at the injection points; numbing keeps it well-tolerated. Appointments run about **45 minutes (under-eye)** to **60 minutes (facial area)**; EZ-Gel runs longer for the heat/cool processing step.

**Downtime & aftercare.** Little downtime relative to more aggressive procedures, but expect **mild swelling, redness, and possible bruising for a few days**; under-eye **swelling is often most noticeable the next morning** and settles over 24–72 hours. Typical guidance: hydrate and eat beforehand; if medically appropriate, pause blood thinners, NSAIDs, fish oil, vitamin E, and alcohol before treatment; keep the area clean and avoid heavy exertion for the first day. Diana provides your specific aftercare.

**Results timeline.** Early changes commonly appear at **2–6 weeks**; collagen remodeling continues to **peak around 3–6 months**; results commonly **last 6–12 months** (varies by skin, age, and lifestyle). EZ-Gel's *volumizing* effect is more temporary (roughly 4–6 months) while its regenerative effect continues longer. **Honest durability caveat:** the aesthetic evidence is limited and one systematic review noted PRF gains **often diminished by around 6 months** — which is exactly why plans are built as a series with maintenance. *Individual results vary.*

**Cadence.** Most plans are a **series of ~3 sessions spaced about 4–6 weeks apart**, with maintenance typically every 6–12 months. House of Rose offers prepaid series (see §8).

## 5. Comparisons (honest)

**Injectable PRF (Diana, RN) vs topical PRF (Amber, non-injected facial adjunct).** Same source material — your own PRF — but two different services in two different lanes. **Amber's PRF is topical only**, applied to the skin surface during a microchanneling/needling facial to support texture, tone, and radiance across the treated surface; it is *not* injected. **Diana's PRF is injected** beneath the skin to target under-eye hollows, dark circles, and deeper facial rejuvenation with subtle volume. *When each wins:* choose **topical PRF (Amber)** for overall skin quality, glow, and texture with a facial experience; choose **injectable PRF (Diana)** when the goal is under-eye rejuvenation or targeted, deeper collagen rebuilding. They can also be sequenced across a plan.

**PRF EZ-Gel bio-filler vs HA dermal filler (autologous vs synthetic).** EZ-Gel is made **entirely from your own blood** (heat-set albumin gel) — no synthetic material, very low allergy/rejection risk, and it *builds collagen* while it holds. HA filler is a **synthetic (lab-made) hyaluronic-acid gel** that delivers **more, and more predictable, volume** and lasts longer per session, and is reversible with hyaluronidase. *When each wins:* choose **EZ-Gel** when you want a natural-derived, regenerative option with subtle volume and skin-quality gains; choose **HA filler (Diana also offers RHA, Juvéderm)** when you need meaningful, structured volume correction (deep folds, cheek projection). EZ-Gel should **never** be marketed as an equivalent to HA for significant volume — that is an honest limit, not a weakness. Many practices legitimately combine the two.

**PRF (or EZ-Gel) under-eye vs HA filler under-eye — why PRF is often preferred for thin under-eye skin.** The under-eye/tear-trough skin is thin and unforgiving. HA filler there is **hydrophilic** — it draws water and can puff or persist, and superficial placement risks a bluish **Tyndall effect**; the vascular anatomy also demands care. **PRF and EZ-Gel** improve **skin quality and darkness** and add only subtle volume, with a low allergy/rejection profile because they're autologous — which is why many clients and providers prefer PRF for the delicate under-eye. *Honest limit:* a **true deep hollow** may still need a small amount of well-placed HA (or a combination). Diana confirms the right approach at consultation.

## 9. FAQ bank

1. **What is injectable PRF?** PRF (platelet-rich fibrin) is made from a small sample of your own blood, spun with no additives, then injected by Diana, RN, to release your body's own growth factors beneath the skin — supporting collagen and softening under-eye hollows and dark circles. It's fully autologous.
2. **Is injectable PRF the same as Amber's PRF facial?** No. Amber's PRF is **topical only**, applied to the skin surface during a microchanneling facial. **Injectable PRF is a separate, RN-led procedure** performed by Diana. Same source material, different lane.
3. **What is PRF EZ-Gel / "bio-filler"?** It's your own plasma gently heated and cooled into a soft gel (Alb-PRF) that adds **subtle, temporary volume** while your growth factors keep working — a natural-derived alternative to synthetic filler, made entirely from you.
4. **Is PRF the same as PRP?** They're related, but PRF uses **no anticoagulant** and a low-speed spin, so its fibrin scaffold releases growth factors **gradually over days** rather than in a fast burst. Many providers consider PRF the more advanced, "second-generation" option.
5. **Does PRF really help under-eye dark circles and hollows?** The under-eye is PRF's most popular and best-studied target. PRF is designed to improve skin quality and the appearance of darkness and mild hollowing, with subtle volume. Deep hollows may still need a little HA filler. *Individual results vary.*
6. **PRF/EZ-Gel vs regular filler — which should I get?** Fillers (HA) add more, more predictable volume and last longer per session. PRF/EZ-Gel are natural-derived, regenerate skin quality, and add only subtle volume. For thin under-eye skin many prefer PRF; for real volume correction, HA often wins. Diana helps you choose — and sometimes combines both.
7. **Is there downtime?** Minimal, but expect mild swelling, redness, and possible bruising for a few days; under-eye swelling is often most noticeable the next morning and settles within 24–72 hours.
8. **When will I see results, and how long do they last?** Early changes at 2–6 weeks, with collagen remodeling peaking around 3–6 months. Results commonly last 6–12 months; EZ-Gel's volume is more temporary (roughly 4–6 months). *Outcomes are individual.*
9. **How many sessions will I need?** Most clients do a **series of about 3 sessions spaced 4–6 weeks apart**, then maintenance every 6–12 months.
10. **Is PRF safe? Any allergy risk?** Because it's made from your own blood, the risk of allergic reaction or rejection is very low. Side effects are usually mild and short-lived. Final candidacy is confirmed by Diana, RN.
11. **Is PRF "FDA-approved"?** PRF is an autologous blood product — made from your own blood. The preparation device is FDA-cleared, but the cosmetic procedure itself is **not** FDA-approved for facial rejuvenation, and we never claim it is.
12. **Do you offer PRF for hair loss?** House of Rose does **not** currently offer PRF hair restoration. Our injectable PRF focuses on under-eye and facial rejuvenation.
13. **Can I combine PRF with Botox or filler?** Often, yes — PRF/EZ-Gel pair well with a neuromodulator or a small amount of HA. Diana confirms the right combination for you at consultation.
14. **Who performs injectable PRF at House of Rose?** Diana, a registered nurse, under medical-director oversight, at our Punta Gorda studio.
15. **What should I do before my appointment?** Hydrate and eat beforehand, and if medically appropriate, pause blood thinners, NSAIDs, fish oil, vitamin E, and alcohol in the days before to reduce bruising. Diana gives you personalized pre-care.

- **"treats/cures/heals" + a condition** (dark circles, scarring, hair loss). Use "for the appearance of," "supports," "designed to," "many clients notice."
