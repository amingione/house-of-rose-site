# Topical PRF (Amber's Advanced-Facial Adjunct) — Research Brief

**Date:** 2026-07-13 · **Provider lane:** Amber (esthetician + RN assistant) — advanced facials; **PRF topical only, NEVER injected** · **Status:** draft for Phase 3/4 consumption · Sources listed at end.

> **The one compliance line this whole brief hangs on:** at House of Rose, topical PRF is your **own platelet-rich fibrin prepared from your own blood and applied on the skin surface during eligible Microneedling.** It is **not injected.** House of Rose performs Microneedling with the Procell Therapies device; **Procell Microchanneling** is the branded name only when Procell is specifically in context, not a second modality. ## 1. The 3-sentence answer

Topical PRF at House of Rose is an autologous add-on to Amber's advanced facials: a small sample of your own blood is prepared as platelet-rich fibrin liquid, then applied to the skin surface during eligible Microneedling. ## 2. How it works

**In plain English.** We draw a small tube of your blood, like routine lab work. It goes straight into a centrifuge and spins gently. Because we use **no anticoagulant**, the blood is allowed to begin clotting naturally, forming a soft three-dimensional **fibrin matrix** (a scaffold) that traps your platelets, white blood cells, and growth factors. That fibrin fluid is applied to your skin during the needling protocol. Over the following days, the scaffold releases those growth factors gradually — a slow, steady renewal signal rather than a single burst — which supports your skin's own collagen and repair response.

**One level deeper (for the curious).**
- **PRF is a "second-generation" platelet concentrate.** Its defining feature is the **absence of anticoagulant** plus **low-speed, single-step centrifugation** (the "Low-Speed Centrifugation Concept" introduced by Choukroun and Ghanaati). This lets fibrin polymerize naturally into a mesh that holds cells and growth factors.
- **PRF vs PRP — the difference is the *timing* of release.** PRP uses an anticoagulant and higher-speed spinning, stays liquid, and once activated dumps most of its growth factors fast (roughly 70% within ~10 minutes, ~95% within the first hour, per platelet-concentrate literature). PRF has no anticoagulant, so its fibrin scaffold releases growth factors **gradually over roughly 7–14 days** — A-PRF (advanced PRF, spun at ~200 g) has been shown to release the highest total growth factors over 10 days among the concentrates studied (Kobayashi et al., PubMed 26809431).
- **The growth factors that matter:** PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF, EGF, IGF — these are the body's own signals for collagen production, new micro-vessel formation (angiogenesis), and tissue repair.
- **Why "autologous" matters:** PRF is 100% from you — no animal-derived additives, no synthetic chemicals, very low risk of allergic reaction or rejection. This is its true differentiator, not any volumizing power.
- **A-PRF vs i-PRF terminology (background literacy only):** these are centrifugation variants — solid membrane forms (A-PRF/A-PRF+) versus liquid injectable forms (i-PRF). For Amber's **topical** application the relevant form is a **fluid/liquid PRF applied to the surface**; the "injectable" (i-PRF) framing belongs to Diana's lane and should not be borrowed into topical copy.
- **The synergy rationale:** applying fluid PRF immediately after channeling places your own growth factors in contact with freshly stimulated skin — a biologically sensible pairing that is intended to amplify the collagen-induction response and support recovery. In-vitro work (Wang et al., J Cosmet Dermatol 2019, PubMed 30990574) found skin fibroblasts migrated markedly more in fluid-PRF than in control or PRP; this is cell-culture data underpinning the rationale, **not** a clinical outcome guarantee.

## 3. Who it's for / not for

**A good fit for someone who:**
- Values a **natural, autologous** approach ("uses my own blood, no synthetics") and is comfortable with a small blood draw.
- Is already a candidate for Microneedling and wants to consider a topical PRF adjunct.
- Is working on overall skin quality — texture, the appearance of fine lines, dullness, enlarged-looking pores — or on the appearance of shallow/rolling acne scars and defined texture (with microneedling), on face or an approved body zone.

**Not a fit / postpone / needs provider review** (this is a screening guide, not the candidacy decision — the licensed provider decides):
- Active skin infection, active rash, current cold-sore outbreak, or any inflamed/open/sunburned skin in the treatment area.
- Bleeding or clotting disorders, or **anticoagulant / blood-thinner** use; recent isotretinoin use; uncontrolled diabetes; immune suppression; certain autoimmune conditions.
- History of keloid or abnormal scarring.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.
- Known allergy/sensitivity to numbing agents, adhesives, or topicals used in the protocol.
- Discomfort with a blood draw, or an inability to follow aftercare (sun/heat/exercise restrictions).

**Honest limits.** Topical PRF is an **adjunct**, not a stand-alone miracle. It does not add meaningful volume (that framing belongs to injectable/EZ-Gel work), it does not "remove" scars or stretch marks, and it should never be promised to outperform needling alone for every person or concern — the aesthetic evidence base for PRF is promising but early (mostly small studies and case series, short follow-up). Not every client is a candidate, and individual outcomes vary.

## 4. The visit

**Protocol, step by step (Amber's topical PRF, added to a needling treatment):**
1. **Consultation & skin analysis** — candidacy, contraindications, treatment area, device/depth, and serum choice confirmed by the provider; honest expectation-setting.
2. **Blood draw** — a small sample, like routine lab work.
3. **Gentle low-speed spin** — no anticoagulant or additives, so a natural fibrin matrix forms.
4. **Skin prep** — cleanse; topical numbing as appropriate; a new single-use cartridge for every session. Dermaplaning or a non-RF prep step may be considered only when the provider selects it; do not assume a fixed same-day stack.
5. **Microneedling** — performed with the Procell Therapies device using the provider-selected area, settings, and eligible material plan. In explicit Procell context, this may be called Procell Microchanneling.
6. **Topical PRF applied** — your fluid PRF is applied to the surface during the protocol. **Not injected.**
7. **Finish & aftercare instructions** — provider-selected recovery support and written aftercare. Do not add RF, ultrasound, or a full Glo2 sequence to a needling visit unless a device-specific, clinician-approved protocol supports it.

**Fibrin recovery-mask idea — internal only.** A more polymerized PRF/fibrin consistency is being evaluated as a possible recovery step. It is not a standardized facial protocol, marketed add-on, or public claim. Do not use "sticky bone," retained-contact-time, occlusion, LED-sequence, or slow-release outcome claims until the medical director and the blood-processing-system manufacturer approve a written protocol.

**Sensation.** Tolerable for most; comfort is supported by numbing. Expect the needling sensation more than the PRF itself.

**Downtime & short-term recovery.** Redness, tightness, dryness, mild roughness, itching, light peeling, tenderness, or pinpoint bleeding can occur; ~24 hours of redness after channeling is common (House PRF brochure). Exact recovery varies with device, depth, area, and skin — the provider gives written aftercare rather than promising a fixed window. If Procell channeling is included, observe the **open-channel window** afterward (no makeup/sunscreen/touching for the period the provider specifies) and follow the prescribed home care. Avoid sun, heat, and strenuous exercise as directed.

**Results timeline.** Early changes commonly appear around **2–6 weeks**; collagen remodeling continues to develop over **weeks to months**. Results are individual and depend on skin, candidacy, and consistency.

**Cadence.** Most corrective goals are a **series** — often **3–6 visits about 4–6 weeks apart** — then individualized maintenance. Body skin is thicker, so a longer series (Series of 6) is realistic. The provider may recommend fewer, more, or a different option.

## 5. Comparisons (when each honestly wins)

- **Topical PRF vs a Procell topical material plan.** These are provider-selected topical options or adjuncts within one Microneedling service, not competing needling modalities. Topical PRF adds a blood draw and additional screening; a Procell material plan does not. The provider determines whether either is appropriate. Do **not** call Procell serums "stem-cell" serums or say growth factors or PRF are "delivered deep into the skin."
- **Topical PRF (Amber) vs injectable PRF (Diana, RN).** Topical PRF is applied **on the surface** during needling — best positioned for **skin texture, fine lines, pores, and overall skin quality**. Injectable PRF is a **separate medical procedure** delivered **beneath the skin** by Diana — the standard for **under-eyes / tear troughs, midface, and hair/scalp**, targeting volume-loss context and deeper tissue quality. **Different lanes, different tools, different goals** — one does not substitute for the other, and Amber never performs the injectable version.
- **Topical PRF vs PRF EZ-Gel bio-filler (Diana, RN).** EZ-Gel combines PRF with the patient's own heated-then-cooled albumin to form an **injectable gel** giving **subtle, temporary volume** plus regenerative benefit — a natural alternative to synthetic filler (but **not** as volumizing as HA filler, and never marketed as its equal). It is squarely **Diana's injectable lane.** Topical PRF adds **no volume** and is a **surface adjunct only.** If a client's real goal is subtle volume restoration, that's an EZ-Gel / injectable conversation with Diana — refer, don't stretch topical PRF to fit.

## 9. FAQ bank (substantive, compliance-clean)

1. **What is topical PRF?** It's your own platelet-rich fibrin—prepared from a small sample of your blood—applied to your skin during eligible Microneedling. At House of Rose it is topical only.
2. **Is topical PRF the same as PRF injections?** No. Topical PRF is applied on the surface during needling (Amber's advanced-facial lane). PRF injections are a separate procedure performed beneath the skin by Diana, RN. They are different treatments with different goals.
3. **Does the device inject the PRF through the microchannels?** No. We describe PRF accurately as a topical adjunct applied during the protocol. The FDA states microneedling devices are not authorized to deliver blood products into the skin.
4. **What's the difference between PRF and PRP?** Both are made from your own blood. PRP uses an anticoagulant, stays liquid, and releases its growth factors in a fast burst. PRF uses no anticoagulant and forms a natural fibrin scaffold that releases growth factors gradually over roughly one to two weeks.
5. **Is PRF safe?** Because it's made entirely from your own blood, the risk of allergic reaction or rejection is very low. Side effects are usually mild and short-lived — swelling, redness, or light bruising for a few days. Candidacy is confirmed by the provider; not every client is a candidate.
6. **Is there downtime?** Typically minimal. Redness, tightness, dryness, or light peeling can occur, and about a day of redness after channeling is common. Your exact recovery depends on the device, depth, and your skin — you'll get written aftercare rather than a fixed promise.
7. **When will I see results?** Many clients notice early changes around two to six weeks, with collagen remodeling continuing over weeks to months. Individual outcomes vary.
8. **How many sessions will I need?** Most corrective goals are planned as a series — often three to six sessions about four to six weeks apart — then individualized maintenance. Your provider may recommend a different plan after your skin analysis.
9. **When can topical PRF be added to Microneedling?** The provider determines whether topical PRF is appropriate after reviewing candidacy, the treatment area, and the concern. It is an adjunct to the same Microneedling service, not a separate needling modality.
10. **Can topical PRF fix my under-eye hollows or dark circles?** Under-eye rejuvenation is best addressed by injectable PRF, which is Diana's separate lane. Amber's topical PRF supports the appearance of texture, fine lines, and overall skin quality, not volume.
11. **Can PRF be used for hair thinning?** PRF scalp/hair treatments are injectable and are performed by Diana, RN — not part of Amber's topical facial service.
12. **Does topical PRF add volume like filler?** No. Topical PRF supports skin quality and texture; it does not add meaningful volume. Subtle natural volume is a conversation about injectable options like EZ-Gel bio-filler with Diana.
13. **Do I need to prepare for my appointment?** Hydrate well and eat beforehand. If your provider advises it and it's medically appropriate, some clients pause blood-thinning items (like fish oil, high-dose vitamin E, or alcohol) and retinoids/exfoliating acids for a few days before — always confirm with the provider first.
14. **Is topical PRF FDA-approved?** PRF is made from your own blood; the preparation/centrifuge device is FDA-cleared, but the aesthetic procedure itself is not "FDA-approved," and we never describe it that way. We also never say a needling device is authorized to deliver blood products into the skin.
15. **Can I do topical PRF on my body?** Yes — as a body-zone upgrade (larger area, more product), commonly for the appearance of stretch marks, scars, or crepey skin, usually planned as a longer series. The zone and candidacy are assessed separately.
