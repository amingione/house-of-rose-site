## Current client answer

House of Rose offers Botox and Daxxify for lines related to facial movement. The useful clue is whether
the line appears or becomes deeper when a person frowns, raises the brows, or squints. Changes in volume
at the lips, cheeks, or folds belong to the separate Dermal Fillers discussion.

Both products are listed at $14 per product-specific unit. That matching rate does not establish the
same dose or total price: Botox units and Daxxify units cannot be compared or converted.

## Product distinction

- **Botox** contains onabotulinumtoxinA in a formulation with human albumin and sodium chloride.
- **Daxxify** contains daxibotulinumtoxinA-lanm with the 35-amino-acid RTP004 peptide excipient.
- Each prescribing label defines potency in units specific to that product. Unit counts are not
  interchangeable or convertible.
- The current House of Rose price is $14 per Botox unit and $14 per Daxxify unit. The matching rate is
  not evidence of matching dose, total, effect, or value.

The reviewed comparison reports product-specific glabellar-line evidence without presenting it as one
head-to-head trial. The Daxxify SAKURA trials reported about 24 weeks to their measured endpoint. A
pooled Botox analysis reported 120 days among participants who had responded by day 30. Those figures
use different populations and endpoints. Do not flatten them into a universal “lasts X months” promise
or use them to declare one product better.

The reviewed comparison likewise reports different onset measures: the Daxxify studies measured a
median three days to subject-rated improvement of at least one point, while the Botox label says
chemical denervation typically begins one to two days after injection and increases during the first
week. Do not present those unlike measures as a race.

Neither prescribing label supplies one universal downtime period. Do not publish a blanket “no
downtime” statement.

## No automatic cross-service pathway

Neurotoxin injections and dermal fillers answer different questions; the current pages explain that
distinction. That difference does not create a recommendation to buy both. PRF, facials, resurfacing,
skin imaging, or home-care products must not be presented as automatic companions or next purchases.

Do not describe a client’s concern as the start of a treatment ladder. Do not tell a visitor which
product to choose, whether to combine services, or what should come next. The current service page and
comparison provide enough verified information for a client to ask a useful question at consultation.

## Claims not authorized by this brief

Do not publish:

- a fixed dose or typical unit count by treatment area;
- an annual treatment schedule or cost-per-year comparison;
- “longer-lasting,” “fewer visits,” “better value,” or a product preference without the exact cited
  endpoint and limitations;
- a promise of natural, undetectable, frozen-free, subtle, conservative, or predictable results;
- a universal onset, recovery, bruising, pain, or downtime statement;
- therapeutic indications, off-label areas, or a medical-condition claim;
- product combinations, treatment stacks, retail recommendations, or maintenance plans;
- direct-treatment booking language that bypasses the current consultation path; or
- a claim that the medical director performs treatment or is present at the practice.
