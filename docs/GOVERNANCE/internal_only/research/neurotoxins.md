## 1. The 3-sentence answer

Neurotoxins — also called wrinkle relaxers or neuromodulators — are precision injections of botulinum toxin type A that temporarily relax the small facial muscles responsible for dynamic expression lines (the creases that appear when you frown, raise your brows, or squint). At House of Rose in Punta Gorda, they're administered by Diana, our injecting RN, under medical-director oversight, and dosed to your individual anatomy so movement is softened — not erased. We offer two: **Botox**, the long-established standard, and **Daxxify**, a newer peptide-stabilized neuromodulator designed to last longer between visits.

---

## 2. How it works

**Plain English.** The lines that show up when you make expressions — the "11s" between the brows, forehead lines, crow's feet at the corners of the eyes — are caused by repeated muscle movement folding the skin. A neurotoxin is injected in tiny amounts directly into those specific muscles. It temporarily quiets the signal that tells the muscle to contract, so the muscle relaxes and the overlying skin creases less. The result is smoother, more rested-looking skin that still moves naturally when dosed conservatively. It is temporary and it wears off — the muscle gradually regains full movement over months, which is why maintenance visits are part of the treatment.

**One level deeper.** Botulinum toxin type A blocks cholinergic (nerve-to-muscle) transmission at the **neuromuscular junction**. It's taken up into the motor-nerve terminal, where it cleaves **SNAP-25**, a protein the nerve needs to dock and release **acetylcholine** — the neurotransmitter that triggers muscle contraction. With acetylcholine release inhibited, the injected muscle undergoes partial, localized, reversible chemical denervation: it can't fully contract, so it stops pulling the skin into a crease. Nerve terminals regenerate over time, movement returns, and the effect fades — hence the need for repeat treatment.

**What makes Daxxify different (the peptide).** All botulinum toxin products need a stabilizing excipient to protect the toxin protein. Most legacy products (including Botox) use **human serum albumin**. Daxxify (daxibotulinumtoxinA) instead uses a proprietary **Peptide Exchange Technology** — a synthetic 35-amino-acid stabilizing peptide with a highly positive charge (the excipient RTP004), and it is free of human- and animal-derived components. The active toxin still works by the same acetylcholine/SNAP-25 mechanism; the peptide is a formulation/stabilization difference that is associated in trials with a **longer median duration of effect** (see §5). The peptide does not change *what* the toxin does — only how the product is stabilized and, clinically, how long results tend to persist.

## 3. Who it's for / not for

**Good candidates (confirmed by Diana at consultation).** Adults bothered by **dynamic** expression lines — frown lines between the brows (glabellar), horizontal forehead lines, crow's feet — who want a subtle softening with natural movement. Also relevant for clients interested in **prevention framing**: because neurotoxins reduce the repetitive folding that etches dynamic lines into deeper static creases over time, some clients begin treatment earlier as maintenance of skin quality rather than correction. Candidacy, area selection, and dosing are always determined by Diana.

**Not for / requires medical clearance.** Neurotoxins are **contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding** — House of Rose does not treat pregnant or nursing clients with neuromodulators. They are also contraindicated or require careful medical evaluation in people with **neuromuscular disorders** such as myasthenia gravis, Lambert-Eaton syndrome, or ALS, because the toxin can compound muscle weakness. Other reasons to defer or decline include: known allergy/hypersensitivity to any botulinum toxin product or a formulation component, active infection or inflammation at the injection site, certain neurological conditions, and use of specific medications that affect neuromuscular transmission — all of which Diana reviews during the medical intake.

**Honest limits.** Neurotoxins relax muscles that cause *movement* lines. They do **not** add volume, do **not** fill deep static folds or hollows, do **not** tighten significant laxity, and do **not** improve surface texture, tone, pores, or sun damage — those need different tools (fillers, PRF, resurfacing, topicals; see §5 and §7). Deep lines that are visible even at rest may soften but often won't fully resolve with neurotoxin alone. Results are temporary. Not every client is a candidate, and individual outcomes vary.

## 4. The visit

**Consultation & mapping.** Diana reviews your medical history, medications, goals, and facial anatomy, and confirms you're a candidate. She assesses your muscle movement (she may ask you to frown, raise your brows, and squint) and maps injection points and a dose plan for the areas you're treating.

**The injection.** The treatment itself is quick — a series of small injections with a very fine needle into the mapped muscles. Local docs schedule neurotoxin visits at roughly **20 minutes**, which makes it a realistic lunch-break or between-errands appointment for Charlotte County clients. Sensation is typically a brief pinch or sting at each point; many clients describe it as very tolerable. Ice or topical comfort measures may be used at the provider's discretion.

**Immediately after.** You can generally return to normal activities the same day. Small red bumps or slight redness at injection points usually settle within 15–60 minutes; minor bruising is possible, especially near the eyes.

**Aftercare (general norms — Diana gives you the final, personalized instructions).** Common guidance includes: **do not rub, massage, or apply firm pressure** to the treated areas for the rest of the day (typically ~24 hours), to avoid the product migrating to unintended muscles; **stay upright** and avoid lying flat for several hours after treatment; **skip strenuous exercise, saunas, and hot yoga** for about 24 hours; and avoid other facial treatments over the injected areas until cleared. Gentle facial movement (frowning, brow-raising) in the first hour is fine and is not required. Follow the specific aftercare Diana provides.

**Results timeline.**
- **Botox:** onset commonly at 3–5 days, with results building to peak around 10–14 days.
- **Daxxify:** onset is often reported around a couple of days to 7–10 days, building to full effect similarly by ~2 weeks. (Onset descriptions vary between sources; treat the "full result at ~2 weeks" framing as the safe client expectation for both.)

A follow-up around the 2-week mark lets Diana assess and, if needed, refine. Ask about touch-up policy at consultation.

**Cadence / how often.** Because the effect is temporary, maintenance is part of the treatment. Botox typically lasts about **3–4 months**; Daxxify's median duration in trials was about **6 months**, with some patients showing improvement out to **9 months**. Individual longevity varies with dose, area, muscle strength, and metabolism.

## 5. Comparisons (honest)

### Daxxify vs Botox (both offered by Diana)
Both are botulinum toxin type A neuromodulators that work by the same acetylcholine-blocking mechanism and treat the same dynamic lines. The practical differences:

| | **Botox** | **Daxxify** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The long-established standard neuromodulator (onabotulinumtoxinA) | Newer, peptide-stabilized neuromodulator (daxibotulinumtoxinA) |
| Stabilizer | Human serum albumin | Synthetic 35-amino-acid peptide; no human/animal-derived components |
| Typical duration | ~3–4 months | Median ~6 months (some up to ~9 months) |
| Onset | ~3–5 days, peak ~10–14 days | Reported fast onset in some sources; plan for full result ~2 weeks |
| Track record | Decades of clinical use and data | FDA-approved 2022; strong trial data, shorter real-world history |
| Best when | You want the proven standard, or want a shorter commitment while you dial in your look | You want fewer touch-up visits per year and longer stretches between appointments |

**How to think about cost-per-result honestly:** Diana prices **both Botox and Daxxify at $14/unit** (see §8). Because Daxxify tends to last longer, fewer visits per year *can* offset a similar or slightly higher total unit spend for some clients — but longevity varies, and a longer-lasting product also means any result you'd want adjusted persists longer too. Diana helps you weigh duration vs. flexibility at consultation. **Neither is universally "better"** — it's a fit conversation.

### Neuromodulators vs dermal fillers (Diana offers both)
These solve **different problems** and are frequently paired, not swapped:
- **Neuromodulators (Botox/Daxxify)** *relax muscles* to soften **dynamic** lines caused by movement (frown lines, forehead lines, crow's feet).
- **Dermal fillers** (Juvéderm, RHA at House of Rose) *add volume* to restore lost fullness, support structure, and soften **static** folds and hollows (cheeks, lips, deeper lower-face lines).
A common combined plan: neurotoxin for the upper-face expression lines + filler where volume has been lost. If your concern is a line that's visible even when your face is completely still, filler or a combined approach is often more appropriate than neurotoxin alone. Diana determines the right mix.

### Neurotoxin vs topical / regenerative options for fine lines
For **fine surface lines, texture, tone, and skin quality** — as opposed to muscle-driven expression lines — muscle-relaxing injections aren't the tool. Regenerative and resurfacing options at House of Rose better address those: **PRF microchanneling** (Amber's lane) and **PRF injections / under-eye PRF** (Diana's lane) work on collagen and skin quality; professional-grade topicals support skin longevity over time. Neurotoxin and these approaches are complementary — one relaxes the muscle, the others improve the skin itself. Many clients do both, staged appropriately.

## 9. FAQ bank (substantive, compliance-clean)

1. **What are neurotoxins / wrinkle relaxers?**
   They're precision injections of botulinum toxin type A that temporarily relax the facial muscles causing expression lines, softening the look of frown lines, forehead lines, and crow's feet. At House of Rose they're administered by Diana, RN, under medical-director oversight.

2. **What's the difference between Botox and Daxxify?**
   Both are botulinum toxin type A and work the same way. Botox is the long-established standard lasting about 3–4 months; Daxxify is a newer, peptide-stabilized option with a median duration of about 6 months (up to ~9 months for some). Diana helps you choose based on how often you want to come in.

3. **Which one should I get?**
   That's a consultation conversation. Choose Botox for the proven standard or a shorter commitment; choose Daxxify if you'd prefer fewer touch-ups per year. Diana confirms candidacy and the right fit for your goals.

4. **How long does it last?**
   Botox typically lasts about 3–4 months; Daxxify's median in trials was about 6 months, with some clients seeing improvement out to 9 months. Individual outcomes vary with dose, area, and metabolism.

5. **When will I see results?**
   Most people see results building over about two weeks, with early changes within the first several days. A follow-up around the 2-week mark lets Diana assess and refine if needed.

6. **Will I look frozen or unnatural?**
   The goal is softened movement, not a frozen look. Diana doses conservatively to your anatomy so expression stays natural. Because the effect is temporary and builds gradually, adjustments are part of the process.

7. **Does it hurt?**
   Most clients find it very tolerable — a brief pinch or sting at each tiny injection point. The visit is quick, roughly 20 minutes, and comfort measures are available.

8. **Is there downtime?**
   Generally none — most clients return to normal activities the same day. You may have small bumps or slight redness that settle within an hour, and occasional minor bruising, especially near the eyes.

9. **What areas can be treated?**
   The most common are the frown lines between the brows, horizontal forehead lines, and crow's feet. Additional areas may be appropriate depending on your anatomy; Diana determines which areas are suitable at consultation.

10. **Can I get neurotoxin and filler at the same time?**
    Often yes — they solve different problems (relaxing muscles vs. restoring volume) and are frequently combined. Diana plans the right combination and sequence for your visit.

11. **Can I get this while pregnant or breastfeeding?**
    No — neurotoxins are not offered during pregnancy or breastfeeding. If your circumstances change, we're glad to see you afterward.

12. **Are there medical reasons I can't have it?**
    Yes. Certain neuromuscular conditions (such as myasthenia gravis), allergies to botulinum toxin or its components, and other medical factors can make neurotoxin inappropriate. Diana reviews your full history to confirm you're a candidate.

13. **Is Daxxify FDA-approved?**
    Yes — Daxxify (daxibotulinumtoxinA) received FDA approval in 2022 for the temporary improvement of moderate to severe frown lines (glabellar lines) in adults. Botox Cosmetic is FDA-approved for moderate to severe frown lines, crow's feet, and forehead lines.

14. **How much does it cost?**
    Both Botox and Daxxify are priced per unit at House of Rose. Your total depends on how many units your treatment areas need, which Diana determines based on your muscle strength and goals. We'll give you a clear estimate at consultation.
