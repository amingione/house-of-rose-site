## 1. The 3-sentence answer

An enzyme exfoliation facial is a gentle, non-abrasive resurfacing treatment that uses fruit- and plant-derived proteolytic enzymes — papaya (**papain**), pineapple (**bromelain**), and pumpkin — to dissolve the protein bonds holding dull, dead surface cells together, revealing smoother, brighter, more even-looking skin with no downtime. Because enzymes work on protein rather than lowering the skin's pH like an acid, it is one of the mildest professional exfoliation options — a relaxing **maintenance** facial well suited to sensitive, reactive, or rosacea-prone skin and to first-time clients. At House of Rose this is **Brandy's** basic-facial lane (relaxing/maintenance work, distinct from Amber's corrective/advanced treatments); final candidacy is confirmed by your provider, not every client is a candidate, and individual outcomes vary.

## 2. How it works

**Plain English.** Your skin is constantly making new cells at the bottom and shedding old, dead ones at the top. When that shedding slows — from age, sun, dehydration, or just life — dead cells pile up and skin looks dull, rough, and congested. An enzyme facial uses natural fruit enzymes, applied as a short-contact mask or gel, that act like tiny scissors: they snip the "spot-welds" that hold dead surface cells to each other, so those cells lift away cleanly and the fresh, smoother skin underneath shows through. There's no scrubbing and no acid sting — the enzymes only work on the dead protein sitting on the very surface, then stop.

**One level deeper.** Papain (from *Carica papaya*), bromelain (from *Ananas comosus* / pineapple), and their relatives are **proteolytic enzymes** — proteins that catalyse the hydrolysis of other proteins. In the skin they target the **corneodesmosomes**, the protein "rivets" (built from structural proteins like desmoglein-1 and corneodesmosin) that bind dead corneocytes together in the stratum corneum. By hydrolysing those keratin/corneodesmosomal protein bonds, the enzymes loosen intercellular adhesion so the uppermost dead-cell layer sheds gently. Papain and bromelain are **thiol (cysteine) proteases**: they cut the protein "mortar" apart directly rather than dissolving it with acidity.

**Enzyme vs. acid — a genuinely different mechanism (this is the whole reason it's gentle).** An acid exfoliant (AHA like glycolic/lactic, or BHA like salicylic) works *chemically by pH*: it lowers the skin's pH, which weakens the ionic bonds and drives "desmosome resolution," causing the surface to desquamate — and acid depth scales with concentration, pH, and contact time (a stronger, longer, lower-pH acid reaches more layers). An enzyme works *biochemically on the protein itself*, is essentially **pH-neutral**, is **self-limiting** (the enzymes are delicate proteins whose activity is sensitive to pH and heat, used as brief wash-off masks, so the action confines itself to the outermost dead-cell layer and then stops), and is **non-abrasive** (no mechanical friction, unlike a scrub or blade). One cosmetic study found ~1% papain more effective at exfoliating the stratum corneum than 5% lactic acid while acting more selectively on dead surface protein and without lowering skin pH. That combination — surface-limited, pH-neutral, self-limiting, non-abrasive — is why enzymes sit at the gentlest end of the exfoliation spectrum.

## 3. Who it's for / not for

**A good fit for:**
- Dull, rough, or uneven-looking texture and mild congestion — clients who want a **brightening, smoothing "glow" refresh** rather than correction.
- **Sensitive, reactive, rosacea-prone, or eczema-prone skin** that can't comfortably tolerate stronger acid peels, retinoids, or physical scrubs — the surface-limited, non-acidic action means less irritation.
- **First-time exfoliators** and anyone wanting the lowest-risk way to add regular professional exfoliation to a routine.
- Clients maintaining results between advanced treatments, or prepping skin so home-care products absorb better.
- Anyone who wants an **unhurried, relaxing facial with no downtime** and an immediate visible freshness.

**Not for / postpone / provider review required** (screening guide — Brandy makes the actual candidacy call at consultation):
- **Allergy to the source fruit / latex-fruit syndrome — the most important enzyme-specific caution.** Papain and bromelain are proteins and can occasionally trigger allergic or irritant reactions. Papaya sits in the highest-risk category of latex-cross-reactive foods, and papaya/pineapple cross-reactivity is documented. Anyone with a known fruit (especially papaya or pineapple) or latex allergy should be flagged and patch-tested; a sensitive-skin client is patch-tested regardless.
- **Active skin infection, open lesions, broken or compromised skin, or an active inflammatory breakout** in the treatment area.
- **Recent other resurfacing** — a recent peel, laser, microneedling, dermaplaning, or an active retinoid course; don't stack resurfacing procedures close together.
- **Pregnancy / breastfeeding:** enzyme facials are generally among the milder, low-contraindication exfoliation options and are often considered acceptable, but the consistent, correct framing is **"check with your healthcare provider first,"** and formulation strength/contact time may be modified. This is general guidance, not medical advice — the provider decides.

**Honest limits.** This is **maintenance-level resurfacing — surface polish, brightness, and glow — not a corrective treatment.** An enzyme facial does not remove deeper texture, acne scarring, wrinkles, or stubborn pigment; those goals need stronger, provider-selected modalities in **Amber's advanced lane** (microchanneling, microneedling, BioRePeel, corrective peels). Results are cumulative and gentle, and individual outcomes vary.

## 4. The visit

**Protocol (typical basic-facial flow — final protocol is the provider's).**
1. **Consult & skin analysis / patch-test** — Brandy reviews skin type, sensitivities, fruit/latex allergies, and recent treatments; a patch test where indicated.
2. **Double cleanse** to remove makeup, oil, and SPF.
3. **Enzyme application** — the fruit-enzyme mask/gel is applied and left for a short, controlled contact time (often under gentle warmth/steam to support activity), then removed. No stinging is expected; a mild tingling or warmth is normal.
4. **Optional extractions / gentle steam** for mild congestion, as appropriate.
5. **Relaxing finish** — a hydrating mask and/or facial massage, serum, moisturizer, and — always — SPF to close.

**Sensation & downtime.** Comfortable and relaxing; most clients feel light tingling or warmth, not the "bite" of an acid peel. There is **no meaningful downtime** and no visible peeling — clients return to daily activities immediately. Skin may look temporarily flushed or feel slightly tight, easing quickly.

**Aftercare.**
- **SPF is essential.** Any exfoliation leaves the surface briefly more exposed and photosensitive — daily **broad-spectrum SPF 30+**, reapplied outdoors. Mineral (zinc/titanium) formulas are well tolerated by sensitive skin.
- **Briefly pause other actives** for a few days — retinoids/retinol, vitamin C, glycolic/salicylic acids, and scrubs — so the barrier settles; don't stack exfoliation on exfoliation.
- **Support the barrier** — gentle non-abrasive cleanser, a barrier-supporting moisturizer, no picking or over-exfoliating.

**Results timeline.** Brightness and smoothness are often visible **immediately to within a day or two**; benefits are maintenance-level and build with regular, consistent treatment rather than from a single dramatic session.

**Cadence.** The rationale is skin-cell turnover — the epidermis renews on the order of **roughly a month** (commonly cited ~28–40 days; a peer-reviewed model puts full epidermal turnover nearer 47–48 days, and turnover slows with age). A **monthly maintenance facial** is the common professional cadence; at-home enzyme products are typically once or twice weekly, and reactive skin often does well with even less. Frequency is individualized by the provider.

## 5. Comparisons — vs. adjacent treatments (the gentle-maintenance → corrective spectrum, honest)

House of Rose runs a clear ladder from **relaxing maintenance (Brandy)** up to **corrective/advanced (Amber)**. An enzyme facial is the gentlest rung.

**vs. Dermaplaning** (`/services/dermaplaning/` — a published, standalone service). Dermaplaning is *physical/mechanical*: a sterile blade held at an angle manually shaves off the outermost dead-cell layer **plus vellus "peach-fuzz" hair**, leaving an instant glass-skin smoothness and a flawless makeup canvas. Enzyme exfoliation is *biochemical* — it dissolves dead-cell protein bonds and does **not** remove hair. **Enzyme wins** when skin is reactive/rosacea-prone or the client wants the mildest option; **dermaplaning wins** when the client also wants peach-fuzz removed and a mechanical polish. They pair well: an enzyme softens the surface, and dermaplaning finishes it (the site already lists an enzyme peel as an optional dermaplaning upgrade).

**vs. Light chemical peels (AHA/BHA)** (House of Rose offers acid/peel resurfacing largely inside Amber's lane and the Face Reality acne program). A light peel is *acid-driven*: it lowers pH to weaken corneocyte cohesion and reaches deeper than an enzyme (BHA/salicylic is oil-soluble and gets into pores). **Enzyme wins** for sensitive skin, no-downtime maintenance, and first-timers; **a light peel wins** when the client tolerates acids and wants more visible resurfacing of texture, mild pigment, or congestion. An enzyme facial is a sensible **on-ramp** before escalating to acids.

**vs. Hydrodermabrasion** (Brandy's lane per Notion, but **currently parked / not a published service** — flag). Hydrodermabrasion (the HydraFacial family) is *mechanical + infusion*: a pressurized water/serum stream, often with light vacuum, lifts dead cells while simultaneously infusing hydrating/active serums. It delivers more of a "deep-clean + hydrate" result; an enzyme facial is a simpler, purely enzymatic polish. **Enzyme wins** for the mildest, most soothing option and for very reactive skin; **hydrodermabrasion wins** when the client wants extractions/hydration/serum-infusion in one device pass. *If Brandy's lane launches, position these as complementary basic-facial options.*

**vs. BioRePeel** (`/services/biorepeel/` — Amber, advanced). BioRePeel is a TCA-based induced/resurfacing peel in the **corrective** lane — a different intensity class entirely, aimed at radiance, texture, scarring, and stretch marks with a real protocol and provider oversight. **Enzyme wins** as gentle maintenance with no downtime; **BioRePeel wins** when the goal is genuine correction. This is the clearest lane line: enzyme = Brandy/maintenance, BioRePeel = Amber/corrective.

**When a client should START with an enzyme facial:** sensitive/reactive/rosacea-prone skin; first-time professional exfoliation; a client who just wants brightness/glow/maintenance or to prep the skin; or anyone unsure how their skin will respond before escalating to acids, dermaplaning, hydrodermabrasion, or corrective work.

## 9. FAQ bank (substantive, compliance-clean)

1. **What is an enzyme facial?** A gentle, relaxing exfoliation facial that uses natural fruit enzymes (papaya, pineapple, pumpkin) to lift away dull, dead surface skin — revealing brighter, smoother-looking skin with no downtime.
2. **How is an enzyme facial different from a chemical peel?** Enzymes dissolve the protein bonds holding dead cells together and are essentially pH-neutral; acid peels work by lowering the skin's pH and generally reach deeper. Enzyme facials are the gentler, maintenance-level option.
3. **Is it good for sensitive or rosacea-prone skin?** It's one of the mildest professional exfoliation choices and is often chosen for sensitive, reactive, or rosacea-prone skin because the action stays on the surface and doesn't rely on acidity. Your provider confirms candidacy — not every client is a candidate.
4. **Will my skin peel or need downtime?** Typically no visible peeling and no meaningful downtime — most clients return to their day right away with an immediate freshness.
5. **How often should I get one?** A monthly maintenance rhythm is common, following the skin's roughly month-long renewal cycle. Your provider will personalize the cadence.
6. **Will it get rid of my acne scars / wrinkles / dark spots?** No — an enzyme facial is maintenance, not correction. It supports the appearance of brightness and smooth texture. Deeper concerns are addressed by our advanced treatments (with Amber), and your provider will guide the right path.
7. **Does it hurt?** It shouldn't — most clients feel a mild tingling or warmth, not the sting associated with stronger acid peels.
8. **Can I combine it with dermaplaning?** Often yes — an enzyme softens the surface and dermaplaning adds a mechanical polish and removes peach fuzz. Your provider confirms what's right for your skin that day.
9. **Is it safe during pregnancy?** Enzyme facials are among the milder options often considered acceptable, but the right step is to check with your healthcare provider first and let your provider adjust the treatment. This is general information, not medical advice.
10. **I'm allergic to pineapple / papaya (or latex) — can I still get one?** Flag it before booking. Because the enzymes come from fruit, a fruit or latex allergy can matter; your provider will patch-test or choose a different formulation, and may recommend against it.
11. **What should I do afterward?** Wear broad-spectrum SPF 30+ daily, and pause other exfoliating actives (retinol, vitamin C, acids, scrubs) for a few days while your skin settles.
12. **Which enzyme is used?** We work with professional fruit-enzyme lines (papaya, blueberry, pumpkin, and more via Skin Script and GlyMed+); your provider selects the formulation for your skin.
13. **Is this the same as microdermabrasion?** No — microdermabrasion/hydrodermabrasion is mechanical (physical resurfacing, sometimes with serum infusion). An enzyme facial is biochemical and gentler.
14. **Will it help my skincare work better?** Many clients notice products absorb better after gentle exfoliation, since dead surface buildup is reduced. Results vary by person.
15. **Who performs it at House of Rose?** This is Brandy's basic/maintenance-facial lane. Final candidacy and combinations are confirmed by your licensed provider at consultation.
