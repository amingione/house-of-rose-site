## 1. Read this before you say a single word about it

Five hard lines. Everything else in this document sits inside them.

1. **It is topical. Always. Only.** We apply exosomes and PDRN to the surface of the skin after a
   procedure. We do not inject either one, we do not describe them as injectable, and we do not
   answer "can you inject it" with anything other than "no — we use it topically." There are no
   FDA-approved exosome or salmon-DNA injectables in the United States. If a client says another
   practice injected it, do not comment on that practice.
2. **Never say "FDA-approved," "clinically proven," "stem cell," or "regenerative therapy."** All
   four are banned by `COMPLIANCE-COPY-RULES.md` § 2. Exosomes are a cosmetic topical here. That is
   the whole claim.
3. **Never repeat the vendor's "results last 21+ months."** That is a manufacturer sales figure. It
   is not something House of Rose has verified, and stating it at the desk turns it into a promise
   we own. If a client asks how long it lasts, use the answer in § 6.
4. **Never say it "heals," "repairs," "cures," "fixes," or "treats"** acne, scarring, rosacea, hair
   loss, or any condition. Describe what we do (apply it after the procedure) and what the client
   may notice (appearance-level, with variation language).
5. **No discount, special, deal, package-savings, or member framing.** State the price plainly. We
   do not offer memberships and never will.

If a client pushes past what you can answer factually, the correct move is: *"That's a provider
question — let me have Amber answer that directly so you get it right."* Deferring is never a
failure. Guessing is.

## 2. The 60-second explanation (say it like this)

> "Exosomes are a topical concentrate we apply right after a treatment, while the skin is most
> receptive. Anything that creates micro-channels — microneedling, Morpheus8, dermaplaning — opens a
> window for a short time, and this is what we put into that window. It's a Korean-manufactured
> human-derived exosome serum, and we can pair it with a PDRN booster, which is a polynucleotide
> derived from salmon. Both are applied to the skin's surface. We don't inject them.
>
> Most people add it to the treatment they were already booking. It's the difference between
> finishing the procedure and finishing it well. Individual results vary, and Amber will tell you
> whether it makes sense for what you came in for."

**When to deploy it:** at booking, at check-in, and at checkout for rebooking. Three touches, not
one. Most conversions happen at check-in, when the client is already committed and the marginal
spend feels small.

**Never lead with the price.** Lead with the window. The price answers a question the client asks
second.

## 4. Where the money actually is — margin math (internal only, never say this out loud)

| Line | Single price | Est. product cost | Gross | Series of 3 | Series cost | Series gross |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sheet mask add-on | $55 | ~$18 | ~$37 (68%) | $145 | ~$53 | ~$92 (63%) |
| Tier 1 | $299 | ~$164 (1 vial) / ~$82 (vial shared, same day) | 45% → 73% | $829 | ~$492 | ~$337 (41%) |
| Tier 2 | $399 | ~$164 | ~59% | $1,050 | ~$492 | ~$558 (53%) |
| Tier 3 | $549 | ~$164 | ~70% | $1,450 | ~$492 | ~$958 (66%) |
| Exosome Infusion Facial | $395 | ~$182 (vial + mask) | ~54% | $1,050 | ~$546 | ~$504 (48%) |

**Tier 1 is the thin one — that's why its series saves only $68.** Do not let anyone "round it down"
to a friendlier number at the desk. At $829 it clears ~41%; below about $780 the series stops being
worth the three vials. If a Tier 1 client hesitates, move them to the $55 mask, not to a lower price.

Vial cost assumes the **Advanced Exosomes — Skin** SKU at $1,639 / 10 treatments = ~$163.90. The
dual-purpose skin & hair SKU at $1,199 / 10 = ~$119.90 improves every line above by ~$44.

**The single biggest margin lever is same-day vial sharing.** A mixed vial holds up for ~72 hours
refrigerated. Two Tier-1 clients back to back on one vial roughly doubles the margin on that vial.
When you're building the schedule, **cluster exosome clients on the same day.** That is a front-desk
decision, and it's worth real money.

Second lever: the $55 mask. It has the best margin percentage on the card and the lowest resistance.
A day with six facials and zero mask add-ons is six missed conversations.

## 5. Upsell triggers — what to say, by service

Match the sentence to what they're already booked for. Do not use a generic pitch on a specific
client.

**Booked for Morpheus8, Lumecca, Forma, or Fusion (Tier 3, $549)**
> "You're already making the biggest investment on our menu today. Amber can finish with a topical
> exosome application while the channels are still open — that window closes within a few hours and
> doesn't come back. It's $549 added to what you're booked for. Want me to note it so she has it
> ready?"

**Booked for Procell or PRF Microneedling (Tier 2, $399)**
> "Since we're microneedling anyway, we can use the exosome concentrate through the treatment
> instead of just after it, and you take the rest of the vial home for the next couple of days. It's
> $399 on top of your Procell. Should I add it?"

**Booked for Glo2Facial, dermaplaning, BioRePeel, or a basic facial (Tier 1, $299 — or the $55 mask)**
> "Two options, and neither is required. The sheet mask is $55 and goes on at the end — that's the
> easy one. The full exosome application is $299 and is more of a step up. Most people start with
> the mask and see how their skin likes it."

*Lead with the mask here.* Tier 1 is the harder sell without channels, and a $55 yes today is worth
more than a $299 no.

**Booked for injectables or IV with Diana Morrison, RN**
> "If you're in the chair anyway, Amber can add a topical exosome mask at the end for $55. It's
> separate from what Diana's doing — surface only."

**Waxing or a walk-in with no skin service booked**
> Do not pitch tiers. Pitch the mask, once, and drop it if they don't bite.

**Rebooking at checkout — the highest-yield moment nobody uses**
> "How did the mask feel last time? If you liked it, Amber usually suggests spacing the fuller
> application with your next microneedling — want me to note it on the booking so you don't have to
> decide at the door?"

## 6. Objection handling — verbatim

**"What even is an exosome?"**
> "Think of it as a very concentrated topical made from human-derived cell material, manufactured in
> Korea under their regulatory standards. We put it on the skin right after a procedure because
> that's when the skin takes things in best. That's honestly the whole idea — timing plus
> concentration."

**"Is it FDA-approved?"**
> "It's a topical cosmetic product, so it isn't in the FDA-approval category the way a drug would be —
> that applies to injectables, and we don't inject it. If you want the manufacturer's documentation,
> I can get it in front of you."
> ❌ Never say "yes." ❌ Never say "it doesn't need approval" in a way that sounds dismissive.

**"Can you inject it? I saw it on TikTok."**
> "We don't. Ours is topical only. There aren't FDA-approved injectable exosome or salmon-DNA
> products in the U.S., so topical is where we stay."
> Do not editorialize about whoever they saw doing it.

**"Salmon DNA? That sounds weird."**
> "Fair. PDRN is a polynucleotide originally derived from salmon — it's been used in Korean skincare
> for years. Ours is a topical booster we can mix into the exosome application. Same rule: surface
> only."

**"$549 is a lot on top of what I'm already paying."**
> "It is, and it's genuinely optional — the Morpheus8 works on its own. The reason people add it is
> that the window only exists on the day of the treatment. If it's not the right month for it, we
> can just do it next time."
> ❌ Do not discount. ❌ Do not offer a "one-time" price. We don't do that.

**"How is this different from the Procell stem cell thing?"**
> "We don't describe Procell as a stem cell treatment — it's microchanneling with the Procell serums.
> The exosome application is a different product that can go on top of it. Amber can walk you through
> which combination makes sense for your skin."
> ⚠️ The Kleos rep still owes us a written Procell comparison (§ 10). Until it arrives, do not
> make comparative efficacy claims in either direction.

**"How long does it last?"**
> "That varies a lot person to person, and I'd rather not give you a number I can't stand behind.
> Amber will tell you what she's seeing and when she'd want you back."
> ❌ Never say 21 months. ❌ Never say "up to two years."

**"Will it help my acne scars / my hair / my rosacea?"**
> "That's a provider question — Amber will look at it and tell you honestly whether this is the
> right tool. I don't want to promise you something on the phone."

**"Can I just buy the serum and do it at home?"**
> "The masks you can take home, and we sell those. The vial gets mixed here and has to be refrigerated
> and used within about 72 hours, so it's tied to the appointment. If you're doing a full application
> we send the remainder home with you anyway."

**"Does it hurt / is there downtime?"**
> "The exosome part itself is just applied to the skin — the sensation is from whatever treatment
> you're having. Amber will go over exactly what to expect for your specific procedure."
> ❌ Never say "no downtime" or "pain-free" as a blanket statement — banned by compliance rules.

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Totally fine. I'll note it on your chart so Amber has the option ready if you change your mind in
> the room — no pressure either way."
> Then stop. One ask, one graceful exit. We don't chase.
