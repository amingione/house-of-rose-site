## Key Findings

### 1. The Science & Safety of PRF
- **What it is:** PRF is a "second-generation" platelet concentrate. A small amount of blood is drawn into tubes **without anticoagulant** and spun at **low speed in a single step**, so the blood clots naturally into a three-dimensional **fibrin matrix** that traps platelets, white blood cells (leukocytes), and a small number of circulating stem cells.
- **PRF vs. PRP — the core difference is timing of growth-factor release.** PRP uses an anticoagulant (often bovine/cattle-derived ACD-A or citrate) and higher-speed centrifugation, stays liquid, and—once activated—releases its growth factors in a fast burst. Per **Drucker Diagnostics**, "When activated (calcium chloride, thrombin), platelets degranulate rapidly—releasing 70% of growth factors within 10 minutes and 95% within the first hour." PRF, by contrast, has no anticoagulant, and its fibrin scaffold releases growth factors **gradually over roughly 7–14 days.** This sustained release is documented in **Kobayashi et al. (PubMed 26809431),** which found that at later time points up to 10 days, advanced-PRF (A-PRF) released the highest total growth factors of the concentrates studied.
- **Key growth factors released:** PDGF (platelet-derived growth factor), TGF-β, VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor), EGF (epidermal growth factor), and IGF. These drive **collagen production, angiogenesis (new blood-vessel formation), and tissue repair.**
- **Laboratory regenerative signal:** Per **Wang et al., Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology, 2019 (PubMed 30990574),** "Skin fibroblasts migrated over 350% more in fluid-PRF when compared to control and PRP (200% increase)…collagen 1, and fibronectin mRNA levels were all significantly highest in the fluid-PRF group." This is in-vitro (cell-culture) data, not a clinical outcome, but it underpins PRF's collagen-stimulating rationale.
- **Why it's considered safe and "natural":** PRF is fully autologous, requires no anticoagulant and no animal-derived additives, and carries a very low risk of allergic reaction or rejection. Reported side effects are mild and short-lived—swelling, bruising, and redness for a few days (under-eye swelling is often most noticeable the morning after).

### 2. PRF Injectables (Face & Body)
- **Most common treatment areas:** under-eyes/tear troughs (the most popular and best-studied), cheeks/midface, temples, smile lines, neck and décolleté, hands, and scalp (for hair). The under-eye area is also one of the first places to age and one of the most clinically successful PRF targets.
- **Realistic timeline and treatment plan:** Early changes typically appear at **2–6 weeks**; collagen remodeling peaks around **3–6 months**; results commonly last **6–12 months** (some clinics cite up to 12–18 months), with maintenance recommended. A typical plan is **2–3 sessions spaced about 4–6 weeks apart.**
- **PRF EZ Gel / "biofiller":** PRF combined with heated-then-cooled **albumin** (also from the patient's blood) forms an injectable gel that delivers **subtle, temporary volume** (commonly ~3 months, some sources 3–6 months) **plus** longer regenerative skin benefits (6–12+ months). It is a genuine natural alternative to synthetic fillers and carries virtually no allergy or granuloma risk—but it is **not as volumizing as hyaluronic-acid filler** and should never be marketed as an equivalent for significant volume correction.
- **PRF as a safer alternative to fillers/fat transfer:** Honest positioning is "regeneration and skin-quality improvement with subtle volume," not "replaces filler." It is autologous and biocompatible, which is its true differentiator. Many clinics legitimately combine PRF with HA filler (e.g., PRF + Restylane-L for tear troughs) for both volume and skin quality.
- **"Non-surgical BBL" — HIGH RISK, recommend avoiding.** PRP/PRF gluteal injection ("non-surgical BBL") is marketed by some clinics, but the evidence is thin, PRP/PRF is not FDA-approved for cosmetic use, and credible plastic-surgery commentary notes there is no published scientific data backing the butt-lift claims. Most genuine "non-surgical BBLs" actually use Sculptra or Radiesse, not PRF. House of Rose should **not** lead with this framing; if offered at all, it requires explicit disclaimers and legal sign-off.

### 3. PRF + Microchanneling vs. Microneedling
- **Microneedling (collagen induction therapy):** fine needles create controlled micro-injuries that trigger the skin's wound-healing/collagen response; needle depth is adjustable (often deeper); it is the more extensively documented technique in the dermatology literature for scars, wrinkles, and texture.
- **Microchanneling:** a precision device uses a **vertical stamping** motion with fixed-depth, precisely spaced needles to create uniform, shallow channels (often around 0.25 mm). It is marketed as **gentler, more uniform, less traumatic, lower downtime, and better at delivering serums** because the device enters and exits cleanly rather than dragging.
- **Proven vs. marketing—be precise:**
  - **Proven:** Microneedling combined with growth factors or platelet concentrate improves skin texture, fine lines, and pigmentation in controlled split-face studies. For example, in melasma, PRP delivered by microneedling outperformed microinjection (70.21% vs. 36.6% improvement in modified MASI score, p<0.001).
  - **Largely marketing:** Claims that microchanneling is categorically "superior" to microneedling, and specific serum-absorption figures (e.g., "up to 300%"), come from device makers and clinics, not head-to-head RCTs. Present microchanneling as "precision, comfort, and uniformity," not as clinically proven superiority.
- **The synergy with PRF:** Applying PRF topically immediately after channeling delivers a patient's own autologous growth factors directly into freshly opened channels—a biologically sensible pairing that amplifies the collagen-induction response, accelerates healing, and improves texture.

### 6. Regulatory & Compliance
- **FDA stance:** PRP/PRF are autologous blood products. The **centrifuge/preparation device requires FDA 510(k) clearance**, but the **procedure itself is not FDA-approved for cosmetic indications.** Never claim PRF is "FDA-approved" for facial rejuvenation; the accurate statement is that PRF uses your own blood and the preparation device is FDA-cleared.
- **Florida Board of Medicine — Rule 64B8-11.001 (Advertising):** No advertising may be "false, deceptive, or misleading." Specifically avoid: unsubstantiated scientific claims; claims that your provider's "qualifications, skills, or other attributes are superior to other" providers; implying specialty board recognition you don't hold; and before/after photos without a clear "results vary / not a guarantee" disclaimer. Per **Etna Interactive's summary of Florida rules,** practices must avoid "making scientific claims that cannot be substantiated" and must show "patient before and after photos…indicating that results vary and the results shown are not a guarantee." Discounted or free services require the disclaimer language in **Fla. Stat. 456.062.** Ads must clearly identify the licensee performing the procedure (MD/PA/etc.), and where non-physicians (NPs, aestheticians) perform services, the ad must make clear who performs each procedure.
- **Exosomes & "stem cells" — avoid.** Per the **FDA Public Safety Notification on Exosome Products: "There are currently no FDA-approved exosome products… exosomes used to treat diseases and conditions in humans are regulated as drugs and biological products under the Public Health Service Act and the Federal Food Drug and Cosmetic Act."** FDA enforcement discretion ended **May 31, 2021**, and the agency has issued warning letters to firms including **Kimera Labs (2023), Chara Biologics and Evolutionary Biologics (2024–25), and Dynamic Stem Cell Therapy (Feb 2026).** House of Rose should not offer exosome injectables and should not describe any treatment as "stem cell injection." (Procell's **topical** serums are a separate cosmetic category but must still be described accurately—recombinant/bone-marrow-derived growth factors, not "stem cell therapy.")
- **Responsible claims summary:** SAFE to say — "natural," "autologous," "uses your own blood," "stimulates your own collagen," "supports skin renewal," "minimal downtime," "results develop gradually." REQUIRES a disclaimer — any results timeline, durations, and all before/after imagery ("individual results vary"). AVOID — "FDA-approved," "permanent," "guaranteed," "best/superior," "cure," "non-surgical BBL," "stem cell injection," and any exosome injectable.
