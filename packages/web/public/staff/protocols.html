🚨 Safety Hard Stops

Nine ways to actually injure a client — and the one question that prevents each one. These are not preferences. These are the rules that keep someone out of an ER and us out of a lawsuit.

If you are unsure, you do not treat. Rescheduling a client costs one appointment. Treating through a contraindication costs a face, a license, and a business. Nobody at House of Rose will ever be criticized for stopping.
🪒
Never wax skin on Accutane or active retinoids#1 preventable injury in this building
Aundrea · Brandy
The rule
No waxing if the client is on isotretinoin (Accutane) currently or within 6 months, or is using any retinoid / retinol / tretinoin on the area. Retinoids must be stopped 5–7 days prior. Same for recent AHA/BHA, peels, laser, or microneedling on the area.
What happens if you don't
The wax pulls the skin off with the hair. Not irritation — an actual avulsion, a raw open wound, weeks of healing, and often a permanent scar or pigment change. On a face.
Why it's dangerous
Retinoids thin the stratum corneum and speed turnover. The epidermis loses its grip on the dermis. Hard wax bonds to skin more than you think — and the client will not volunteer this information, because they don't know it matters.
Ask this — every client, every appointment, not just intake"Any retinol, tretinoin, Retin-A, or Accutane — even a little, even a while ago?"
💉
Never open a filler syringe without hyaluronidase on-site and in dateVascular occlusion = tissue death in hours
Diana, RN
The rule
Physically verify hyaluronidase is in the building and not expired — before the syringe is opened. Every single time. Not "we have some somewhere."
What happens if you don't
Filler enters a vessel and blocks blood supply. Without prompt hyaluronidase you get skin necrosis — the tissue dies. In the glabella and nose, an occlusion can travel and cause irreversible blindness. This is a race measured in hours.
Watch for, continuously, while injecting
Blanching · pain out of proportion to the injection · dusky or mottled skin · any vision change. Any one of these → STOP. Hyaluronidase protocol. Escalate immediately. Do not "wait and see."
Check before every filler appointment"Hyaluronidase — in the building, in date, and I know exactly where it is."
🤲
Never massage over fresh tox or fillerYou will move someone else's work
Brandy · Aundrea · Amber
The rule
No facial massage, lymphatic drainage, sculpting, dermaplaning pressure, ultrasound, or RF over an area that has had recent neurotoxin or filler. This hits Sculpt & Lift, Royal Rose, Luxury Facial, and every massage-forward service hardest.
What happens if you don't
Tox migrates — into the levator, and you have given the client a drooping eyelid for three months. Or filler shifts out of the placement Diana paid attention to, and now it's lumpy and asymmetric. Either way: it happened in your room, on your table.
The window — now written
Neurotoxin → 7 days before massage / lymphatic / sculpting · 14 days before needling or heat.
Filler → 14 days before massage · 28 days before RF / heat.
Bruising or swelling still present → do not treat, regardless of the calendar. Full table in the ⏱ Timing & Pregnancy tab. (Draft — pending Diana + medical-director sign-off.)
Ask every facial client — this is not on the intake form"Any Botox, Daxxify, or filler recently? How recently?"
⚡
Never weld permanent jewelry on a pacemaker or implanted deviceElectrical interference with a cardiac device
Aundrea
The rule
Screen every client for a pacemaker, ICD, neurostimulator, insulin pump, or any implanted electronic device. If yes → do not weld. Physician clearance required, and the default answer is no.
What happens if you don't
The welder produces an electrical arc. Interference with a cardiac device can cause malfunction or arrhythmia. This is a cardiac event, in your chair, over a $65 bracelet.
Also
Always place the protective card between the weld point and the skin. Defer in pregnancy — err conservative. Nickel allergy → steer to 14k gold-filled.
Ask before you measure — not after"Do you have a pacemaker, defibrillator, insulin pump, or any implanted electronic device?"
👁️
Never skip the lash tint patch testA dye reaction on an eyelid is a medical event
Aundrea
The rule
Patch test 24–48 hours before — every new client, and again after any formula change. No same-day tint on a first-time client. Ever.
What happens if you don't
Allergic contact dermatitis on the thinnest skin on the body. Eyes swell shut. Severe reactions can involve the airway. This is an ER visit and it is entirely on us.
Also — the lift itself
Time every solution. Set an actual timer. Do not eyeball it. Over-processing = chemically damaged, broken lashes that take a full cycle to grow back. Never let solution touch the eye or the skin.
When they want it today"I can't tint you today — I need to patch test you first. That's not me being careful, that's me not risking your eyes. Let's book you for Thursday."

Amber · Diana · front desk
The rule
Amber applies PRF topically. Full stop. No injections, ever. Diana injects PRF — under-eye, facial, EZ Gel. These are two different services and a client can book the wrong one.
What happens if you don't
An esthetician injecting a blood product is practicing outside her license. That is not a policy violation — that is a Board of Medicine matter, and it ends a career.
What you may never say

🔥
Never treat aggressively over a melasma flagHeat and aggression rebound it — permanently darker
Amber · Brandy
The rule
M17 flags melasma, or the pattern is consistent with it → consult before you choose anything. No aggressive microneedling, no aggressive peel, no heat device in the moment. Amber or Diana reviews first.
What happens if you don't
Melasma is heat-reactive. The treatment the client expects — deeper, stronger, more — makes it worse, and the rebound can be permanent. You will have taken money to darken the exact thing she came in about.
Same principle
High Fitzpatrick + PIH history = go gentler, go slower, longer intervals. Post-inflammatory pigment is the failure mode of the entire corrective lane. Over-extraction causes it too — leave some congestion, get it next visit.
The de-escalation that builds trust"I want Amber to look at this before we choose anything. Going gentler here isn't me holding back — with melasma, aggressive is how you make it worse."
🧷
Single-use, every time. No double-dipping, no reused cartridge, no reused blade.Cross-contamination and bloodborne pathogens
Everyone
The rule
New sterile cartridge every needling session. New sterile blade every dermaplane. New stick every wax dip — no double-dipping, ever. No brush back into cream product — decant onto a palette.
What happens if you don't
Needling and dermaplaning draw blood. A reused cartridge is a bloodborne-pathogen transmission route. A double-dipped wax stick puts one client's bacteria into the pot every other client shares.
Also non-negotiable
Never mix a microneedling device and a microchanneling device in the same appointment — pick one pathway before you start. Never needle over sunburned, inflamed, infected, or open skin. Never save, refrigerate, or send PRF home — discard as biohazardous waste.

3 · The four overrides that beat the calendar

These outrank the table. Every time.›
  1. Visible bruising, swelling, or tenderness → do not treat, no matter how many days have passed. The calendar is a floor, not a permission slip.
  2. Never same-day. No injectable + needling, peel, or energy device on the same day. Not as a package, not as a favor, not because she drove from Naples.
  3. When in doubt, ask Diana. She has the chart, the product, the volume, and the placement. You have a guess.
  4. An outside injector's instructions win — get them in writing. If you can't, apply the longest window in the table.

7 · The product tray

🚫 Never — pregnant or breastfeeding

  • Retinoids — retinol, retinal, tretinoin, Retin-A, adapalene, tazarotene, any Vitamin A derivative. High-dose Vitamin A in pregnancy is linked to birth defects. This is the single most important line on this card.
  • Hydroquinone — 30–40% systemic absorption after topical use. By far the highest of anything on our shelf.
  • High-% salicylic acid · BHA peels — low-strength (<2%) leave-on is generally considered acceptable, but our house rule is to avoid it entirely. That removes the judgment call from a busy room.
  • TCA · BioRePeel · medium/deep peels
  • Benzoyl peroxide (high %) — house rule: skip it
  • Aromatherapy / essential-oil-heavy blends — several are contraindicated; not worth parsing which
  • Formaldehyde releasers · chemical depilatories

✅ Safe — reach for these

  • Hyaluronic acid · glycerin · ceramides · peptides
  • Niacinamide · azelaic acid · Vitamin C (L-ascorbic)
  • Bakuchiol — the pregnancy-safe retinol alternative. Say this to her. She will want to know there's an option.
  • PHAs · low-strength lactic / glycolic / mandelic
  • Enzyme exfoliants — papain, bromelain
  • Mineral SPF — zinc oxide / titanium dioxide

Also

No hydroquinone, no aggressive peels, no heat — not now. Tell her: "We'll build the real plan once you're done breastfeeding. Between now and then, sun protection is the single highest-value thing you can do — and it's what keeps this from getting harder to treat later." That conversation keeps the client. It doesn't lose her.
Breastfeeding: the hard stops still apply. Needling, injectables, IV, GLP-1 — still out. Retinoids and hydroquinone — still out. The safe-lane products above are fine. When in doubt: her OB or pediatrician, not us.

🚫 Never market a service as "safe for pregnancy." Say: "we adjust the treatment and the products, and your OB has the final say." 🚫 Never treat a pregnant client "just gently" through a hard-stop service. The hard stops are hard.

Universal SOP — every treatment, every provider

No service starts until all six of these are done. No exceptions.

The four things that stop a treatment cold. If any of these is true, you do not treat today — you reschedule or refer. 1) Active infection, open lesion, cold sore, or sunburn in the treatment area. 2) Isotretinoin (Accutane) currently or within the provider-specified window. 3) Pregnancy or breastfeeding — for any needling, peel, tox, filler, IV, or GLP-1. 4) No signed consent on file for today's service.
The 6-Step Universal OpenBefore any hands-on step›
  1. Intake & contraindication screen. Meds, allergies, isotretinoin, pregnancy/breastfeeding, autoimmune, bleeding disorders, keloid history, cold-sore history, recent peels/lasers/injectables, recent sun.
  2. Signed consent for today's service. Every service has its own form. A consent from a prior visit does not carry forward to a different service.
  3. Standardized photos. Same lighting, same angles, same distance (front + both 45s). No jewelry, no makeup, hair back. Photo/model release is a separate consent from treatment consent.
  4. M17 skin analysis where indicated — and check the melasma / sensitivity flags before choosing depth or aggression.
  5. Fitzpatrick type + PIH history documented. Higher Fitzpatrick and PIH history = go gentler, longer intervals.
  6. Confirm the plan out loud. "Here's what I'm doing today, here's what you'll feel, here's what tomorrow looks like." Then treat.

Universal contraindications — screen on every service

  • Pregnancy / breastfeeding
  • Active infection, open wound, rash, dermatitis, or sunburn in the area
  • Active or recent cold sore (HSV) — needling can trigger an outbreak; antiviral prophylaxis is the prescriber's call
  • Isotretinoin — current or within provider-specified window
  • Uncontrolled diabetes · immune suppression · active cancer treatment
  • Bleeding/platelet disorder or anticoagulant therapy (needling, PRF, injectables)
  • Keloid or abnormal scarring history
  • Allergy to a product, anesthetic, or stainless steel
  • Any suspicious lesion in the treatment area → refer, do not treat over it
Never tell a client to stop a prescription medication. That is the prescribing clinician's decision. You may say "I'd like you to check with your prescriber before we book this."
The 4-Step Universal CloseBefore they leave the room›
  1. Written aftercare in their hand. Verbal is not aftercare. Physical SPF on the way out where indicated.
  2. Rebook before they stand up. Cadence is the treatment — a series that drifts is a series that failed.
  3. Retail the two products that protect the result — not a shelf. Tie it to what you just did.
  4. Document. Device, settings/depth per zone, cartridge lot, products used, endpoint reached, client tolerance, aftercare given.
Call the provider for: increasing pain · spreading redness · warmth · pus or drainage · fever · blistering · significant swelling · persistent bleeding · cold-sore symptoms · delayed healing. Give clients the number: (844) 941-7673.

Universal Needling Aftercare — hand this to every needling client

Before — client instructions

  • Stop Retin-A / retinol / Vitamin A creams 3 days prior.
  • Avoid alcohol, caffeine, cigarettes 3 days before & after. Smoking slows healing — expect longer recovery.
  • Avoid NSAIDs (aspirin, ibuprofen, Aleve), ginkgo, garlic, fish/flax oil, Vit A & E supplements 3 days–1 week before & after — we are creating a controlled inflammatory response and these blunt it.
  • No waxing / depilatories / aggressive exfoliation for 5–7 days.
  • Arrive hydrated. Eat beforehand.

After — client instructions

  • 64 oz water day-of and daily for a week.
  • Do not touch / rub / press for 8 hours.
  • Ice 10 on / 10 off for swelling if needed.
  • No SPF or makeup for 12 hours. First wash no sooner than 6 hours — gentle cleanser, lukewarm water, remove all dried serum.
  • 12–72 h: minimal makeup OK. Gentle cleanser + HA serum + mineral SPF 25+. Occlusive balm if provider directed.
  • 48–72 h: resume normal routine. Retinol strongly recommended once resumed.
  • No actives (AHA/BHA/retinol/low-pH Vit C) for at least 2 hours; no alcohol toners 10–14 days.
  • No sun / tanning / heat 3 days. No swimming 24 h. No exercise or sweating 3 days.
  • Normal: mild bruising, redness, itching, soreness, swelling — resolves 3–10 days. Arnica + bromelain may help; Tylenol for discomfort.

Language — the hard stops

  • 🚫 Never say a needling device injects, infuses, or delivers PRF into the skin, or to "a depth." PRF at House of Rose is topical only. FDA: microneedling devices are not authorized to deliver blood products into skin.
  • 🚫 Never say "sticky bone facial," "stem-cell facial/serum/mask," "exosome therapy," or "non-surgical BBL."
  • 🚫 Never say treats · cures · heals · clears · erases · removes · reverses aging · permanent · guaranteed. Say "the appearance of," "designed to," "supports."
  • 🚫 Never mix a microneedling device and a microchanneling device in the same appointment. Pick one pathway before you start.
  • 🚫 Never re-use a cartridge or blade. Single-use, every session, every client.
  • 🚫 Never needle over sunburned, inflamed, infected, or open skin.
  • 🚫 Never save, refrigerate, or send PRF home. Discard as biohazardous waste.
  • 🚫 Never market GlowTox / Micro-Tox. Daxxify is not topical and must not enter a needling protocol. Pending medical-director approval.
  • 🚫 Discounts/packages need the Fla. Stat. 456.062 disclaimer. Before/afters need written consent + "individual results vary." Identify the licensee performing the service.
