Consent

• A wri en informed consent must be obtained from the patient before photography.

• The consent must include a statement that the photographs are a part of the patient's
  medical records.

• The consent must be a part of the physical/digital patient clinical record.

• Whenever reproduced, care must be taken that the patient's identity is not revealed
  through the photographs.

• Clinicians must be sensitive to the fact that some patients may not want to be
  photographed and may not consent for the same.

Patient Preparation
Hair
The patient's hair must be tucked away or tied such that none of the facial features are obscured.
The strands of hair that drop on the face from the sides should be neatly
tucked behind the ear or secured with bobby pins.
Make-up
It is ideal that any patient being photographed should be make-up free.
Hypoallergenic makeup wipes are helpful for patient prep.
Furthermore, any garments or accessories, such as scarves, that                 interfere with the
interfere with the required pose or the visibility of the area to be
photographed must be removed or repositioned. Jewelry can be
distracting and hence should be removed before photographing
to maintain uniformity.

           HOW DOES IT WORK?
     Skin resurfacing - removal of skin surface to stimulate the production of the new surface
     by removal of epidermal and dermal cells (in the form of ablation, peeling, etc)

     Skin rejuvenation - coagulation and necrosis of dermal and epidermal layers which as
     response promotes body to create new cells

     Skin tightening - volumetric heating of the dermis to stimulate neocallagenesis by
      broblast and collagen remodeling
